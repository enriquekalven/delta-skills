# GTM Launch Playbook

## Purpose & Scope

This reference provides the playbook for executing a GTM strategy. How do we plan a launch? What's the timeline? How do we create demand? How do we enable sales? How do we measure success?

A GTM strategy on paper is worth nothing. Execution wins. This playbook gives you the concrete steps, timeline, and templates.

---

## Part 1: Launch Planning Framework

### T-90 to T+30 Timeline

Every GTM launch follows a predictable timeline. Adapt the specifics to your situation, but the phases are universal.

#### **T-90 to T-60: Strategy & Planning**

**What's happening:**
- GTM strategy is finalized (product positioning, ICP, pricing, motion, channels)
- Launch team is assembled (product, marketing, sales, operations, executive sponsor)
- Resources are allocated (budget, headcount, tools)

**Deliverables:**
- GTM Launch Plan (one-pager summarizing strategy, timeline, success metrics, owner)
- Product positioning and messaging (value prop, differentiators, competitive positioning)
- Sales motion documentation (if SLG or hybrid)
- Launch charter and success criteria
- Budget allocation ($X for demand generation, $Y for enablement, $Z for tools)

**Checkpoints:**
- Executive review & approval of strategy
- Sales + Marketing alignment on success metrics
- Product team confirms feature readiness

#### **T-60 to T-30: Enablement & Systems**

**What's happening:**
- Sales team is trained (process, positioning, objection handling, demos)
- Marketing assets created (battle cards, case studies, one-pagers, email templates)
- Systems are set up (CRM, lead routing, demand gen tools, tracking)
- Demand generation campaigns are built (email sequences, landing pages, ad creative)
- Channel partners are recruited and onboarded (if applicable)

**Deliverables:**
- Sales playbook (30-page guide with discovery flow, demo script, objection handling, deal structure)
- Sales enablement package (presentations, demos, battle cards, FAQ, competitive positioning)
- Marketing asset library (case studies, white papers, data sheets, email templates, social assets)
- Demand gen campaigns (email sequences built, landing pages live, ad copy in review)
- CRM setup (lead scoring, sales process stages, workflow automation)
- Pricing & packaging documentation (finalized and communicated to sales)

**Checkpoints:**
- Sales team is trained and certified (can articulate positioning, handle objections, run demo)
- Marketing team has 4-week supply of content, emails, and campaigns ready
- All systems are in place and tested (CRM, landing pages, email platform, ad accounts)
- Partner onboarding completed for channel partners

#### **T-30 to T-7: Soft Launch & Testing**

**What's happening:**
- Beta customers or pilot accounts are launched (real revenue test)
- Demand gen campaigns begin (small budget, test messaging and channels)
- Sales team runs discovery conversations (product is ready, process works, positioning resonates?)
- Customer success is prepared (onboarding, training, support for new customers)

**Deliverables:**
- Pilot customer results (How did they discover you? How long was sales cycle? Did product work? Are they happy?)
- Campaign performance data (Email open rates, landing page conversion, cost per lead)
- Sales feedback (What objections are we hearing? Is positioning landing? Are demo scripts working?)
- Product readiness status (Any blockers for full launch?)
- Customer success playbooks (Onboarding, training, support for new customer segment)

**Checkpoints:**
- 3-5 pilot customers have signed and started using product
- Demand gen campaigns are running with positive early signals (CTR > 2%, conversion > 2%)
- Sales team has given thumbs-up (positioning works, customers are willing to buy, objections are handleable)
- Customer success has run 1 full customer onboarding and feedback is positive
- Product team has no blockers for full launch

#### **T-7 to T-0: Final Launch**

**What's happening:**
- All systems are live and monitored
- Full demand gen campaigns launch (budget fully allocated)
- Sales team goes live (outbound, inbound, partner selling)
- Press release issued (if applicable)
- Customer success is fully staffed
- Daily standups begin (daily pulse check, issues flagged immediately)

**Deliverables:**
- Launch checklist (all systems live, all assets deployed, all teams ready)
- Daily monitoring dashboard (metrics being tracked, thresholds set for alerts)
- Executive communication plan (weekly updates to board/CEO on launch metrics)
- Incident response plan (if something breaks, who handles it)

**Checkpoints:**
- Launch readiness review (green light on all fronts)
- All team members know their role and success metrics
- Monitoring systems are live (dashboards, alerts, daily syncs)
- Day 1 post-launch retrospective is scheduled

#### **T+0 to T+30: Launch Optimization**

**What's happening:**
- Metrics are tracked closely (daily monitoring, weekly reviews)
- Issues are identified and fixed immediately (campaign not converting? Pause and iterate. Sales process broken? Fix it.)
- Learning loops are tight (A/B tests run daily, results reviewed immediately)
- Demand gen budget is shifted to best-performing campaigns
- Sales coaching is provided based on call recordings

**Deliverables:**
- Weekly launch metrics report (leads, conversions, pipeline, revenue)
- Campaign performance dashboard (email, landing page, paid ads, organic)
- Sales performance dashboard (calls, demos, closes, pipeline by rep)
- Learnings document (what's working, what's not, what to change)
- Optimized campaigns (best performers scaled, underperformers paused/reworked)

**Checkpoints:**
- Week 1 post-launch: Early indicators (positive/negative)
- Week 2: First optimization decisions (what's working, pause what's not)
- Week 3: Learnings applied (campaigns optimized, sales process refined)
- Week 4: Launch retrospective and roadmap for T+30 to T+90

## Part 2: Demand Generation Engine

The demand gen engine is the fuel that converts a GTM strategy into revenue. Design it explicitly.

### Demand Gen Mix (Choose 2-3)

#### 1. **Inbound (Content, SEO, Community)**

Content attracts prospective customers and positions you as a thought leader.

**Tactics:**
- **Blog posts** (Target keywords customers search for. Example: "how to reduce sales cycle length")
- **Webinars** (Educational. Example: "Best practices for scaling your sales team")
- **Whitepapers/Reports** (Gated content. Example: "2024 Go-to-Market Benchmark")
- **Community** (Forum, Slack, Discord where customers hang out. Example: Revenue Ops community on Slack)
- **SEO optimization** (Technical SEO, keyword targeting, backlinks)

**Metrics:**
- Website traffic
- Lead generation (% of visitors who convert to lead)
- Lead quality (% of inbound leads that become SQL)
- Content engagement (time on page, pages per session)
- SEO rankings (rank for target keywords)

**CAC:** Low ($100-500 per customer, but acquisition is slow — 6-12 months)

**Best for:** Established brands with SEO authority, horizontal products with strong search volume, long-tail demand

#### 2. **Outbound (Email, Calls, LinkedIn)**

Direct outreach to target accounts.

**Tactics:**
- **Email sequences** (Personalized to ICP. 5-7 email sequence over 3 weeks)
- **LinkedIn campaigns** (Connection requests, then personalized messages)
- **Cold calling** (Still effective if list is warm and objection handling is good)
- **Account-Based Marketing (ABM)** (Targeted campaigns to top 100 accounts)
- **Networking events** (Conferences, roundtables, dinners)

**Metrics:**
- Outreach volume (emails/calls/connections per rep per day)
- Response rate (% who reply to outreach) — 2-5% typical
- Meeting booked rate (% of responses who take a meeting) — 20-40%
- Sales-qualified lead rate (% of meetings that become SQL) — 20-40%

**CAC:** Medium ($5-10K per customer, but acquisition is predictable)

**Best for:** Enterprise and mid-market SaaS, complex products, high ACV

#### 3. **Paid (Google Ads, LinkedIn, Facebook)**

Paid advertising to drive traffic and leads.

**Tactics:**
- **Google Search Ads** (Search intent is strongest. Example: "sales acceleration software")
- **LinkedIn Ads** (B2B targeting by job title, company, etc.)
- **Facebook/Instagram Ads** (Lower cost, but audience less relevant for B2B)
- **Retargeting** (Show ads to people who visited your site; conversion rates 2-3x higher)
- **Demand generation ads** (Education-focused, less directly selling)

**Metrics:**
- Cost per click (CPC): $1-10 depending on keyword, industry, competitiveness
- Click-through rate (CTR): 2-5%
- Conversion rate (click → lead): 1-5%
- Cost per lead (CPL): $20-100 depending on conversion rate
- Cost per acquisition (CAC): $500-5K depending on lead-to-close rate

**CAC:** Medium ($500-5K per customer, depending on conversion funnel)

**Best for:** Products with strong product-market fit (can afford paid CAC), competitive keywords, high ACV

#### 4. **Partner & Channel**

Leverage partners to drive demand.

**Tactics:**
- **Co-marketing** (Partner puts you in front of their customer base. Example: Integrated platform partners co-hosting a webinar)
- **Affiliate** (Partners get paid commission per customer)
- **Agency partnerships** (Agencies recommend you to their clients)
- **Reseller demand gen** (Partners do the outbound/inbound on your behalf)

**Metrics:**
- Partner-sourced leads
- Partner conversion rate
- Partner CAC (usually lower than direct)

**CAC:** Low ($100-1K per customer from partner, but you share margin)

**Best for:** Ecosystem plays, marketplace listings, channel-heavy models

#### 5. **Product-Driven (Free Trial, Freemium, Demo)**

The product itself is the demand gen engine.

**Tactics:**
- **Free trial** (Users experience product, get hooked, convert to paid)
- **Freemium** (Free tier, users hit ceiling, upgrade)
- **Product tour/demo** (Automated or in-app guide shows value quickly)
- **Viral loops** (Referrals, invitations, shared content)

**Metrics:**
- Trial signup rate (% of visitors who sign up for trial)
- Trial activation rate (% of signups who complete key action)
- Trial-to-paid conversion (% of activated trials who become paid)
- Time-to-value (how long to realize benefit)

**CAC:** Lowest (near-zero if organic; CAC is amortized across free users)

**Best for:** PLG businesses, horizontal products, self-service, low ACV

## Part 3: Sales Enablement Package

Salespeople can't succeed with a strategy document. They need concrete tools.

### Minimum Sales Enablement Package

#### 1. **Sales Playbook** (30-50 pages)

**Sections:**
- Sales process overview (stages, exit criteria, timeline)
- Discovery conversation guide (questions to ask, listening cues, needs assessment)
- Competitive positioning (your positioning vs. top 3 competitors; what to say if they compare)
- Demo script (15-min, 45-min, deep-dive versions; customizable for different personas)
- Objection handling (top 10 objections + response scripts)
- Deal structure (pricing, terms, typical contract)
- Close criteria (when is a deal ready to close?)
- Account expansion (how do we expand into existing accounts?)

#### 2. **Battle Cards** (One-pager per competitor)

**Content per battle card:**
- Competitor overview (who they are, what they do, pricing)
- Head-to-head comparison (features, price, support, ease of use)
- Why you win (your advantages)
- What they'll say (how they'll position vs. you)
- How to respond (what you say back)
- Case study (customer who chose you over them)

#### 3. **Demo Script & Product Walkthroughs**

**Three versions:**
- **15-minute demo** (High-level, for busy executives)
  - Problem statement (5 min)
  - Product demo (7 min)
  - Call to action (3 min)

- **45-minute demo** (Standard sales demo)
  - Warm up / discovery (5 min)
  - Problem statement (5 min)
  - Product walkthrough (20 min)
  - Q&A (10 min)
  - Next steps (5 min)

- **Deep-dive demo** (Technical evaluation with engineers)
  - Architecture (10 min)
  - Feature deep-dive (45 min)
  - Integration (15 min)
  - Q&A (20 min)

**Customization playbook:**
- For different personas (CMO, CTO, CEO, end user)
- For different use cases (they want A, demo A first, then B)
- For different company sizes (enterprise demo ≠ SMB demo)

#### 4. **Frequently Asked Questions & Objection Handling**

**Top objections to handle:**

| Objection | Root Cause | Response Script |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| "We're evaluating X competitor" | You're not the only option | Acknowledge. "That's smart to evaluate. What are your top 3 criteria? Let me show you how we score on each." |
| "It's too expensive" | Price objection | "I understand. Help me understand your budget. Also, what's the cost of the problem you're trying to solve? Usually, the ROI is [X]." |
| "We need to think about it" | Not ready, fear of change | "Of course. Let me ask — is there a piece of missing information that would help? Or is it a timing thing?" |
| "We'll do it in-house" | DIY option | "I appreciate that. Most of our customers tried that first. Usually they find [X problem]. Can I share how others solved this?" |
| "We're locked into [existing vendor]" | Switching cost | "Understood. When does your contract end? And what are the top 3 reasons you might want to switch?" |

#### 5. **Case Studies & Social Proof**

**Create 3-5 case studies highlighting:**
- Customer profile (company, size, industry)
- Problem they had
- How you solved it
- Results (quantified: X% faster, Y revenue impact, Z cost savings)
- Customer quote

**Placement:**
- Website (case study page)
- Sales enablement (reference during demos)
- Website footer (social proof badges: "Trusted by 500+ companies")
- LinkedIn (customer testimonials)

#### 6. **Pricing & Packaging Documentation**

Every salesperson must be able to articulate:
- What tiers exist
- What's in each tier
- Pricing per tier (or pricing methodology if custom)
- How to upgrade/downgrade
- Discounts (if negotiable, what's the floor?)
- Contract terms (annual, monthly, multi-year)
- Examples of typical deal structures

## Part 4: Marketing-to-Sales Handoff SLA

The handoff from marketing to sales is critical. Define it explicitly.

### Service Level Agreement (SLA)

```
MARKETING-TO-SALES SLA
════════════════════════════════════════════════

DEFINITION OF MQL (Marketing-Qualified Lead)
A prospect that has demonstrated interest and meets the ICP:
  Criteria:
    1. Identified company is in ICP (correct industry, size, location)
    2. Identified business user/decision maker (not admin)
    3. Engagement signal (downloaded asset, attended webinar, visited pricing page)
    4. No recent contact from sales (not already in pipeline)
  NOT counted as MQL if:
    1. Personal email (Gmail, Yahoo)
    2. No company name identified
    3. Bot signup
    4. Competitor company

MARKETING COMMITMENTS
  Lead Volume: [X] MQLs per month
  Lead Quality: [Y]% of MQLs become SQL (agreed definition)
  Lead Response: [Z] hours SLA from conversion to lead routing
  Lead Dedupe: Remove duplicate leads before handing to sales

SALES COMMITMENTS
  SQL Definition: Lead has been contacted, qualified via conversation
  Contact SLA: Sales must contact MQL within [Z] hours of receipt
  Feedback Loop: Sales reports back within 3 days: SQL / Not-Ready / Bad-Fit
  Feedback Quality: Weekly meeting to review lead quality and score adjustments

MONITORING & ESCALATION
  Weekly Review: Quality of leads, conversion rate, feedback
  If MQL → SQL conversion drops below [X]%, marketing will audit lead quality
  If sales doesn't contact leads within SLA 20% of the time, escalate to leadership
  Monthly Scorecard: MQLs generated, qualified, closed, metrics

ADJUSTMENTS
  If lead quality is poor: Adjust audience targeting, lead scoring, nurturing
  If sales isn't contacting: Discuss workload, priorities, territory alignment
  If conversion drops: Discuss sales process, qualification criteria
════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## Part 5: GTM Metrics Dashboard

You manage what you measure. Define the metrics upfront and track them obsessively.

### Dashboard Structure

**Tier 1: Revenue Metrics (The Goal)**
- MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
- ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)
- New ARR (from new customers)
- Expansion ARR (from existing customers)
- Churn (lost revenue)

**Tier 2: Pipeline Metrics (Early Indicators)**
- Opportunities in pipeline (by stage)
- Pipeline value (how much is in flight)
- Win rate (% of opportunities that close)
- Sales cycle (days from first contact to close)

**Tier 3: Conversion Metrics (Funnel Health)**
- Marketing → Sales Qualified Lead conversion
- SQL → Opportunity conversion
- Opportunity → Closed Won conversion
- Overall lead-to-close conversion rate

**Tier 4: Efficiency Metrics (Unit Economics)**
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
- CAC Payback (months to recover CAC)
- LTV (Lifetime Value)
- LTV:CAC ratio (should be >3:1)
- Gross margin (should be >60% for sustainable SaaS)

**Tier 5: Operational Metrics (Execution)**
- Demand gen volume (leads per month by channel)
- Lead quality (% of leads that qualify)
- Sales team productivity (demos per rep, closes per rep, quota attainment)
- Customer success (onboarding time, feature adoption, health score)

## Part 6: Post-Launch Optimization Cadence

The launch is day 0, not day 30. Design a cadence for continuous optimization.

### Weekly Standup (30 min)
- Review key metrics (MRR, pipeline, lead quality, sales activity)
- Identify blockers (product, customer success, sales support)
- Rapid fixes (kill campaigns not working, redirect budget)

### Weekly Deep-Dive (1-2 hours, rotating focus)
- Week 1: Demand gen performance (which channels working?)
- Week 2: Sales performance (which reps closing fastest? Any objection patterns?)
- Week 3: Customer success (onboarding time, adoption, early churn signals)
- Week 4: Financial review (unit economics, runway, path to profitability)

### Monthly Business Review (2 hours)
- Review month: Performance vs. plan, learnings, wins, losses
- Win/loss analysis: Why did we win deals? Why did we lose?
- Forecast next month: Pipeline, activity plans, resource needs
- Adjust: Pricing? Sales process? Demand gen mix?

### Quarterly GTM Review (Half-day)
- 90-day retrospective: What worked, what didn't, why?
- Decision: Scale, iterate, pivot, or shut down?
- 90-day plan: What's next (optimize existing motion, expand to new segment, build new channel)?

## Quality Gates for Launch

Before launching:

1. **Are all systems connected?** (CRM talks to email platform, landing pages track properly, data flows to dashboard)
2. **Is the sales team trained and comfortable?** (Ask them: "Can you articulate positioning? How would you handle objection X?")
3. **Are success metrics baselined?** (Do you know what "good" looks like? Benchmarks from competitors or prior launches?)
4. **Is customer success ready?** (Onboarding process documented, support team staffed, training materials ready)
5. **Can you identify problems in real-time?** (Dashboards active, alerts set, someone is watching daily)
