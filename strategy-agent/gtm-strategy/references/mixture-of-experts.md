# GTM Mixture of Experts Panel

## Purpose & Scope

When you've built a GTM strategy, run it through a panel of five expert personas. Each expert questions from their unique lens. Together, they catch blindspots, validate unit economics, identify execution risks, and pressure-test the strategy before it goes live.

This is not consensus-building. This is stress-testing. The experts may disagree. That's the point. Their tension surfaces the critical tradeoffs.

---

## Panel Composition & Personas

### Expert 1: Revenue Operations Leader

**Identity:** Former head of sales operations at a $100M SaaS company. Data-driven, metrics obsessed, funnel optimization expert. Thinks in funnels, cohorts, and unit economics. No tolerance for vague targets.

**Lens:** "Does this math work? Can we execute at scale? Are metrics tracked and monitored?"

**Key Questions:**

1. **Funnel Architecture:**
   - What's the lead-to-close conversion rate? (By stage)
   - What's the CAC? (Fully loaded, by channel, by segment)
   - What's the payback period? (Months to recover CAC from gross margin)
   - Is each metric tied to a real person's responsibility?

2. **Capacity & Scaling:**
   - If you're targeting $10M ARR from sales motion, how many reps do you need?
   - What's quota per rep? (Based on ACV, win rate, cycle, activity)
   - What's the ramp time for new reps? (When do they hit productivity?)
   - Do you have enough pipeline to support that many reps without destroying win rates?

3. **Unit Economics:**
   - CAC < LTV ÷ 3? (If not, you're money-losing)
   - Gross margin > 70%? (If not, can't sustain)
   - What's the CAC to LTV ratio by segment? (Should be consistent)
   - What happens to unit economics if you're wrong about price, churn, or ACV?

4. **Systems & Tracking:**
   - Is this measurable? (Can you track every metric in real-time?)
   - Is accountability clear? (One person owns each metric)
   - What's the leading indicator? (What predicts revenue 3 months out?)
   - Do you have targets for each week of launch? (Or just month-end?)

5. **Stress Tests:**
   - If we miss lead targets by 20%, what happens to revenue?
   - If sales cycle extends from 60 to 90 days, what's the impact?
   - If churn is 5% instead of 3%, does the unit economics still work?
   - If we have to drop price 20% to be competitive, are we still profitable?

**Verdict Scoring:**
- GREEN: All metrics math out. Unit economics are sound. Systems are in place.
- YELLOW: Metrics are reasonable but depend on several assumptions. Systems need upgrade.
- RED: Unit economics don't work. Too many unknowns. Numbers don't add up.

**Hard Stop Conditions:**
- CAC > LTV ÷ 2 (Deal is money-losing)
- Gross margin < 50% (Model is not scalable)
- No way to measure success metrics (Can't manage what you can't measure)
- Pipeline coverage ratio < 2:1 (Reps won't have enough to sell)

### Expert 2: Enterprise Sales VP

**Identity:** 15+ years closing complex enterprise deals (ACV $500K-$5M). Negotiated with procurement teams, board members, CFOs. Built enterprise sales teams from scratch. Knows deal dynamics intimately.

**Lens:** "Is this real? Can we actually win these deals? What's really going to happen in a deal?"

**Key Questions:**

1. **Deal Dynamics & Complexity:**
   - How many people are in the buying committee? (Real estimate)
   - Who's the economic buyer? (CFO, CRO, CEO?)
   - Is there procurement involvement? (Will we need SOC 2, DPA, custom terms?)
   - What's the real risk/concern they're evaluating against?

2. **Proof Points & Evaluation:**
   - Do we have reference customers at their scale? (They want to call a peer)
   - What will they want to see? (Demo? POC? Case studies? Analyst report?)
   - What's the procurement timeline? (90 days? 6 months? Legal review?)
   - Are we prepared for technical evaluations? (Security questions? Architecture review?)

3. **Objection Reality:**
   - What's the real objection? (Not "it's too expensive" — the real barrier)
   - Is this a price objection or a fit objection? (Different responses)
   - Will they just buy from the vendor already in the budget? (Switching cost is real)
   - What's the probability they actually execute after evaluation? (Many evaluate but never buy)

4. **Sales Process:**
   - Is the sales cycle realistic for the ACV? ($500K deal in 60 days is unrealistic)
   - Do we have enough proof points in time for close? (If we need 180-day eval, can't close in 90)
   - Who owns the relationship at close? (If it's a junior rep, enterprise deals fail)
   - What's the win vs. tie-goes-to-incumbent risk? (In enterprise, ties usually go to the vendor already in place)

5. **Expansion & Retention:**
   - What's the post-close risk? (Implementation disasters destroy retention)
   - Do we have customer success capability for $500K contracts? (Can't use light-touch CS)
   - What's the expansion story? (How does $500K deal become $1M?)
   - Are we prepared for champion churn? (If champion leaves, deal is at risk even after close)

6. **Competitive Conversation:**
   - Who are we really competing against? (Incumbent, alternative, or DIY?)
   - What's our actual competitive advantage at this account?
   - What will our competitor claim? (And will it land?)
   - How will we differentiate in technical evaluation?

**Verdict Scoring:**
- GREEN: Deal dynamics are clear. Buying committee is mapped. Objections are real but handlable. Competitive position is strong.
- YELLOW: Deal dynamics are unclear. Buying committee is larger than expected. Competitive intensity is higher.
- RED: Buying committee is too large to close quickly. Procurement will block. Competitor is entrenched.

**Hard Stop Conditions:**
- Buying committee >8 people and very siloed (deals won't close)
- Economic buyer isn't identified or engaged (won't move forward)
- Procurement is not discussed or planned for (deal will stall)
- No reference customer at similar scale (enterprise will not buy)
- Competitor is entrenched with multi-year contract (switching will be forced loss)

### Expert 3: PLG Growth Leader

**Identity:** Built growth motions at a $50M+ ARR PLG company (e.g., Slack, Figma, Notion). Obsessed with activation, engagement, and viral mechanics. Thinks in product metrics, cohorts, and retention.

**Lens:** "Can we remove friction? Is the product actually sticky? Can we build network effects?"

**Key Questions:**

1. **Product-Market Fit Signals:**
   - What's the aha moment? (When does the user realize value? Day 1 or week 3?)
   - What's activation rate? (% of signups who complete the key action)
   - What's retention? (% of users still active 30 days after signup)
   - Is retention linear or exponential? (Are they getting MORE engaged or less?)

2. **Friction Analysis:**
   - How many steps to value? (If >5, too many)
   - Where do users drop off? (At signup, onboarding, or feature discovery?)
   - Is anything forced into the onboarding? (Optional setup should be optional)
   - Are there any payment friction points? (Credit card upfront kills conversion)

3. **Engagement & Stickiness:**
   - What drives repeat usage? (Daily? Weekly? Monthly?)
   - What's the engagement curve? (Declining = retention problem)
   - Are there power users pulling the retention up? (Or average retention is bad?)
   - Is there a natural usage limit that forces upgrade? (Or is upgrade forced and arbitrary?)

4. **Viral & Growth Loops:**
   - Is the product naturally multi-user? (Or is it single-user without expansion?)
   - Are there viral mechanics? (Referrals, invitations, shared content?)
   - What's the viral coefficient? (1 user brings X new users; if <1, not viral)
   - Can we improve virality without compromising product?

5. **Free-to-Paid Conversion:**
   - When do users hit the paywall? (Day 1? Day 30? Never?)
   - Is the limit clear or punishing? (Transparent = better conversion)
   - Is the pricing relevant to usage? (Usage-based tiers make sense for PLG)
   - What's the free-to-paid conversion rate? (If <5%, paywall is wrong or product isn't ready)

6. **Freemium Economics:**
   - How much of the product is free? (Too much = won't convert; too little = won't activate)
   - What's the marginal cost of free users? (Infrastructure, support, payment processing?)
   - Free-to-paid ratio (if 100 free users support 5 paid users, that's 5% conversion)
   - LTV of paid user vs. marginal cost of free user (need to be 10x difference minimum)

**Verdict Scoring:**
- GREEN: Activation is strong. Retention curve is good. Paywall is well-designed. Viral loop exists.
- YELLOW: Activation is OK but could be faster. Retention dips after 30 days. Paywall needs tuning.
- RED: Activation is low (<25%). Retention drops off a cliff. Paywall is too early or too late.

**Hard Stop Conditions:**
- Activation rate <25% (Too many users leaving before experiencing value)
- 30-day retention <40% (Users aren't sticky; product-market fit is not there)
- Viral coefficient <0.5 (Won't grow; will grind to a halt)
- Free-to-paid conversion <3% (If paywall is good, this should be 5%+)

### Expert 4: Pricing Strategist

**Identity:** Studied behavioral economics under Kahneman. Built pricing models for SaaS and enterprise software. Ran pricing experimentation at scale. Understands willingness-to-pay, packaging, and price elasticity.

**Lens:** "Are we capturing the right amount of value? Is packaging optimized? Will customers pay?"

**Key Questions:**

1. **Value Capture & Price Justification:**
   - What's the value delivered? (Quantified: ROI, time saved, revenue generated?)
   - What's the alternative cost? (What would customer do if we didn't exist?)
   - What's our price relative to value? (Should capture 30-50% of value)
   - Can we justify the price to a customer in 2 sentences?

2. **Willingness-to-Pay Research:**
   - Have we researched WTP? (Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger, conjoint?)
   - What do customers say they'd pay? (Hypothetical)
   - What do customers actually pay? (Negotiated deals tell the real story)
   - Is there pricing elasticity data? (What happens if we raise price 10%?)

3. **Packaging Architecture:**
   - Why are the tiers different? (Feature gating or usage-based or seats?)
   - Are features gated optimally? (Gate features that unlock expansion, not core value)
   - Is there a clear upgrade path? (Can customer upgrade from Good to Better without buying bigger than needed?)
   - Does pricing align with value? (Higher-value tier should be bought by higher-value customers)

4. **Price Positioning vs. Competitors:**
   - What are competitors charging? (Know this cold)
   - Why are we more/less expensive? (Justification must be clear)
   - Is our positioning defensible? (Or are we just cheaper/more expensive without reason?)
   - What happens if competitor drops price 20%? (Can we compete? Should we?)

5. **Pricing Psychology:**
   - Are we using anchoring? (Show expensive option first = higher perceived value)
   - Is there a decoy option? (Third option that makes higher tier look attractive)
   - Are we bundling intelligently? (Bundle can increase perceived value)
   - Is the price framing optimized? (Is "$100/month" or "$1,200/year" better?)

6. **Price Change Management:**
   - How will customers react to price increases? (Churn elasticity = X%?)
   - Do we have a grandfather policy? (Protect existing customers to minimize churn)
   - Is the communication clear? (Why are we increasing price? What's new?)
   - Have we tested with a segment first? (Or are we raising across-the-board?)

**Verdict Scoring:**
- GREEN: Price is justified by value. Packaging is optimized. WTP research validates. Positioning is defensible.
- YELLOW: Price is reasonable but could be higher. Packaging has feature gating issues. Competitor pricing is close.
- RED: Price is unjustified. Packaging is confusing. Customers will churn if price increases.

**Hard Stop Conditions:**
- No value justification (Can't explain why we're worth the price)
- Price is below competitor without clear reason (Will face downward pressure)
- Packaging gates core features (Customers feel punished for tiers)
- Willingness-to-pay research contradicts price (Pricing too high or too low)
- LTV:CAC won't work at this price point (Unit economics broken)

### Expert 5: Channel Chief

**Identity:** Built partner/channel ecosystems at 3 companies. Recruited, onboarded, scaled, and managed 100+ partners. Understands partner incentives, conflict, program design.

**Lens:** "Can we scale through partners? Will they commit? What's the true channel economics?"

**Key Questions:**

1. **Partner Fit & Recruiting:**
   - Have we identified target partners? (Or is this theoretical?)
   - Do target partners have distribution into our ICP? (Or are we hoping?)
   - Are there 5+ viable partners? (Need redundancy; can't be dependent on 1 partner)
   - What's the partner incentive? (Why will they sell us vs. competitors?)

2. **Program Design & Economics:**
   - What margin are we offering partners? (30%? 50%? If <25%, won't commit)
   - What's the payback to partner? (If they need 6 months to sell first deal, they'll drop out)
   - Do partner economics work? (If partner makes <$50K in year 1, they deprioritize)
   - How does this compare to what partners make from other vendors?

3. **Enablement & Support:**
   - Can we scale enablement to 50 partners? (Or will we run out of resources?)
   - What's the support model? (Email? Slack? Dedicated person?)
   - Do partners have warm leads already, or are we asking them to cold sell?
   - What's the time-to-productivity for a partner? (Months 1-3 or months 1-6?)

4. **Channel Conflict & Governance:**
   - Do we sell direct AND through channel? (Conflict is inevitable)
   - How do we handle territorial disputes? (If partner says "only sell to my accounts")
   - What's the escalation path if partner is underperforming?
   - Can we terminate a partner if needed, or are they too entrenched?

5. **Growth & Scaling:**
   - What's the growth rate we're asking of partners? (30% YoY is typical; 100% is unrealistic)
   - If channels works, can we manage 100+ partners? (Operational scaling challenge)
   - What happens to channel revenue if key partner leaves? (Concentration risk)
   - How do we build a healthy mix (no single partner >30% of channel revenue)?

6. **Competitive Positioning:**
   - Can partners sell both us and competitors? (If yes, they have no incentive to prioritize us)
   - What's the co-sell story? (Are we asking partners to replace a competitor in an account, or add us?)
   - Do partners understand why they should push us vs. others?
   - Is there a kill condition if partner sells competitor? (Or is it OK?)

**Verdict Scoring:**
- GREEN: Partners are identified and interested. Program design is attractive. Enablement is scalable. Conflict is manageable.
- YELLOW: Partners need recruiting still. Program economics could be better. Enablement will be resource-intensive.
- RED: No partners identified yet. Program will cannibalize direct sales. Conflict is unresolved.

**Hard Stop Conditions:**
- No partners identified (Can't launch channel without partners)
- Margin to partners is <20% (Won't be prioritized)
- Partner economics don't work in year 1 (They'll leave)
- No plan for direct-channel conflict (Sales will sabotage partners or vice versa)
- Operational capacity doesn't exist (1 person to manage 50 partners = failure)

## Synthesis Protocol: Running the Panel

### Pre-Panel Preparation

Assemble the GTM strategy in this format:

```
GTM STRATEGY SUMMARY
═══════════════════════════════════════════

COMPANY PROFILE
  Stage: [Pre-PMF / Growth / Mature]
  Current ARR: $[X]
  Target ARR: $[Y] in 12 months
  Team: [# sales, # marketing, # product]

GTM STRATEGY
  ICP: [One-sentence definition]
  Segments: [Top 2-3 segments with ACV]
  Positioning: [Core value prop]

  Sales Motion: [PLG / SLG / Hybrid]
    - Sales cycle: [X] days
    - ACV: $[X]
    - Buying committee: [N] people

  Pricing: [Model, tiers, price ranges]

  Channels: [Direct / Partner / Marketplace / Hybrid with %]

  Demand Gen: [Channels and budget]

FINANCIAL MODEL (Annual)
  Revenue target: $[X]
  Gross margin: [Y]%
  CAC: $[X]
  LTV: $[Y]
  LTV:CAC: [Z]:1
  CAC payback: [Z] months
  Marketing spend: $[X]
  Sales spend: $[Y]

EXECUTION PLAN
  Launch timeline: [T-90 to T+30]
  Team: [Who owns what]
  Key risks: [Top 3]
  Success metrics: [How we measure success]
═══════════════════════════════════════════
```

### Panel Review Process

**Step 1: Each expert reads the strategy independently (15 min)**

**Step 2: Each expert scores the strategy (10 min)**
- Green: I'm confident this will work
- Yellow: This will work, but with these caveats / adjustments needed
- Red: This won't work as written. Material changes needed.

**Step 3: Each expert presents their verdict (5 min each; 25 min total)**
- What's the verdict? (Green / Yellow / Red)
- Top 3 strengths
- Top 3 concerns
- Hard stops (if any)

**Step 4: Moderated discussion (30 min)**
- Where do experts agree? (Shared concerns = real concerns)
- Where do experts disagree? (Different lenses = tradeoff decision)
- Can concerns be addressed? (Mitigated, not eliminated)
- What changes would move a Red to Yellow, or Yellow to Green?

**Step 5: Synthesis & recommendation (15 min)**
- Summary verdict (Pass / Conditional Pass / Reject)
- Top 3 items to fix
- Top 3 items that are strong
- Conditional passes: "Proceed if you do X and Y"

---

## Reconciling Expert Disagreement

Experts will disagree. That's good. It surfaces tradeoffs.

**Example:**
- **Revenue Ops expert says:** "LTV:CAC is 3:1. That's barely sustainable. Recommend raising price."
- **Pricing strategist says:** "If we raise price, WTP research shows we'll lose 15% of customers. Revenue goes down."
- **Enterprise Sales VP says:** "At enterprise scale, we can raise price. SMB is price-sensitive, but they're not our core. Focus on enterprise."

**Resolution:** Not consensus. Clear tradeoff. Decision: Focus on enterprise (higher ACV, price-tolerant) and reduce investment in SMB channel.

## When to Use the Panel

Run the panel:
1. **Before major GTM launch** — Stress-test the strategy
2. **Before significant pivots** — New pricing, new channel, new ICP
3. **When unit economics seem off** — Reality-check the math
4. **When experts disagree internally** — External perspectives break ties
5. **Quarterly review** — Keep strategy sharpened as reality evolves

Don't run the panel:
- On minor optimizations (tweaking email sequence copy)
- On new marketing campaigns (unless GTM-level shift)
- Too frequently (creates analysis paralysis)

## Hard Stop Conditions (Across Panel)

If ANY of these are true, reject the strategy and rework:

**Financial Hard Stops:**
1. LTV < 3x CAC (Unit economics are broken)
2. Gross margin < 50% (Model won't scale)
3. CAC payback > 24 months (Assume churn, won't survive)

**Market Hard Stops:**
1. ICP is too broad (No clear target = diffuse sales effort)
2. No customer validation (Haven't talked to real customers who want this)
3. Competitor already dominates with 10x market share (Won't displace)

**Product Hard Stops:**
1. Activation time > 30 days (Too slow for PLG; too risky for enterprise)
2. Churn is >10% monthly (Product-market fit not there)
3. NPS < 50 (Customers don't love it enough to refer)

**Execution Hard Stops:**
1. Team doesn't exist (Can't hire fast enough)
2. No product-market fit evidence (Won't land with customers)
3. Resource commitment is misaligned (Company isn't serious about this)

If you have 2+ hard stops, rework the strategy. Don't proceed.
