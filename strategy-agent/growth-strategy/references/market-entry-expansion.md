# Market Entry & Expansion Strategy

This reference operationalizes market entry and expansion: geographic expansion, segment expansion, channel expansion, adjacency analysis, beachhead selection, and entry mode decision frameworks.

---

## Market Attractiveness Framework

Before entering any new market, score on these dimensions:

```
MARKET ATTRACTIVENESS SCORECARD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Market: [Name (geography, segment, channel)]

SIZE & GROWTH (Weight: 30%)
  TAM (Total Addressable Market): [$] or [# customers]
  CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate): [%]
  Score: [0-10]
    - <$50M TAM: 3/10 (too small)
    - $50M-$500M TAM: 5-7/10
    - >$500M TAM: 8-10/10
    - <0% CAGR: 0/10 (declining)
    - 0-5% CAGR: 3-5/10
    - 5-15% CAGR: 7-8/10
    - >15% CAGR: 9-10/10

COMPETITIVE INTENSITY (Weight: 25%)
  # of Entrenched Competitors: [#]
  Leader Market Share: [%]
  Competitor Differentiation: [Low / Medium / High]
  Score: [0-10]
    - 1-2 weak competitors: 8-10/10
    - 3-4 medium competitors: 5-7/10
    - 5+ strong competitors, consolidated: 2-4/10

ENTRY BARRIERS (Weight: 20%)
  Capital Required: [$]
  Regulatory/Legal: [Low / Medium / High]
  Localization Required: [Low / Medium / High]
  Relationships Required: [Low / Medium / High]
  Score: [0-10]
    - Low barriers overall: 8-10/10
    - Medium barriers: 5-7/10
    - High barriers: 2-4/10

STRATEGIC FIT (Weight: 15%)
  Product Fit: [0-10] — Does product work with <2 changes?
  Customer Overlap: [0-10] — Can you reuse sales/marketing?
  Brand Fit: [0-10] — Does it fit brand/positioning?
  Score: [Average of 3]

OVERALL SCORE: [Weighted average of all dimensions, 0-10]

GO/NO-GO DECISION:
  8-10: GREEN — Pursue aggressively
  6-7: YELLOW — Pursue with caution; assess barriers further
  <6: RED — Skip; low probability of success

EXAMPLE:
  Market: UK enterprise SaaS
  TAM: $800M, CAGR: 8% = 9/10 (SIZE)
  Competitors: 4 strong (Salesforce, Microsoft, Oracle, SAP) = 4/10 (COMPETITION)
  Barriers: Need 3-month localization, GDPR compliance, partnership with local SI = 5/10 (BARRIERS)
  Fit: Product works with <1 change, same ICP, brand fits global positioning = 8/10 (FIT)
  Weighted: (9×0.3) + (4×0.25) + (5×0.2) + (8×0.15) = 2.7 + 1 + 1 + 1.2 = 5.9 = YELLOW
  Decision: Pursue, but assess barriers (GDPR, partnership dependencies) first
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Geographic Expansion Framework

### Step 1: Select Beachhead Geography

Don't try to enter all markets at once. Own one geography ruthlessly first.

```
BEACHHEAD SELECTION CRITERIA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Candidate Geographies: [List 3-5 options: Canada, UK, Germany, Japan, Australia, etc.]

For EACH candidate, score:

SIZE: Is TAM >$100M?
  [Yes: 8/10 | $50-100M: 5/10 | <$50M: 2/10]

LOCALIZATION: How much product/compliance work required?
  [None (English-speaking, no compliance): 9/10]
  [Low (language translation, light compliance): 7/10]
  [Medium (product changes, moderate compliance): 5/10]
  [High (significant product changes, heavy compliance): 2/10]

ACCESSIBILITY: How easy to reach customers?
  [Same language, direct sales possible: 9/10]
  [Different language, need partner: 6/10]
  [Heavily regulated, need specialized partner: 3/10]

COMPETITIVE PRESSURE: How strong are entrenched competitors?
  [Weak incumbents, clear white space: 9/10]
  [Medium incumbents, some differentiation possible: 5/10]
  [Strong incumbents, hard to differentiate: 2/10]

PARTNER AVAILABILITY: Can you find good partners/distribution?
  [Strong partner ecosystem: 9/10]
  [Partner options exist: 6/10]
  [Limited options: 3/10]

ECONOMIC CONDITIONS: What's the buying power / growth stage?
  [High GDP per capita, strong growth: 9/10]
  [Medium GDP, medium growth: 5/10]
  [Low GDP, slow growth: 2/10]

SCORE EACH CANDIDATE:
  Score = (Size × 0.25) + (Localization × 0.20) + (Accessibility × 0.20) + (Competition × 0.15) + (Partners × 0.12) + (Economy × 0.08)

SELECT: Choose beachhead = highest score that you can execute (6-month entry target)

EXAMPLE:
  Option A (Canada): 8+9+9+8+8+8 → Weighted = 8.3
  Option B (UK): 9+7+8+5+7+8 → Weighted = 7.5
  Option C (Germany): 9+6+7+4+6+7 → Weighted = 6.8
  Option D (Japan): 9+3+4+6+5+6 → Weighted = 5.9

  Beachhead: Canada (highest score, easiest to execute)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Step 2: Select Entry Mode

| Mode | Timeline | Capital | Control | Risk | Best For |
|------|----------|---------|---------|------|----------|
| **Self-Serve/Direct** | 12-24 months | Low-Medium | High | Product-market fit required | Small markets, low localization, English-speaking |
| **Sales Hiring** | 9-18 months | Medium | High | Finding talent, ramp time | Mid-size markets, enterprise sales required |
| **Partner (Distributor/VAR)** | 3-6 months | Low | Low | Partner incentive, quality control | Fast entry, complex markets, need local expertise |
| **Acquisition** | Immediate | High | Medium | Overpayment, integration risk | Market consolidation, established player needed |
| **JV/Minority Investment** | 6-12 months | High | Low | Partner dependency, control loss | Co-market entry, high capital required, risk-sharing |

```

### Step 3: Execute Entry (by Mode)

#### A. Self-Serve / Direct Model

**Timeline:** 6-12 months to meaningful revenue

```
SELF-SERVE ENTRY PLAN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Month 1: Localization
  - Translate product (or assess if needed)
  - Add local payment methods
  - Ensure compliance (GDPR, data residency, etc.)
  - Budget: $50K-200K

Month 2-3: Marketing Setup
  - Run paid ads in new market (same channels as home market)
  - A/B test messaging (is product positioning same?)
  - Create local case studies (if possible)
  - Budget: $50K-100K

Month 3-6: Pilot & Iterate
  - Target 10-20 customers in pilot phase
  - Gather feedback on product fit, pricing, messaging
  - Iterate quickly based on feedback
  - Budget: $100K marketing + $0 product

Month 6-12: Scale
  - If metrics support (CAC:LTV ≥ 1:3), scale marketing
  - Hire local customer success (if needed)
  - Invest in local partnerships for distribution
  - Budget: $200K-500K

SUCCESS METRICS (Gate Criteria):
  - Month 3: ≥ 5 customers, ≥ 50% activation rate
  - Month 6: ≥ 20 customers, CAC:LTV trending toward 1:3
  - Month 12: ≥ 100 customers, clear profitability path

RISKS:
  - Marketing CAC higher than home market (competition, language issues)
  - Product doesn't fit (need localization)
  - Payment/compliance issues delay launch
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

#### B. Sales Hiring Model

**Timeline:** 9-18 months to revenue traction

```
SALES HIRING ENTRY PLAN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Month 1-2: Hire Sales Leader
  - Recruit Country/Regional GM or Sales Lead (not just AE)
  - Leader should know local market, have relationships, understand sales process
  - Budget: $150K-250K annual salary + 6-month to productivity

Month 2-3: Sales Training & Enablement
  - Sales leader learns product in detail
  - Develops sales playbook for new market
  - Creates local case studies if possible
  - Budget: $50K

Month 3-6: Hire Sales Team & Start Pitching
  - Hire 2-3 AEs (sales leader hires, not you)
  - Sales leader takes 5-10 meetings with prospects (warm intros)
  - Build pipeline with 15-20 opportunities
  - Budget: $300K-500K (2-3 AEs × $150K salary + benefits)

Month 6-12: Ramp & Close
  - AEs ramping; expect 6-month to full productivity
  - Close 5-10 customers from pipeline
  - Build repeatable process
  - Budget: $500K-1M (full team + marketing support)

Month 12-18: Scale or Evaluate
  - If 10+ customers and LTV trending positive: Hire more AEs
  - If <10 customers or LTV negative: Pause and reassess
  - Budget: Scale based on performance

SUCCESS METRICS (Gate Criteria):
  - Month 4: Sales leader hired, pipeline of 15-20
  - Month 6: First 2-3 customers closed, AEs producing
  - Month 12: 8-10 customers, unit economics trending positive
  - Month 18: 20+ customers, hiring second AE, expansion revenue starting

RISKS:
  - Sales leader is wrong person for market (chemistry, skills)
  - Sales cycle longer than expected (different buying process)
  - Deals fall through due to localization or compliance issues
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

#### C. Partner / Distribution Model

**Timeline:** 3-6 months to first customers

```
PARTNER ENTRY PLAN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Month 1: Identify Partners
  - Research 10-15 potential partners (resellers, systems integrators, VARs)
  - Criteria: Already serve your ICP, have sales team, reputable
  - Warm intro from network > cold outreach
  - Budget: $0-20K (research, travel for partner meetings)

Month 1-2: Recruit Partner(s)
  - Target 2-3 partners for initial launch (not 1; need diversity)
  - Propose economics: Partner margin (typically 20-30%)
  - Co-marketing commitment: You provide assets, partner brings customers
  - Signed contract with exclusivity (optional; typically 12-month territory exclusivity)
  - Budget: $50K-100K (partner enablement, co-marketing fund)

Month 2-3: Partner Enablement
  - Train partner sales team on product (1-2 day workshop)
  - Provide marketing materials, case studies, demo environment
  - Establish support escalation path (you or partner supports customers?)
  - Set up deal registration process (partner claims account, avoids conflicts)
  - Budget: $50K-100K

Month 3-6: First Customers & Iteration
  - Partners pitch to 10-20 prospects
  - Close 3-5 customers via partner channel
  - Gather feedback on partnership model (is it working?)
  - Iterate: Increase partner margin? More enablement? Different partners?
  - Budget: $50K marketing + co-op funds

SUCCESS METRICS (Gate Criteria):
  - Month 2: 2+ partners signed, contracts executed
  - Month 3: Partners trained, 10+ prospects in pipeline
  - Month 4: 3+ customers closed via partners
  - Month 6: 10+ customers, repeatable process, partner retention >80%

RISKS:
  - Partners prioritize competitors' products (they sell 50+ products)
  - Partner channel conflicts with your own sales (should you hire direct too?)
  - Partner quality control (bad partners give you bad reputation)
  - Customers have issues with partner (support quality, alignment)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Segment Expansion Framework

**Segment = Different customer persona within the same market**

Examples:
- SMB vs. Enterprise (within same geography)
- Marketing vs. Sales (within same industry)
- Consulting firms vs. Law firms (within professional services)

```

  [Buyers have budget / can spend freely: 9/10]

## Channel Expansion Framework

**Channel = How customers discover and buy (direct sales, partnerships, marketplace, etc.)**

Examples:
- Direct sales → Add partner channel
- Direct → Add marketplace channel
- Sales → Add self-serve channel

### Channel Selection

```
CHANNEL ATTRACTIVENESS

Channel: [Name, e.g., "System Integrator partners"]

REACH: How many customers can this channel access?
  [>10K customers in channel: 9/10]
  [1K-10K: 6/10]
  [<1K: 2/10]

WILLINGNESS: Will channel partners actively promote your product?
  [Yes, strong incentive: 8/10]

  [Yes, both win at standard margins: 8/10]

  Average: 7/10 = MEDIUM-HIGH, worth pursuing

## Adjacency Analysis (Bain Methodology)

Use adjacency analysis to identify the most attractive new markets to expand into.

```
ADJACENCY MAPPING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Core Business: [Your current market]
  Example: Project management software for marketing teams

Adjacent Market Concentric Rings:

RING 1 (Closest): Same customer, different use case
  Example: Project management for sales teams, customer success teams
  Characteristics: Same buyer, same budget line, small product changes
  Entry effort: Low
  Timeline: 3-6 months
  Probability of success: 90%+

RING 2: Different customer in same market
  Example: Finance/HR teams (same company, different department)
  Characteristics: Different buyer, same workflow problem, likely same product
  Entry effort: Low-Medium
  Timeline: 6-12 months
  Probability of success: 80%+

RING 3: New market with similar product needs
  Example: Professional services firms (different from marketing, but use PM for projects)
  Characteristics: Different customer, similar problem, product might need 1-2 changes
  Entry effort: Medium
  Timeline: 9-18 months
  Probability of success: 60-70%

RING 4 (Furthest): Completely new market or product
  Example: Manufacturing operations (completely different problem, different product needed)
  Characteristics: Different customer, different problem, new product likely
  Entry effort: High
  Timeline: 18-36 months
  Probability of success: 30-40%

STRATEGIC PRINCIPLE:
  Expand Ring 1 first (highest probability, fastest)
  Expand Ring 2 & 3 in parallel once Ring 1 is mature
  Only pursue Ring 4 if core market is saturated and capital available

EXAMPLE - Slack Adjacency Expansion:
  Core: Team communication for tech companies
  Ring 1: Team communication for finance, sales, operations (same company, different department)
  Ring 2: Team communication for non-tech companies (different customer, same problem)
  Ring 3: Asynchronous communication, video conferencing (different problem, expansion of product)
  Ring 4: Project management, CRM (completely different)

  Actual Path: Slack focused on Ring 1-2 (same customer base expansion), hired VP for each vertical
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Beachhead Strategy: Dominating Your Entry Market

Don't try to win the whole market on entry. Own one niche completely, then expand.

```
BEACHHEAD SELECTION & DOMINATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Beachhead Criteria:
  1. Size: Large enough to be meaningful ($10M-$100M market)
  2. Focus: Narrowly defined (specific buyer, specific problem)
  3. Accessibility: Reachable without massive CAC (partner, word-of-mouth possible)
  4. Defensibility: Can you own 50%+ of this niche?
  5. Leverage: Can you expand to adjacent niches from here?

EXAMPLE - Salesforce Geographic Beachhead:
  Not: "Enterprise software globally"
  But: "Sales pipeline software for tech companies in Silicon Valley"
  Why: Niche was small enough to understand, accessible (many tech companies nearby),
       defensible (no good competitors), leverage point (expand to other US geographies,
       then to other industries, then to finance/consulting)

BEACHHEAD CAPTURE PLAN
  Goal: Capture 50%+ of beachhead market within 12-24 months

  Month 1-3: Get to 5-10 customers (proof)
  Month 3-6: Get to 20-30 customers (word-of-mouth starts)
  Month 6-12: Get to 50%+ penetration of addressable beachhead
  Month 12-24: Expand to adjacent beachhead

EXPANSION SEQUENCE FROM BEACHHEAD
  Beachhead 1: Tech companies in San Francisco (captured)
    ↓
  Expansion 1: Tech companies in California (similar geography, similar companies)
    ↓
  Expansion 2: Tech companies nationally in US (same segment, different geography)
    ↓
  Expansion 3: Consulting firms nationally in US (different segment, proven go-to-market)
    ↓
  Expansion 4: Financial services globally (different segment, different geography)

  Each expansion layer builds on prior layer's advantages (brand, case studies,
  operational model, partnerships)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Risk Assessment by Entry Mode

```
ENTRY MODE RISK MATRIX
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

MODE: Self-Serve / Direct Sales / Partnership / Acquisition / JV

EXECUTION RISKS (can you deliver the plan?):
  Self-Serve: Product localization delays, marketing doesn't work in new market
  Direct Sales: Hiring right sales leader takes longer than expected
  Partnership: Partners deprioritize, poor quality, channel conflict
  Acquisition: Integration harder than expected, culture clash, key people leave
  JV: Partner misalignment, control loss, governance friction

MARKET RISKS (does market behave as expected?):
  All modes: Market growth slower than forecast
  All modes: Competitive incumbent retaliation
  All modes: Regulatory changes, compliance cost overruns

FINANCIAL RISKS (can you afford it?):
  Self-Serve: Low capital risk; marketing burn if demand weak
  Direct Sales: High payroll burn; expects 6+ months to productivity
  Partnership: Low capital; potential margin loss if channel margin too high
  Acquisition: Very high capital; overpayment risk; integration cost
  JV: High capital; shared loss if market weak
