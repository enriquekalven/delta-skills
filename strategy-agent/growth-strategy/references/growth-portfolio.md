# Growth Portfolio Management

This reference operationalizes the Three Horizons framework, growth portfolio scoring, stage-gate processes, kill criteria, and portfolio rebalancing triggers for allocating capital and resources across growth bets.

---

## Three Horizons Framework (McKinsey)

The Three Horizons framework divides growth strategy by time horizon and resource allocation.

```
THREE HORIZONS OVERVIEW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HORIZON 1: CORE GROWTH
Focus: Deepen existing market, product, channels; extract maximum value
Revenue today: 85-95% of total
Contribution to revenue in 3 years: 60-70%
Capital allocation: 50-60% of budget
Time to payoff: Immediate to 12 months (operational improvements)
Risk level: Low (proven model)

Examples:
  - SaaS company: Land-and-expand in existing customer base
  - DTC: Scale proven marketing channels
  - Enterprise: Penetrate new personas in existing accounts

Mindset: Optimization, operational excellence, predictable execution
Team: Mature, experienced, cost-conscious

Success metrics:
  - Revenue growth rate: >20% YoY
  - CAC:LTV ratio: Improving
  - Market share: Increasing in core markets
  - Profitability: Improving

─────────────────────────────────────────────

HORIZON 2: EMERGING GROWTH
Focus: Adjacent products, markets, channels (validated but not yet scale)
Revenue today: 5-15% of total
Contribution to revenue in 3 years: 20-30%
Capital allocation: 30-40% of budget
Time to payoff: 18-36 months (near-term profitability possible)
Risk level: Medium (some validation, but not proven at scale)

Examples:
  - SaaS company: Enter new customer segment (SMB if enterprise)
  - DTC: Launch new product line / geographic market
  - Enterprise: Develop adjacent product for existing customers

Mindset: Growth + profitability, selective risk-taking, stage-gate discipline
Team: Mix of experienced + entrepreneurial; strong PM + operators

Success metrics:
  - Revenue: $X by Y-month gate
  - Customer validation: 10+ paying pilot customers
  - Unit economics: Clear path to CAC:LTV 1:3+
  - Operational fit: Doesn't distract core business

─────────────────────────────────────────────

HORIZON 3: EXPLORATION
Focus: New business models, markets, technologies (high-risk, high-upside)
Revenue today: <5% of total
Contribution to revenue in 3 years: 5-10%
Capital allocation: 5-10% of budget
Time to payoff: 3-5+ years (expect 50%+ failure rate)
Risk level: High (unproven business model, market, or technology)

Examples:
  - Salesforce: Entering AI/ML (early stage, not yet core to offer)
  - Amazon: Cloud computing (AWS started as internal tool, became $80B business)
  - Netflix: Streaming vs. DVDs (was "Horizon 3," now "Horizon 1")

Mindset: Experimentation, fast learning, portfolio approach (kill fast)
Team: Entrepreneurs, risk-takers, scientists; comfortable with ambiguity

Success metrics:
  - Learning: Clear hypothesis, evidence of progress
  - Customer validation: Early signs of product-market fit
  - Capability: Learning new capabilities
  - Kill decision: Clear go/no-go at each gate

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Horizon Balance

The key to sustainable growth is balance across horizons.

```
HORIZON BALANCE TARGETS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

For a healthy growth company:
  - H1 revenue today: 85-95%
  - H2 revenue today: 5-15%
  - H3 revenue today: <5%

  Capital allocation today:
  - H1 budget: 50-60%
  - H2 budget: 30-40%
  - H3 budget: 5-10%

Three years forward (healthy outcome):
  - H1 revenue: 60-70% (H1 items from 3 years ago matured)
  - H2 revenue: 20-30% (current H2 becoming H1)
  - H3 revenue: 5-10% (new H3 explorations starting)

This progression shows healthy innovation pipeline:
  - Core business is still strong (60-70% of revenue)
  - Near-term growth is working (H2 becoming bigger)
  - New bets are starting (H3 has future option value)

RED FLAG SIGNALS (indicating imbalance):
  - [ ] H1 is >98% of revenue; no H2/H3 pipeline (maturity risk)
  - [ ] H1 is stagnant but >80% of budget (starving innovation)
  - [ ] H2/H3 consuming >50% of budget but no clear winners (capital waste)
  - [ ] H1 shrinking; no H2/H3 offsets (existential risk)
  - [ ] H1 profitability declining to fund H2/H3 (unsustainable burn)

REBALANCING TRIGGER:
  Review horizon allocation quarterly
  - Are growth rates aligning with targets?
  - Should we shift capital from H1 → H2 or vice versa?
  - Are H3 explorations producing insight or just burning cash?

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Stage-Gate Process for Growth Investments

Every growth initiative must pass gates before escalating capital.

```
STAGE-GATE PROCESS (5 Gates)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GATE 0: DISCOVERY (Scoping)
Purpose: Validate the problem before investing
Duration: 1-2 weeks
Capital authorized: $0 (internal time only)
Team: Product + growth leader

Deliverable:
  - Problem statement (what's the growth constraint?)
  - Customer discovery (10+ customer interviews)
  - Market sizing (TAM estimate)
  - Competitive analysis (who else solves this?)

Gate Criteria (Must pass ALL to proceed):
  - [ ] 10+ customer interviews conducted
  - [ ] 70%+ of customers agree problem is acute
  - [ ] TAM >$50M (problem is big enough)
  - [ ] 1-2 competitors exist (market validated, but not commoditized)

Pass/Fail Decision:
  - [ ] PASS — Problem validated, proceed to Gate 1
  - [ ] FAIL — Problem not validated, stop or pivot hypothesis

Example:
  "Market entry to Germany"
  Interviews: 15 German SaaS companies
  Result: 12/15 agree product solves problem
  TAM: $200M (enterprise SaaS in Germany)
  Competitors: 2 strong players + us = 3-way market
  Decision: PASS ✓

─────────────────────────────────────────────

GATE 1: CONCEPT (Scoping + Design)
Purpose: Validate solution concept before building
Duration: 2-4 weeks
Capital authorized: $50K-$100K
Team: Product + growth leader + engineering lead

Deliverable:
  - Wireframe / mockup of solution
  - Go-to-market hypothesis (how will we reach customers?)
  - Revenue model (how will customers pay?)
  - Resource plan (how many people for 3 months?)
  - Pitch deck

Gate Criteria (Must pass):
  - [ ] Wireframe/concept resonates with 5+ customers
  - [ ] >60% of interviewed customers say "would use" or "would pay"
  - [ ] Go-to-market path is clear (not "we'll figure it out")
  - [ ] Solution can be built with existing resources (or identified hires)
  - [ ] Cost to build estimate is <3x revenue in first year

Pass/Fail Decision:
  - [ ] PASS — Concept validated, proceed to Gate 2
  - [ ] CONDITIONAL — Concept OK but needs changes before Gate 2
  - [ ] FAIL — Concept doesn't resonate, kill or pivot

Example:
  Gate 1 for "SMB segment of enterprise product"
  Wireframe: Simplified dashboard for SMB needs
  Customer feedback: 6/10 SMBs say "yes, would use"
  GTM: Use existing sales team (part-time) + self-serve trial
  Resources: 1 PM, 2 engineers for 3 months
  Decision: PASS ✓

─────────────────────────────────────────────

GATE 2: MVP (Proof of Concept)
Purpose: Build minimal product and prove customer willingness to use/pay
Duration: 6-12 weeks
Capital authorized: $200K-$500K
Team: Product, engineering, growth, customer success

Deliverable:
  - Functioning MVP with 10+ customer pilots
  - Customer feedback from pilots (adoption, satisfaction)
  - Refined go-to-market (messaging, positioning)
  - Unit economics model (estimated CAC, LTV, payback)

Gate Criteria (Must pass):
  - [ ] 10+ customers successfully using MVP
  - [ ] >50% of pilot customers rate as "would recommend" or better
  - [ ] 3+ pilot customers willing to reference you
  - [ ] You can articulate why customers use it (clear value)
  - [ ] Path to monetization clear (customers willing to pay at target price point)

Pass/Fail Decision:
  - [ ] PASS — MVP validated, scale to pilot (Gate 3)
  - [ ] CONDITIONAL — MVP works but needs product changes before scale
  - [ ] FAIL — Customers don't use it or won't pay; kill or pivot

Example:
  Gate 2 for "market entry to Germany"
  MVP: Product localized for German market, German payment methods
  Pilots: 12 German companies using product for 4 weeks
  Feedback: 10/12 rate as "good fit"; 3 willing to reference
  Unit econ: CAC ~$5K (partnership model), LTV ~$20K, payback 12 months
  Decision: CONDITIONAL PASS — Need to resolve support language before scaling

─────────────────────────────────────────────

GATE 3: PILOT (Feasibility at Scale)
Purpose: Scale to 50+ customers and validate unit economics + operational readiness
Duration: 3-6 months
Capital authorized: $500K-$2M
Team: Expanded (dedicated product, sales, CS, ops)

Deliverable:
  - 50+ paying customers
  - Validated unit economics (CAC, LTV, payback period)
  - Repeatable go-to-market process
  - Customer success playbook

Gate Criteria (Must pass):
  - [ ] 50+ customers acquired (shows scale is possible)
  - [ ] CAC:LTV ratio ≥ 1:2.5 (not yet 1:3 for new segment; lower bar)
  - [ ] >70% of customers retained to month 3 (indicates product-market fit)
  - [ ] Sales process is repeatable (same playbook works for reps)
  - [ ] Go-to-market team can operate with <20% overheads cost

Pass/Fail Decision:
  - [ ] PASS — Ready to scale investment (Gate 4)
  - [ ] CONDITIONAL — Unit econ not quite there, but could improve with iteration
  - [ ] FAIL — Unit econ won't work, kill or substantially pivot

Example:
  Gate 3 for "SMB product variant"
  Customers: 75 SMB customers in pilot
  CAC: $3K (via self-serve), LTV: $12K (1-year payback 3 months)
  Retention: 65% to month 3 (lower than enterprise 90%, but reasonable for SMB)
  Sales process: 3-step playbook works consistently
  Decision: PASS ✓

─────────────────────────────────────────────

GATE 4: SCALE (Ready to 10x Investment)
Purpose: Full scale investment with expectation of profitability
Duration: 12-24 months
Capital authorized: $2M+
Team: Full team (product, sales, CS, finance, etc.)

Deliverable:
  - 3-year business plan with revenue/profit projections
  - Detailed resource plan (hiring, budget by function)
  - Go-to-market strategy (channels, messaging, partnerships)
  - Contingency plan (what if market conditions change?)

Gate Criteria (Must pass):
  - [ ] CAC:LTV ≥ 1:3 or clear path to 1:3 within 6 months
  - [ ] Unit economics support profitability at scale
  - [ ] Market opportunity is large (>$100M TAM)
  - [ ] Competitive position is defensible
  - [ ] Execution confidence is high (team, processes, dependencies clear)

Pass/Fail Decision:
  - [ ] PASS — Full investment approved, scale aggressively
  - [ ] CONDITIONAL — Scale with caveats (monitor specific risk, throttle spend)
  - [ ] FAIL — Not ready to scale (stay at pilot size or kill)

Example:
  Gate 4 for "Germany market expansion"
  Projected Year 1 revenue: $5M (200 customers × $25K ACV)
  Path to profitability: Year 2 (high gross margin, spread fixed costs)
  Resources: Hire Sales Lead + 3 AEs, CS person, marketing
  Budget: $2M (salaries + marketing + localization)
  Decision: PASS ✓ — Allocate $2M for 12-month scale

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Kill Criteria & Portfolio Rebalancing

Not every initiative will succeed. Kill fast, learn, reallocate.

```
WHEN TO KILL A GROWTH INITIATIVE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

HARD STOPS (Kill immediately, no debate):

1. Core Hypothesis Disproven
   - Evidence: [Specific data that contradicts core assumption]
   - Example: "60%+ of customers churn within 3 months despite good onboarding"
   - Example: "Market is declining faster than expected; TAM shrinks 30% per quarter"
   - Example: "5th competitive attack from incumbent; we can't differentiate"
   - Action: Kill immediately, reallocate capital

2. Unit Economics Unrepairable
   - Evidence: [CAC too high, LTV too low, margin too compressed to ever be profitable]
   - Example: "CAC $20K, LTV $25K; even at scale, only 1:1.25 ratio"
   - Example: "Gross margin 15%; can't support operating costs"
   - Action: Kill immediately, reallocate capital

3. Team/Execution Failure
   - Evidence: [Key leader departed; team capacity exhausted; can't hire talent]
   - Example: "Sales lead left; replacement takes 6 months to ramp; pipeline stalled"
   - Example: "Engineering overcommitted; product milestones slip 6 months consecutive"
   - Action: Kill (recruit failure if you can't recover quickly)

4. Strategic Conflict
   - Evidence: [Initiative conflicts with core business; can't do both]
   - Example: "Partner channel conflicts with direct sales; customers choose partner, we lose margin"
   - Example: "New product cannibalizes existing product; net revenue negative"
   - Action: Kill the conflicting initiative or change strategy

5. Market Conditions Changed
   - Evidence: [Market dynamics fundamentally shifted; original thesis no longer holds]
   - Example: "Regulation banned our use case; market no longer viable"
   - Example: "Large incumbent entered market; we can't compete; window closed"
   - Action: Kill or pivot to adjacent market

─────────────────────────────────────────────

SOFT STOPS (Kill if 2+ conditions met):

1. Missed Gate Milestone
   - Evidence: [Initiative failed to pass gate; should kill, not extend]
   - Example: "Gate 3 was "50 customers by month 4"; only achieved 20 customers"
   - Condition: If behind >3 months, consider killing vs. extending

2. Slower Growth Than Forecast
   - Evidence: [Growth trajectory <50% of plan for 2 consecutive periods]
   - Example: "Forecast 100 customers/month; achieving 40 customers/month"
   - Condition: If underperforming by 50%+ for 6 months, reassess

3. Opportunity Cost
   - Evidence: [Better opportunity emerged; capital should be reallocated]
   - Example: "New market opportunity has 10x larger TAM; existing bet now looks small"
   - Condition: If better ROI opportunity exists, kill and reallocate

4. Team Distraction Impacting Core
   - Evidence: [Core business metrics declining because team is distracted]
   - Example: "H1 revenue growth slowing 20% due to team pulling to H2 initiative"
   - Condition: If core business is harmed, prioritize core or kill initiative

─────────────────────────────────────────────

KILL DECISION FRAMEWORK

For HARD STOPS:
  → Kill immediately. No debate. Reallocate within 1 week.

For SOFT STOPS:
  → Convene: CEO + initiative owner + affected function leads
  → Assess: Is underlying hypothesis still valid?
    If YES → Extend gate timeline, add resources, reset expectations
    If NO → Kill initiative, reallocate capital, communicate learning
  → Decision: Kill / Extend / Pivot / Hybrid

KILL COMMUNICATION PLAYBOOK
  - Acknowledge the learning (what we discovered)
  - Explain the decision (clear rationale, not blame)
  - Celebrate effort (people worked hard; not their fault market moved)
  - Announce reallocations (where capital is going)
  - Commit to applying learning (how this informs future decisions)

Example Kill Message:
  "We're stopping the Germany expansion. Here's why: After 4 months and 25
   customers, we've learned that SMB buying cycle is 6 months (vs. 3 for
   enterprise). Our 12-month payback timeline assumed 3-month cycle. To make
   this work, we'd need $500K more capital and 12-month extension. Instead,
   we're redirecting resources to the Japan opportunity (same company, different
   problem, 3-month sales cycle). Thanks for the learning."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Portfolio Rebalancing Triggers

Quarterly (or when triggered), reassess and rebalance portfolio.

```
QUARTERLY PORTFOLIO REVIEW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

STEP 1: Assess Current State
  - H1 initiatives: How many on track / at risk / off track?
  - H2 initiatives: Which passed their current gate? Which are at risk?
  - H3 initiatives: Which are learning well? Which are burning cash?
  - Overall portfolio health: [Good / Concerning / Critical]

STEP 2: Review Outcomes vs. Plan
  - Company revenue growth: [% YoY] vs. target [%]
  - Core business (H1): Growing at [%] vs. target [%]
  - Emerging (H2): Revenue at [$] vs. target [$]
  - Exploration (H3): Learning at [pace] vs. expected [pace]

STEP 3: Identify Changes Needed

If growth is below target:
  - [ ] Are we under-investing in growth? (capital allocation too low)
  - [ ] Are growth initiatives underperforming? (execution issue)
  - [ ] Is core business declining? (distracting growth efforts)
  - Action: [Increase capital allocation / improve execution / protect core]

If core business is declining:
  - [ ] Is H2/H3 distraction hurting H1? (over-diversification)
  - [ ] Are competitors attacking? (need to defend)
  - [ ] Have market conditions changed? (need to pivot)
  - Action: [Reallocate back to H1 / defend core / pivot strategy]

If H2 initiatives are stalling:
  - [ ] Do they need more resources? (under-staffed)
  - [ ] Do they need executive attention? (orphaned initiative)
  - [ ] Is product-market fit missing? (kill vs. pivot)
  - Action: [Add resources / add sponsor / kill or pivot]

If H3 is burning cash without learning:
  - [ ] Are learnings clear? (should we know something by now?)
  - [ ] Is timeline realistic? (is hypothesis testable in this timeframe?)
  - [ ] Should we kill? (better opportunity emerged)
  - Action: [Clarify learning thesis / extend or kill]

STEP 4: Rebalance Capital Allocation

Current allocation vs. recommended:
  - H1: [Current %] → [Recommended %] (delta: [+/- %])
  - H2: [Current %] → [Recommended %] (delta: [+/- %])
  - H3: [Current %] → [Recommended %] (delta: [+/- %])

Rebalancing actions:
  - [ ] Reallocate $X from H1 to H2 (core is healthy, growth needs more)
  - [ ] Reallocate $X from H3 to H1 (exploration isn't working, protect core)
  - [ ] Kill H3 initiative [Name]; reallocate $X to H2
  - [ ] Accelerate H2 [Name]; add $X for scale phase

STEP 5: Staffing & Resource Adjustments

If rebalancing required:
  - [ ] Move [# people] from [function] to [initiative]
  - [ ] Hire [# roles] for high-priority initiatives
  - [ ] Pause hiring in [function] to manage burn rate

STEP 6: Communicate Changes

Communicate to organization:
  - [ ] Overall portfolio health assessment
  - [ ] Which initiatives are accelerating, which are decelerating
  - [ ] Why we're making these changes
  - [ ] What's expected from each team

Example communication:
  "This quarter, we're adjusting our portfolio. H1 (enterprise) is ahead of plan
   (35% YoY growth), so we're reducing investment slightly. H2 (SMB) is growing
   well and has proven unit economics, so we're doubling the team from 3 to 6
   people. H3 exploration on [product] didn't validate (market interest lower
   than expected), so we're killing it and redirecting $200K to H2. The goal
   is to maintain healthy balance: 65% on H1, 30% on H2, 5% on new H3 bets."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

This is the "report card" for your growth strategy.
