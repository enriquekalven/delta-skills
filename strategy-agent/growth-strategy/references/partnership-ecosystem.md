# Partnership & Ecosystem Strategy

This reference operationalizes partnership strategy and ecosystem architecture: alliance typologies, partner scoring, ecosystem mapping, platform decisions, partnership lifecycle, and value sharing frameworks.

---

## Partnership Typology

Not all partnerships are equal. Align scope and investment to strategic value.

```
PARTNERSHIP TIERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

TIER 1: TRANSACTIONAL PARTNERSHIPS
  Purpose: Quick distribution expansion, minimal integration
  Scope: Reseller, VAR, affiliate, listing on partner marketplace
  Relationship: Arm's length, legally defined, low integration
  Economics: List price discount (partner keeps 20-30%), volume incentives
  Timeline: Weeks to months to get first customer
  Capital Required: Minimal ($0-100K partner enablement)
  Integration: None (partner uses your product as-is)
  Risk: Partner deprioritizes, channel quality control issues

  When to use: Fast distribution, proven product, low localization
  Examples: Shopify app partners, Salesforce marketplace partners, channel resellers

  Metrics:
    - Partner acquisition rate (# new partners per month)
    - Partner retention (% of partners active next period)
    - Customers per partner (should grow over time)
    - Revenue per partner

TIER 2: STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIPS
  Purpose: Mutually beneficial growth via co-marketing, integration, co-selling
  Scope: Co-sell, API integration, bundled offering, joint GTM
  Relationship: Planned, contractual, collaborative
  Economics: Revenue share (typically 10-30% to partner) or fixed fees
  Timeline: 3-6 months to first joint customers
  Capital Required: Medium ($100K-$1M: co-marketing, integration costs, shared sales)
  Integration: Product integration, shared GTM, aligned incentives
  Risk: Partner misalignment, integration delays, revenue share disputes

  When to use: Complementary products, mutual benefit, 10-50x revenue opportunity
  Examples: HubSpot + Drift (chat integration), Shopify + Stripe (payments), Slack + Salesforce

  Metrics:
    - % of revenue from partnerships
    - Average contract value via partnerships (should be higher)
    - Shared revenue growth YoY
    - Customer satisfaction from integrated experience

TIER 3: TRANSFORMATIONAL PARTNERSHIPS
  Purpose: Build network effects, new market category, co-investment
  Scope: Platform partnership, ecosystem hub, revenue-sharing marketplace
  Relationship: Deep, strategic, co-invested
  Economics: Revenue split (typically 50/50 or other split based on contribution)
  Timeline: 6-24 months to meaningful revenue (but long-term play)
  Capital Required: High ($1M-$100M+: joint R&D, co-investment, long-term commitment)
  Integration: Deep product integration, co-developed features, shared roadmap
  Risk: Partner dependency, diluted control, execution complexity

  When to use: Platform strategy, network effect opportunity, billion-dollar upside potential
  Examples: Apple + Uber (on Apple Maps), Amazon + 3rd-party sellers, Stripe + Shopify ecosystem

  Metrics:
    - GMV through marketplace (total transaction volume)
    - Number of active complementors (2nd side of marketplace)
    - Network effects strength (growth compounds as platform scales)
    - Ecosystem revenue as % of total platform revenue
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Partnership Lifecycle Management

Partnerships aren't one-time deals. They need ongoing management.

```
PARTNERSHIP LIFECYCLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PHASE 1: SOURCING & EVALUATION (1 month)
  Activities:
    - Identify partnership candidates
    - Run partnership scorecard (see above)
    - Initial conversations with prospects

  Exit Criteria:
    - [ ] Partner scorecard: 6+ (proceed) or <6 (stop)
    - [ ] Executive sponsor identified on both sides
    - [ ] Mutual interest confirmed

PHASE 2: NEGOTIATION & CONTRACTS (1-3 months)
  Activities:
    - Define partnership scope (Transactional / Strategic / Transformational)
    - Negotiate economics (margin, revenue share, territory, exclusivity)
    - Draft & execute legal agreements
    - Define success metrics

  Key Negotiation Points:
    - Territory/Exclusivity: Worldwide vs. regional? Exclusive or non-exclusive?
    - Margin: What % does partner keep? Minimum volume targets?
    - Term: 1-year? 3-year? Renewable?
    - Termination: How/when can either party exit?
    - MDF (Marketing Development Funds): Who pays for co-marketing?

  Exit Criteria:
    - [ ] Agreement signed by both parties
    - [ ] Economics agreed and documented
    - [ ] Success metrics defined (see below)
    - [ ] Kickoff meeting scheduled

PHASE 3: ENABLEMENT & LAUNCH (2-6 months)
  Activities:
    - Partner training (product, positioning, sales process)
    - Marketing asset creation (one-pagers, case studies, templates)
    - Process setup (deal registration, support escalation, order fulfillment)
    - Joint GTM planning

  Partner Enablement Plan:
    - Product training: [Topics, dates, format (virtual/in-person)]
    - Sales playbook: [What do partners say/do to sell?]
    - Marketing assets: [List of deliverables partner will use]
    - Support model: [How are partner issues resolved?]
    - Success stories: [Any existing case studies to share?]

  Exit Criteria:
    - [ ] Partner trained on product and sales process
    - [ ] Marketing assets created and approved
    - [ ] Partner has pipeline (3+ opportunities identified)
    - [ ] Support escalation process working

PHASE 4: ACTIVATION & GROWTH (6-24 months)
  Activities:
    - Partner is actively selling (closed customers coming)
    - Regular touchpoints (monthly business reviews, quarterly strategy)
    - Continuous enablement (new product training, updated marketing materials)
    - Joint optimization (what's working? What needs improvement?)

  Monthly Partner Health Check:
    - [ ] # of new opportunities in pipeline: [#]
    - [ ] # of deals closed this month: [#]
    - [ ] Revenue: [$]
    - [ ] Partner satisfaction (1-10): [ ]
    - [ ] Issues/blockers: [List]
    - [ ] Actions for next month: [List]

  Quarterly Business Reviews (QBRs):
    - Review performance vs. targets
    - Discuss pipeline
    - Identify gaps (training, tools, marketing)
    - Plan next quarter

  Exit Criteria:
    - [ ] Partner meeting revenue targets (or clear path)
    - [ ] Customer satisfaction from partnership is positive
    - [ ] Partner retention intent confirmed (willing to continue)

PHASE 5: SCALE OR OPTIMIZE (12+ months)
  Decision Point: Is partnership working?

  If YES:
    - [ ] Scale investment (more training, larger co-marketing)
    - [ ] Add more partners in same channel
    - [ ] Evolve to Tier 2 or 3 partnership (if applicable)

  If NO:
    - [ ] Identify what's broken (partner quality, product fit, economics)
    - [ ] Optimize (different messaging? Better training? Adjust margin?)
    - [ ] Kill (if 6+ months in and not working, end gracefully)

PHASE 6: RENEWAL OR EXIT (3-year term, approaching end)
  Activities:
    - Evaluate: Should we renew?
    - Renegotiate: Has market changed? Should economics change?
    - Celebrate: Recognize partner contributions
    - Plan: What's next? (expand, evolve, or end)

  Renewal Decision:
    - Is partner still strategic? [Yes/No]
    - Are they meeting targets? [Yes/No]
    - Has market/strategy changed? [Yes/No]
    - Recommendation: [Renew / Renew with changes / End]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Value Sharing Framework

How do you share value fairly with partners? This is critical for partnership sustainability.

```
PARTNER VALUE SHARING MODELS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

MODEL 1: TRANSACTIONAL (List Price Discount)
  Partner keeps: 20-30% discount off list price
  You keep: 70-80%

  Example:
    - Your SaaS: $10K ACV
    - Partner discount: 25%
    - Partner gets: $2.5K per customer
    - You get: $7.5K per customer

  Good for: Fast distribution, partners need simple economics
  Bad for: Reduces your margin, doesn't incentivize quality

MODEL 2: REVENUE SHARE (Percentage of Revenue)
  Partner keeps: 15-30% of customer revenue
  You keep: 70-85%

  Example:
    - Customer pays: $10K/year
    - Partner gets: 20% × $10K = $2K/year (ongoing)
    - You get: 80% × $10K = $8K/year

  Good for: Ongoing partnership, partners stay engaged
  Bad for: High processing cost, complex accounting

MODEL 3: HYBRID (Discount + Upsell Opportunity)
  Partner keeps: 25% of first deal
  Partner keeps: 10% of expansion revenue (upsell/cross-sell)
  You keep: 75% first deal + 90% expansion

  Example:
    - Year 1: Partner closes customer @ $10K → Partner gets $2.5K
    - Year 2: Customer expands to $15K → Partner gets $500 (10% of $5K expansion)
    - You get: $7.5K first deal + $4.5K expansion

  Good for: Incentivizes both acquisition AND expansion
  Bad for: Complex accounting, requires trust on expansion revenue

MODEL 4: FIXED FEE (SLA-Based)
  Partner gets: Fixed $ per customer or per deal
  You keep: Remainder

  Example:
    - Partner gets: $2K per customer acquired (fixed, regardless of customer size)
    - You get: Remaining revenue
    - Works for: Early customers (small ACV), predictable cost

  Good for: Simple accounting, predictable cost
  Bad for: Doesn't incentivize quality (big customer = same reward as small)

MODEL 5: TIERED (Performance-Based)
  Partner gets: Higher % as they hit volume targets

  Example:
    - 0-10 customers/quarter: 20% margin
    - 11-20 customers/quarter: 25% margin
    - 21+ customers/quarter: 30% margin

  Good for: Incentivizes scale, rewards high performers
  Bad for: Complex to manage, can create perverse incentives

─────────────────────────────────────────────

HOW TO CHOOSE MODEL:

Questions to Answer:
  1. Is customer acquisition or expansion your priority?
     [Acquisition → Revenue share per deal]
     [Expansion → Revenue share ongoing + percent of expansion]

  2. How much do you trust the partner's accounting?
     [High trust → Revenue share (ongoing)]
     [Low trust → Fixed fee (simple, predictable)]

  3. How important is this partnership?
     [Tier 1 (transactional) → Simple (discount or fixed fee)]
     [Tier 2 (strategic) → Revenue share ongoing]
     [Tier 3 (transformational) → Equity / co-investment]

  4. What margins can you afford?
     [60%+ gross margin → Can afford 20-30% partner take]
     [40-60% gross margin → Should limit to 15-20%]
     [<40% gross margin → Too tight for meaningful partner share]

─────────────────────────────────────────────

VALUE SHARING FAIRNESS CHECKLIST:

For Partner:
  - [ ] Margin is meaningful (at least $5K-$10K per year for effort)
  - [ ] Partner can earn more by exceeding targets (incentive alignment)
  - [ ] Payment is timely and accurate (trust in you)
  - [ ] You're transparent about customer revenue (partner sees they're valued)

For You:
  - [ ] Margin is sustainable (partner's take doesn't make you unprofitable)
  - [ ] You can afford to support the partnership (not all margin goes to partner)
  - [ ] Incentives align (partner wants you to succeed, not just make quick sales)
  - [ ] Economics work if partnership scales 10x

Bad Value Sharing (Creates Friction):
  - ❌ Partner margin is <$1K/customer (not worth their effort)
  - ❌ You're unprofitable after partner margin (unsustainable)
  - ❌ Incentives misaligned (partner wants big deals, you want many small deals)
  - ❌ Partner earns more by steering customer to competitor (conflicts of interest)
  - ❌ No transparency on customer revenue (partner doesn't trust you)

```
