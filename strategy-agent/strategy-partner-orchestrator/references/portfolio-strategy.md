# Portfolio Strategy (10/10 Complete Framework)

## What Changed from the Original BCG Matrix
The original BCG Matrix (1970s) assumes relative market share equals competitive advantage and market growth equals attractiveness—both wrong for modern businesses. This version replaces the rigid 2x2 with multi-criteria assessment, adds synergy and risk correlation analysis, incorporates growth gap analysis, values strategic options, stress-tests against scenarios, diagnoses sacred cows, links to M&A strategy, and embeds rigorous capital allocation with explicit hurdle rates.

## When to Use
Annual strategic portfolio review with board. Post-M&A rationalization. When portfolio feels unbalanced. When you need to decide: hold, divest, acquire, or partner to close growth gaps. When sacred cows are consuming capital despite weak performance.

## Step 1: Multi-Criteria Mapping

**Market Attractiveness (Y-axis):**
- Market size, growth rate, and growth stability
- Margin structure and profit pool per competitor
- Competitive intensity and threat of disruption
- Regulatory/tech tailwinds or headwinds
- Customer switching costs and stickiness

**Competitive Position (X-axis):**
- Market share and share trend (gaining/flat/losing)
- Revenue growth rate vs. market growth
- Profitability vs. competitors
- Customer loyalty/NPS/retention
- Sustainable capability advantage (tech, brand, distribution, data)

Rate 1–5 with explicit evidence. Size bubbles by revenue or contribution margin.

## Step 2: Synergy & Risk Correlation Matrix

**Synergy Assessment (value creation between units):**
- Revenue synergies: shared sales channels, cross-selling, brand halo?
- Cost synergies: shared infrastructure, procurement leverage, manufacturing?
- Capability sharing: data, tech, talent, customer insights?
- Ecosystem value: do units strengthen each other's competitive positions?

Map each pair. Flag destroyers (units that cannibalize or require separate channels).

**Portfolio Risk Correlation:**
- Are all units exposed to same macro risk (interest rates, commodity prices, regulatory change)?
- Geographic concentration?
- Customer/supplier concentration?
- Technology obsolescence risk clustering?

Units with low or negative correlation reduce overall portfolio volatility. High correlation = concentration risk.

## Step 3: Growth Gap Analysis

Formula: `Gap = Target Revenue − (Current Portfolio @ Trend Growth)`

Example: Target $500M in 5 years. Current portfolio (at 3% trend growth) reaches $350M. **Gap = $150M.** Now diagnose how to fill it:

- **Accelerate existing units** (invest in high-margin units to 8% growth)
- **Fix underperformers** (can unit in "Invest to Strengthen" reach 6% if resourced?)
- **Acquire or partner** (external revenue)
- **New business development** (internal ventures)

For each option: calculate IRR, payback, and capital requirement. Rank by capital efficiency and strategic fit.

## Step 4: Strategic Option Valuation

Early-stage or speculative units don't fit NPV. Use real options:
- **Expansion option**: What's the value if this scales? (probability × upside)
- **Platform option**: Does this unlock other markets?
- **Technology option**: Does this create competitive moat for core business?
- **Insurance option**: Does this hedge a threat to core business?

Assign annual cost to maintain (R&D, minimal marketing). Accept losses if option value > annual cost. Kill if neither option value nor near-term profitability justifies it.

## Step 5: Scenario Stress Testing

Overlay macro scenarios on portfolio:
- Recession (demand -20%, price pressure, competitor exit/consolidation)
- Margin compression (rising input costs, wage pressure)
- Disruption (tech shift, new entrant, channel shift)
- Regulatory change (new compliance, tax, antitrust)

For each scenario: Which portfolio units suffer most? Where is correlation dangerous? What portfolio moves reduce downside?

## Step 6: Sacred Cow Diagnosis & Debiasing

Identify units that receive disproportionate protection despite weak metrics:
- Founder attachment, legacy pride, political power of sponsor
- Sunk cost fallacy ("we invested so much")
- "We'll turn it around" syndrome (repeated, unmet turnaround plans)

Debiasing techniques:
- **Explicit shelf life**: "This unit has 18 months to hit [specific metric]. If not, we exit."
- **Brutal honesty check**: "If we didn't own this, would we acquire it at today's valuation?"
- **Sponsor rotation**: Move unit under different leader to break emotional attachment
- **Red team**: Assign someone to argue for exit; force counterargument in writing
- **External benchmark**: How do peer companies treat similar units?

## Step 7: M&A Implications (Build/Buy/Partner)

For each growth gap and portfolio weakness:

**Build:** Develop internally
- Pros: Control, culture fit, capability building
- Cons: Slow, capital-intensive, execution risk, talent bottleneck
- Use when: Capability is rare/proprietary, internal strength is unique

**Buy:** Acquire existing business
- Pros: Immediate revenue/capability, market position, reduces competitor
- Cons: Integration risk, overpayment, cultural clash, distraction
- Use when: Time-to-market is critical, target is reasonably priced, synergies are clear

**Partner:** Joint venture, licensing, distribution deal
- Pros: Low capital, fast, shared risk
- Cons: Control loss, limited upside, strategic leakage
- Use when: You lack capability or capital, or market is too small to own

Create explicit acquisition criteria (size, margin, growth, capex intensity, synergy potential). Avoid "we'll buy anything in this space" without criteria.

## Step 8: Lifecycle Management (S-Curve)

Map each unit on the S-curve (launch → growth → mature → decline):
- **Early S-curve**: Invest to accelerate adoption
- **Peak S-curve**: Harvest cash, defend share
- **Declining S-curve**: Milk cash, prepare exit or pivot

Common mistake: Not starting next-generation products before current gen declines. Plan succession 3-5 years in advance.

## Step 9: Capital Allocation Rigor

Don't say "invest here." Be specific:

```
CAPITAL ALLOCATION FRAMEWORK:

Base Case (% of FCF):
  Invest to Grow: [Unit name] — [% FCF] — Hurdle rate [IRR%] — ROI horizon [years]
  Invest to Strengthen: [Unit name] — [% FCF] — Hurdle rate [IRR%] — Decision gate [date]
  Harvest: [Unit name] — Minimal invest, maximize cash extraction
  Maintain/Exit: [Unit name] — [% FCF] — Divestiture plan and timeline

Required Returns:
  Core business / Invest to Grow: [15%+ IRR preferred]
  Invest to Strengthen: [20%+ IRR to justify risk]
  Strategic Options: [Positive option value vs. cost]

Opportunity Cost: [Alternative uses: debt paydown, shareholder return, M&A]
```

## Step 10: Worked Example

**Company: TechCorp ($800M revenue)**

Portfolio:
1. **Cloud Platform** (45% revenue, 25% growth, strong position) — Invest to Grow. Allocate 40% FCF.
2. **Legacy Software** (35% revenue, 2% growth, strong position, margins 40%) — Harvest. Allocate 10% FCF. Defend, don't expand.
3. **AI Services** (10% revenue, 60% growth, weak position, early adoption risk) — Invest to Strengthen. Allocate 30% FCF. Decision gate in 18 months: hit $30M revenue + path to profitability or exit.
4. **Professional Services** (10% revenue, 3% growth, weak position, requires bespoke delivery) — Fix or Exit. Evaluate for sale to niche player. Allocate 0% FCF.

**Growth Gap:** Target $1.2B in 3 years. Trend portfolio = $920M. **Gap = $280M.**

Fill via: (1) Cloud acceleration to 40% growth = +$100M; (2) AI scaling to $50M revenue = +$50M; (3) Acquire adjacent platform (+$100M revenue, 20% margin) = +$100M. Total = $250M. Remaining $30M from organic productivity.

**Synergies:** Cloud + AI create product bundle (upsell, lower CAC). Cloud + new acquisition share infrastructure (20% cost synergy on acquired unit). Professional Services drain management attention with no synergy—exit justified.

**Risk Correlation:** Cloud and AI both exposed to macro tech cycle. Acquire platform company with countercyclical revenue (e.g., enterprise optimization in downturns) to de-risk.

**Sacred Cow Diagnosis:** Professional Services has sponsor (Head of Sales, whose org built it). Reframe: "We're divesting to a best-in-class partner who will grow this better than we can." Appoint third-party advisor to lead sale. Make Head of Sales sponsor of integration/transition win.
