# Scenario Planning (10/10 Framework)

## What Changed
The original was a competent 2x2 template but stopped at awareness. This framework goes further: it teaches you to distinguish real uncertainties from researched knowables, tests scenarios for consistency, models financial impact per scenario, values strategic options using real options thinking, stress-tests your existing portfolio, and creates an update protocol so this isn't a one-time exercise. Most critically: scenarios drive decisions, not just conversation.

## When to Use
Use when facing a 2-5 year decision under genuine uncertainty and you have 2+ defensible views on how the world evolves. Scenario planning is preparedness, not prophecy—most valuable when smart people disagree.

Don't use if: (1) the future is researchable to high confidence (scenario planning adds noise), (2) decision horizon is <12 months (use Hypothesis Engine), or (3) you're trying to forecast rather than prepare.

## Step 1: Separate Uncertainty from Knowable

**This is where most scenario planning fails.** Teams mistake "I haven't researched this" for "this is genuinely uncertain."

### Filter Test: Is this really uncertain?

For each proposed uncertainty, ask:
- Can we research this to 80%+ confidence in 2-4 weeks? If yes, research it. Don't scenario-plan ignorance.
- Is there structural disagreement among domain experts? If no, there's a consensus answer you're missing.
- Does this have measurable leading indicators now? If yes, we already have information—use it.

**Example of collapse:** "Will AI adoption accelerate?" Research shows enterprise AI adoption is already 60%+ in financial services, lagging in manufacturing. Not uncertain—unevenly distributed. Real uncertainty: "Will regulatory frameworks slow AI deployment in healthcare?" That's genuinely contested by smart people.

### Genuine uncertainties have two characteristics:
1. **Structural disagreement** — Reasonable professionals hold opposite views backed by evidence
2. **Impactful divergence** — The two endpoints create fundamentally different business scenarios

Macro shifts (recession yes/no), technology inflection points (will this tech mature in 2 years or 10?), regulatory pivots (tighten vs. loosen), and competitive disruption (will a new player upend the game?) are usually legitimate.

Rank all uncertainties on a 2x2 grid: **Impact on Strategy** (vertical) × **Legitimate Disagreement** (horizontal). Work with the top 2-3.

## Step 2: Build Coherent Scenarios

### Matrix (2 uncertainties) or Narrative (3+)

**For 2 clear uncertainties:** Use a 2x2 grid.
**For 3+ or multi-causal:** Build 3-5 narrative scenarios (Base, Upside, Downside, Disruption, optional Wildcard).

### Scenario Coherence Test — Hard Rules

Each scenario must pass all four:

1. **Causal consistency** — Does Scenario A's narrative explain itself? If "high adoption + high regulation," show why both occur together. (Tighter regulation often slows adoption, so explain the mechanism. Example: "Regulators accelerate approval pathways to compete with China.")

2. **Plausibility check** — Has any precedent existed for this combo in history or close analogs? If "zero regulation + explosive adoption," find a comparable market that did this. If none exist, the scenario may be fantasy.

3. **Feedback loop test** — Does the scenario stabilize or collapse? If "high prices + low demand," prices should fall—creating feedback. Describe the equilibrium.

4. **Expert pressure test** — Could a domain expert defend this scenario to a skeptical audience? If the narrative is hand-wavy, it's not coherent.

### Probability Weight (Honest Assessment)

Assign probabilities. If you get 25%/25%/25%/25%, you're facing maximum uncertainty—which is rare and worth stating explicitly. More often: 40%/35%/20%/5%. If all scenarios cluster above 35%, you don't have enough genuine uncertainty—collapse to fewer scenarios.

**Pitfall check:** Are your scenarios "symmetric"—evenly split between "everything good" and "everything bad"? Real futures are usually lumpy. You might have 50% base case + 30% slow growth + 15% disruption + 5% black swan.

## Step 3: Model Financial Impact Per Scenario

Scenarios are abstract unless you model the money. For each scenario, estimate ranges:

- **Revenue impact** — Market size × adoption rate × share → 3-year revenue trajectory
- **Margin pressure** — Competition, input costs, pricing power in this scenario → gross/EBITDA margin
- **Cash flow** — Revenue - OpEx - CapEx (capex often varies wildly across scenarios)
- **Valuation multiple** — Growth rate + risk profile in this scenario → likely EV/EBITDA or EV/Sales

**Concrete template:**
```
Scenario: High Adoption
  Year 1-3 revenue range: $50-70M (base: $45M, upside from market expansion)
  Gross margin: 68% (vs. 65% now—scale efficiency)
  EBITDA margin: 22% (vs. 18% now—R&D scales slower than revenue)
  3-year FCF: $40-55M
  Implied valuation multiple: 4.5-5.5x revenue (higher growth justifies premium)
```

This makes scenarios testable. Scenario disagreement becomes "I think adoption margins compress" vs. "I think scale enables margin expansion"—a specific claim you can validate.

## Step 4: Value Strategic Options (Real Options Framework)

Don't just list "big bets"—value them. For each strategic move, estimate:

**Option value = Payoff in upside scenario - Cost of preserving option - Payoff in downside**

Example:
- **Move:** Acquire AI capability (small startup team, $3M/year)
- **Upside scenario** (AI adoption accelerates): This capability becomes core—avoids $50M+ acquisition later. Payoff: $40M
- **Cost:** $3M/year × 3 years = $9M
- **Downside scenario** (AI adoption stalls): Capability unused, drag on P&L. Cost: -$2M earnings impact
- **Option value:** $40M - $9M - (-$2M) = $33M

This separates genuine options (high payoff, low cost) from pet projects (speculative, expensive).

**Real options classification:**
- **Wait-and-see options** — Small spend to gather information before big commitment
- **Staged bets** — Initial investment, scale gate (like VC funding rounds)
- **Abandonment options** — Ability to exit early if scenario doesn't materialize
- **Growth options** — Small beachhead that scales if upside scenario emerges

## Step 5: Portfolio Stress Testing

Overlay all scenarios on your **current strategy + portfolio**. For each scenario, assess:

- **Revenue exposure** — Which business units / geographies / products get hit or boosted?
- **Capability gap** — What do you lack to win in this scenario?
- **Competitive position** — Who else might thrive here? Where do you become vulnerable?
- **Cash impact** — Total 3-year cash swing across all scenarios

**Portfolio vulnerability matrix:**

```
                Scenario A    Scenario B    Scenario C    Scenario D
Product 1       -30% revenue  +20% revenue  -5% revenue   Irrelevant
Product 2       +5%           +25%          -15%          Dead
Geography 1     Safe          Safe          -40%          Collapse
Geography 2     Fragile       Strong        Strong        Boom
```

This reveals: Geography 2 is a hedge (performs well in 3/4 scenarios); Product 2 is a big bet (wins big in 1, loses in another); Geography 1 needs insurance.

## Step 6: Scenario Update Protocol

Scenario planning is **not** a one-time exercise. Schedule quarterly updates:

1. **New data review** — What leading indicators have moved? (Customer surveys, competitive moves, regulatory signals, tech adoption rates)

2. **Probability reset** — Did signal movement change scenario likelihood? If Scenario A was 35% and three strong indicators now favor it, nudge to 40%. Document why.

3. **Narrative test** — Are details in the scenario playing out? If "Scenario B assumes high regulation," and regulatory signals are weak, adjust or demote the scenario.

4. **Decision point triggers** — Have you hit any decision triggers yet? If "trigger activates when 60% of enterprise customers adopt X," and actual adoption is at 40%, you're not yet triggered—but watch next quarter.

5. **Options revaluation** — Have costs or payoffs of your strategic options changed? Reprice them.

**Update cadence:** Quarterly review (30 min) + annual deep rebuild (2 days) when major assumptions shift.

## Step 7: Identify Leading Indicators & Triggers

For each scenario, list 5 concrete signals. **Avoid vague metrics.**

**Bad:** "Customer adoption increases"
**Good:** "Quarterly net new customer additions exceed 50 (we're at 30 now; threshold is 67th percentile of market leaders)"

**Template:**
```
Scenario: High Adoption
  Signal 1: Enterprise NPS > 55 (currently 48) — Source: Quarterly NPS survey
  Signal 2: Win rate vs. Competitor X > 45% (currently 32%) — Source: Sales pipeline analysis
  Signal 3: Price elasticity shifts positive (customers accept 10% price increase) — Source: Pricing experiments
  Signal 4: Regulatory approval timelines compress to <6 months (currently 12-18) — Source: FDA/EMA guidance updates
  Signal 5: VC funding to competing startups drops >30% YoY — Source: PitchBook / Crunchbase

  Activation threshold: 3 of 5 signals hit strongly within same quarter
```

## Step 8: Common Pitfalls (With Examples)

**1. Anchoring to base case** — You list scenarios but secretly believe "probably base case anyway." Mitigation: Assign hard probabilities. If base case is >50%, you're not scenario planning—you're forecasting. Reset.

**2. Symmetric scenarios** — (25%, 25%, 25%, 25%) or (Good, Bad, Neutral, Wildcard). Real distributions are skewed. Mitigation: Rank on impact × legitimacy. Stop forcing balance.

**3. Fantasy upside** — Scenario A: "Market doubles, competitors disappear, we capture 40% share." Pitfall: This isn't a scenario, it's a wish. Mitigation: Coherence test. If no historical precedent or causal mechanism, demote it.

**4. Scenarios vs. forecasts** — Using scenarios to pick "the one true future" defeats the purpose. Mitigation: Commit to no-regret moves and decision triggers, not scenario betting.

**5. Ignoring black swans** — Scenario planning is about known uncertainties, not unknown unknowns. (By definition, you can't plan unknowns.) But: reserve 5% probability for "wildcard"—something you haven't modeled. This acknowledges epistemic humility.

## Step 9: Black Swan vs. Fat Tail (Conceptual Clarity)

- **Fat tail:** Extreme outcome within a known distribution. Example: "Inflation hits 10% in Scenario C"—unlikely but causal, within the frame.
- **Black swan:** Outside your scenario frame entirely. Example: "Pandemic collapses travel demand"—not in your 2024 scenarios because unthinkable. But once it happens, it's clearly a tail event, not a black swan anymore.

**Operationally:** Build scenarios around fat tails (researchable extremes). Flag 5% wildcard for unknown unknowns. Annual review: what happened this year that wasn't in our scenarios? Add it next year.

## Step 10: From Scenarios to Decisions

**This is the payoff.** Sort every strategic move by robustness:

**No-Regret Moves** — Create value across all scenarios (do now, fund fully):
- Moves like "improve customer retention" or "upgrade core platform" work everywhere.
- These are your bulk allocation.

**Hedges** — Reduce downside in bad scenarios, cost modest premium:
- Example: "Build supplier redundancy" protects against supply shock scenarios.
- Cost is 5-10% premium; payoff is 30-50% downside protection.

**Big Bets** — Win huge in one scenario, lose in others (fund only if >40% probability scenario):
- Example: "Launch premium tier" wins if Scenario A (high-end expansion) hits; underperforms if Scenario B (price compression) hits.
- Only take if Scenario A > 40% and total firm can absorb Scenario B downside.

**Staged / Conditional Bets** — Small initial spend, scale gates tied to scenario signals:
- Example: "Pilot in-house manufacturing" ($2M year 1), scale to $20M capex only if supply disruption signals hit.
- Preserves optionality without full commitment.
