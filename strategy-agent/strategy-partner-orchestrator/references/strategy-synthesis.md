# Full Strategy Synthesis (10/10 Version)

## What Changed
Added eight missing rigor layers: weighted decision matrix (structured option scoring), stakeholder impact analysis (political feasibility), change readiness assessment (execution capability), resource-loaded implementation plan, governance framework with decision rules, decision-under-uncertainty protocol, communication cascade by audience, and strategy failure pre-mortem. Plus competitive response mapping and coherence testing. Includes worked example template.

## When to Use
Use at the end of multi-framework engagement to convert analysis into decision. This separates synthesis thinking from communication packaging. Not a summary — a higher-order argument where insights only emerge from seeing all evidence together.

## Step 1: Evidence Assembly

**Strategy Context Review:**
- All key findings with source framework and confidence (H/M/L)
- All hypotheses with status (confirmed/challenged/untested)
- Open questions and unresolved gaps
- Core assumptions made

**Convergence Analysis:**
- Where do 2+ frameworks point to same conclusion? (High confidence)
- Where do frameworks contradict? (Which evidence is stronger? Resolve it.)
- What pattern emerges across frameworks that no single one surfaced? (Synthesis insight)

## Step 2: Core Strategic Question

Restate the single question the engagement answers. If it evolved:
- Original question: [What was asked]
- Refined question: [What analysis revealed as the real question]

Everything connects to this question. Cut anything that doesn't.

## Step 3: Strategic Options (2-4 Distinct Approaches)

**Option [X]: [Descriptive Name]**
- **What:** 2-3 sentence description
- **Strategic logic:** Why this works — advantage or opportunity
- **Expected outcome:** Specific metrics if possible
- **Key requirements:** What must be true or built
- **Timeline:** When returns emerge
- **Investment:** Money, time, organizational effort
- **Primary risk:** Biggest failure mode
- **Evidence strength:** H/M/L

**Options Quality Check:**
- Genuinely distinct? (Merging loses something?)
- Dominated option exists? (Drop it — wastes attention)
- "Do nothing" included? (Should be, to show insufficiency)

## Step 4: Weighted Decision Matrix

Score each option (1-10 scale) against criteria with explicit weights. This replaces narrative vagueness with numbers.

| Criterion | Weight | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|-----------|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| Alignment to strategy | 25% | 9 | 7 | 5 |
| Market speed advantage | 20% | 8 | 9 | 6 |
| Implementation capability | 20% | 7 | 6 | 9 |
| Investment required (inverted) | 15% | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| Risk-adjusted return | 20% | 8 | 7 | 5 |
| **Weighted Score** | **100%** | **7.75** | **7.45** | **6.35** |

Include sensitivity analysis: "If [criterion] weight changes by ±10%, does recommendation still hold?"

## Step 5: Stakeholder Impact & Political Feasibility

**Who wins/loses under each option:**

| Stakeholder | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|-------------|----------|----------|----------|
| Sales team | +++ | ++ | - |
| IT department | - | +++ | ++ |
| CFO | ++ | - | +++ |
| Board | +++ | ++ | + |
| Customers | ++ | +++ | + |

**Political feasibility by option:**
- Blocking coalitions? (Who could kill this?)
- Coalition-building needed? (Who must be convinced?)
- Sequencing strategy? (Build support for objectors early)

## Step 6: Change Readiness Assessment

Can the organization actually execute this?

**Capability Gaps:**
- Skills/talent missing: [Gap] → Hire, train, or hire firm? Timeline? Cost?
- Systems/tools missing: [Gap] → Build, buy, or integrate? Timeline? Cost?
- Process/governance missing: [Gap] → Design, implement, embed. Timeline? Cost?

**Cultural Barriers:**
- Belief system conflict: [Barrier] → How to shift mindset?
- Incentive misalignment: [Barrier] → What compensation/KPI changes?
- Risk aversion/past failures: [Barrier] → Proof point strategy?

**Execution Confidence:** H/M/L — what must happen first to increase confidence?

## Step 7: Resource-Loaded Implementation Plan

Not just timeline — actual resource commitment.

**Phase 1 (Months 1-2): [Initiative Name]**
- Workstream A: [Task] — FTE: [#] — Budget: $[X] — Owner: [Role]
- Workstream B: [Task] — FTE: [#] — Budget: $[X] — Owner: [Role]
- Dependencies: [What must happen first?]
- Key decision: [What approval is needed by Day 30?]

**Phase 2 (Months 3-6):** [Initiative] — Total FTE: [#] — Budget: $[X]

**Cumulative investment through 12 months:** [Total FTE-months] = $[X] people + $[X] external spend

**Resource constraints:**
- Do we have available capacity? (Or are we trading off other work?)
- Market rate for needed talent: [Cost/scarcity indicators]
- Contingency for scope creep: [+20%?]

## Step 8: Governance Framework for Execution

**Decision Rights (who decides what?):**
- Strategy refresh trigger: [Quarterly re-evaluation condition, e.g., "if NPS drops below X"]
- Budget reallocation authority: [Who approves mid-year budget moves?]
- Scope/timeline changes: [When does the team escalate vs. absorb variance?]

**Review Cadence:**
- Weekly ops standup: Track resource utilization and blockers
- Monthly steering: Review against plan, surface emerging risks
- Quarterly strategy: Assess progress vs. success factors, course correction decision

**Escalation Paths:**
- Escalation trigger 1: [Condition] → Escalate to [Role] within [Timeframe]
- Escalation trigger 2: [Condition] → Escalate to [Role] within [Timeframe]

**Course Correction Protocol:**
- If we're >15% behind plan: Pause, diagnose, modify (don't just push harder)
- If evidence contradicts assumptions: Trigger strategy refresh
- If market conditions shift materially: Reassess vs. original thesis

## Step 9: Decision Under Uncertainty Protocol

When evidence is 55/45, not 80/20:

**For HIGH-stakes, REVERSIBLE decisions:**
- Decide with current evidence if confidence is M/H
- Set decision review gate at [Timeframe] to reassess

**For HIGH-stakes, IRREVERSIBLE decisions:**
- Run small pilot/proof-of-concept first if confidence is M
- Do not proceed if confidence is L without more data
- Identify what data would increase confidence to H

**For LOW-stakes decisions:**
- Decide and move; adjust based on learning

**Example:** "We're 60% confident in the market size. We'll proceed with Phase 1 because it's reversible. If Q2 customer acquisition costs are >$[X], we'll pivot to Option B."

## Step 10: Communication Cascade Plan

Different messages for different audiences — not a single "talking points" doc.

**Board/Investors:**
- Lead with: Business case (growth, ROI, competitive position)
- Frame: Risk-adjusted opportunity, staged approach, governance in place

**Executive Team:**
- Lead with: Who owns what, resource commitment, success metrics
- Frame: Alignment with other initiatives, interdependencies

**Middle Management:**
- Lead with: What changes for them, what stays same, why we're doing this
- Frame: Clear role in execution, success factors in their function

**Front-line (team doing work):**
- Lead with: What's in it for them (growth, learning, stability)
- Frame: How their daily work contributes, where to escalate

**Customers/External:**
- Lead with: Value to them, timeline, transition plan
- Frame: Continuity or positive disruption depending on strategy

**Tone/frequency:** Board (quarterly deep); Exec (bi-weekly ops); Middle mgmt (monthly all-hands); Front-line (weekly standup)

## Step 11: Strategy Failure Pre-Mortem

Assume strategy failed in 24 months. What went wrong? Address each failure mode.

**Failure modes to surface:**
- "Execution stalled at [phase] because [capability gap surfaced]" → What's the backup plan?
- "Competitors responded faster than expected" → How do we maintain differentiation?
- "Key person left mid-execution" → Succession plan for critical roles?
- "Market shifted and our assumptions broke" → What's the trigger to pivot?
- "Org culture resisted the change" → Were we honest about change readiness?
- "We solved the technical problem but missed the organizational one" → Is governance robust enough?

For each: "To mitigate, we will [specific preventive action]."

## Step 12: Competitive Response Anticipation

Your move signals to competitors. Model what happens next.

**Competitor A likely response:** [Based on their strategy, capabilities, constraints]
- **Your counter-move:** [How do you maintain advantage?]

**Competitor B likely response:** [...]
- **Your counter-move:** [...]

**Market dynamic this creates:** [What second/third-order effects emerge?]

**Window of opportunity:** [How long until competitors catch up? Adjust timeline if window is closing.]

## Step 13: Strategy Coherence Test

Do all elements reinforce each other, or are there contradictions?

**Element A [e.g., pricing strategy] reinforces:**
- Element B [e.g., premium positioning] ✓
- Element C [e.g., target customer segment] ✓

**Element D [e.g., aggressive cost-cutting] conflicts with:**
- Element A [premium positioning intended] ✗ — Resolve: [Why contradiction exists or what to change]

**Coherence score:** All elements reinforce (9-10/10) vs. multiple internal contradictions (3-5/10)?

## Step 14: The Recommendation

Choose one option. "Three options and it depends" is a failure.

**Direct statement:**
"We recommend [Option X] because [1-2 sentence rationale]."

**Case For (pyramid structure):**
- Governing thought: [Main argument]
  - Supporting argument 1: [Evidence]
  - Supporting argument 2: [Evidence]
  - Supporting argument 3: [Evidence]

**Case Against (strongest objections):**
- Objection 1: [Statement] → Response: [Why it doesn't defeat recommendation]
- Objection 2: [Statement] → Mitigation: [How we reduce impact]

**Why not the others:** One sentence per rejected option.

**Decision confidence:** H/M/L — What additional information would increase it?

## Worked Example: Tech Platform Expansion

**Question:** Should we build or acquire our next-gen AI capability?

**Options evaluated:** (1) Build internal | (2) Acquire fast-growing startup | (3) Strategic partnership | (4) Do nothing / outsource only

**Decision matrix weights:** Strategic fit (25%), Time-to-market (20%), Integration risk (20%), Cost (20%), Team capability (15%)
- Weighted scores: Build (7.1) | Acquire (7.8) | Partnership (6.4) | Do nothing (5.2)

**Stakeholder impact:** Sales team wants fast feature availability (favors Acquire). R&D wants to own technology roadmap (favors Build). CFO concerned about acquisition integration risk and cost. Board wants defensible moat within 18 months.

**Change readiness:** Organization has never integrated an acquisition. Need dedicated integration lead (+$200k), change management (6 months, embedded), knowledge transfer process (new). Capability gap: M&A due diligence expertise (hire firm, $100-150k).

**Implementation (Acquire path):**
- Month 1: Finalize deal, secure team retention, communication plan. Budget: $50k (legal/tax). Owner: CEO
- Months 2-4: Integrate product roadmap, align GTM, migrate customers. Budget: $200k + 8 FTE. Owner: VP Product
- Months 5-6: Unified go-to-market, cross-sell. Budget: $150k + 4 FTE. Owner: Head of Sales

**Governance:** Monthly steering committee (CEO, CFO, VP Product, Acquisition lead). If integration slips >2 weeks, escalate to board. Customer churn >3% triggers communication refresh.

**Failure pre-mortem:** Acquired team leaves → retention bonuses + clear 18-month roadmap + quarterly equity grants. Customers churn → dedicated retention manager + special pricing. Integration takes 12 months (not 6) → Have contingency resource pool identified.

**Competitive response:** Competitor likely acquires similar startup within 6 months. Our counter: accelerate customer wins in months 1-3 while we still have feature advantage.

**Coherence:** Growth ambition (acquire for speed) aligns with customer feedback (wants integrated solution), aligns with board timeline (18-month defensible position), aligns with team capability gap (need outside expertise we can hire from acquired company).

**Recommendation:** Acquire [Company X]. They have the talent, customers, and technology we need, arriving 12 months faster than building. Integration risk is real but manageable with a dedicated lead and retention plan. We're prepared to invest $500k and 18 FTE through calendar year-end.
