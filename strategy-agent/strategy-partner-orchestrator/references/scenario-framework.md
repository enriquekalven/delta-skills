# Unified Scenario Planning Framework

**Purpose:** Define a single, consistent scenario framework that all 11 strategy skills use to model alternatives, test resilience, and make robust decisions. Every skill uses the same 4 scenarios with the same definitions and assumptions.

**Version:** 1.0 | **Created:** 2026-03-10 | **Audit Reference:** P1-2

---

## 1. Framework Overview

The StrategyOS ecosystem uses a unified 4-scenario framework. Every skill, in every engagement, models strategies and plans across the same scenarios with consistent definitions.

### Why Unified Scenarios?

- **Consistency:** All skills speak the same language; no "Financial's Base Case ≠ GTM's Base Case" confusion
- **Resilience Testing:** Strategy is validated across coherent alternatives, not arbitrary variations
- **Probability Weighting:** Every scenario has explicit probability, driving capital allocation and risk decisions
- **Assumption Validation:** Tied to macro dimensions; if assumptions change, scenarios shift predictably
- **Decision Quality:** Strategy robust across multiple futures, not optimized for one

### The 4 Standard Scenarios

Every engagement models these scenarios:

1. **UPSIDE Scenario** (Probability: 20%)
   - Favorable market conditions, strong execution capability, enabling tech trajectory
   - Not "best case optimism"; it's favorable but plausible
   - Use for opportunity assessment and upside value capture

2. **BASE CASE Scenario** (Probability: 50%)
   - Expected conditions; most likely evolution of market, competition, execution
   - The "steady state" against which we build strategy
   - Use for primary strategy validation and target-setting

3. **DOWNSIDE Scenario** (Probability: 20%)
   - Adverse market conditions, constrained execution, disruptive competition
   - Not catastrophic; it's plausible adversity (e.g., market growth slows, competitor moves faster)
   - Use for resilience testing and contingency planning

4. **DISRUPTIVE Scenario** (Probability: 10%)
   - Black swan / transformation scenario; a discontinuity that changes market structure
   - Low probability but high impact (e.g., new technology, regulatory shock, business model disruption)
   - Use for strategy flexibility and long-term positioning

**Probability allocation rationale:** Base=50% (steady-state expected), Upside+Downside=40% (meaningful variations around steady state), Disruptive=10% (low-probability, high-impact tail risk). Total = 100%.

## 2. Scenario Dimensions

Each of the 4 scenarios is defined by 5 macro dimensions. All skills evaluate each scenario using these same dimensions:

### Dimension 1: Market Growth Trajectory

**Upside:**
- Acceleration of market growth above historical trend
- Customer appetite for solution above expectations
- Expansion of addressable market (TAM growth)
- Example: "Market growth 15%+ CAGR vs. historical 8%"

**Base Case:**
- Market grows at historical trend or expert consensus expectations
- Customer adoption follows projected S-curve
- TAM remains as currently defined
- Example: "Market grows 8-10% CAGR; TAM stable"

**Downside:**
- Market growth slows below historical trend
- Customer adoption hesitation or budget constraints
- TAM contracts or expands slower than expected
- Example: "Market growth 3-5% CAGR; limited TAM expansion"

**Disruptive:**
- Market structure changes; historical growth rates become irrelevant
- New customer segments emerge or traditional segments disappear
- TAM redefined (expanded or contracted) due to disruption
- Example: "Market consolidates around new technology; legacy players lose TAM; new players emerge with larger TAM"

---

### Dimension 2: Competitive Dynamics

**Upside:**
- Favorable competitive environment for our strategy
- Incumbent competitors slow to respond or constrained
- New entrants don't disrupt our strategy
- Customer switching costs or strong preference toward our position
- Example: "Incumbents distracted by legacy business; we gain share"

**Base Case:**
- Competitive environment as currently observed
- Competitors respond at historical speed and aggression
- Market share shifts follow competitive positioning logic
- Example: "Competitors maintain current market positions; normal competitive dynamics"

**Downside:**
- Aggressive competition; price pressure
- Competitors more focused or better capitalized than expected
- Competitive moat erodes faster than expected
- Customer preference shifts toward competitors
- Example: "Price competition intensifies; margins compress 20%"

**Disruptive:**
- Competitive structure transforms; new competitors or alliances
- Business model disruption undermines our competitive positioning
- Industry consolidation or fragmentation creates new competitive landscape
- Example: "New competitor emerges with platform that commoditizes our solution; entire value chain shifts"

---

### Dimension 3: Execution Capability

**Upside:**
- Our execution exceeds expectations
- Talent acquisition/retention better than plan
- Organizational alignment and speed exceed norms
- Customer success and satisfaction exceed targets
- Example: "Team hiring ahead of plan, execution velocity 20% above forecast"

**Base Case:**
- Execution matches plan assumptions
- Talent and org perform as expected
- Customer success meets targets
- Timeline and resource assumptions hold
- Example: "Execution matches forecast; team delivers on plan"

**Downside:**
- Execution lags plan
- Talent acquisition slower or more costly than expected
- Organizational friction or capability gaps emerge
- Customer success or satisfaction shortfalls
- Example: "Key hires delayed 6 months; execution velocity 20% below plan"

**Disruptive:**
- Major execution disruption (key leader departure, critical failure, etc.)
- Org unable to adapt to changing conditions
- Capability gaps emerge in critical areas
- Example: "Key technical founder leaves; recruiting for replacement takes 12 months; execution stalls"

---

### Dimension 4: Technology Trajectory

**Upside:**
- New technologies accelerate our strategy
- Tech adoption by customers faster than expected
- Our tech roadmap feasible and delivers value ahead of schedule
- Enabling tech removes constraints
- Example: "AI/ML adoption accelerates customer decision-making, our AI features drive faster adoption"

**Base Case:**
- Technology trajectory follows current expectations
- Our tech roadmap remains viable and on schedule
- Adoption of enabling tech progresses as forecast
- Tech risks manageable
- Example: "Tech roadmap on track; enabling tech adoption at forecast pace"

**Downside:**
- Technology adoption slower than expected
- Our tech roadmap encounters delays or increased complexity
- Enabling tech disrupts but in directions we didn't anticipate
- Tech talent constraints or cost increases
- Example: "AI ML adoption slower; our ML capabilities harder to build; timeline slips 6-12 months"

**Disruptive:**
- Discontinuous technology change; new paradigm emerges
- Our tech strategy becomes partially or fully obsolete
- New entrants with better tech architecture
- Example: "Quantum computing disrupts current crypto assumptions; or new foundational tech paradigm (e.g., neurotech, biotech) emerges that changes industry structure"

---

### Dimension 5: Regulatory Environment

**Upside:**
- Favorable regulatory developments
- Regulation removes barriers or favors our position
- Compliance costs lower than planned
- Regulatory moat protects our position
- Example: "Regulation favors SaaS over on-premise; our positioning becomes more attractive"

**Base Case:**
- Regulatory environment remains as currently understood
- Compliance costs and timelines as planned
- Regulatory changes modest and manageable
- No major regulatory surprises
- Example: "Regulatory environment stable; compliance costs as budgeted"

**Downside:**
- Regulatory headwinds or unexpected requirements
- Compliance costs higher than planned
- Regulatory changes create barriers or increase competitive intensity
- Example: "New data privacy regulation increases compliance costs 15%; creates barriers for new entrants but we're already established, so relative advantage small"

**Disruptive:**
- Transformative regulatory change
- Industry fundamentals change (e.g., banking deregulation, pharma patent changes)
- Regulatory moat collapses or new regulations create new moat
- Example: "Antitrust action breaks up market leader; new competitive landscape emerges"

## 3. Standard Scenario Definitions

### UPSIDE SCENARIO (20% probability)

**Narrative:** Market conditions accelerate our strategy. Market growth outpaces expectations, competitors are slower to respond, execution goes smoothly, and enabling technologies emerge exactly when needed.

**Dimension Ratings:**
| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Market Growth | Acceleration | TAM grows 15%+ vs. historical 8-10%; customer adoption exceeds forecast |
| Competitive Dynamics | Favorable | Incumbent competitors distracted or slow; we gain share faster than plan |
| Execution Capability | Strong | Talent acquisition ahead of plan, org velocity 20% above forecast |
| Technology Trajectory | Enabler | AI/ML or other key tech accelerates our adoption; tech roadmap beats schedule |
| Regulatory Environment | Favorable | Regulation favors our position; compliance costs lower than planned |

**Financial Implications (Example - SaaS Company):**
- Revenue: +30% vs. Base Case at year 3
- CAC: -15% vs. Base Case (easier customer acquisition)
- LTV: +20% vs. Base Case (stronger retention/expansion)
- Profitability: EBITDA margin 35%+ vs. 25% Base Case

**Strategic Implications:**
- Market opportunity larger than planned
- Competitive moat builds faster
- Can invest more in growth / R&D without profitability impact
- Should accelerate market entry / product roadmap if resource-constrained
- Talent acquisition and retention easier

**Key Assumptions for Validation:**
1. Market growth accelerates (validate quarterly: check customer growth, pricing power, expansion revenue)
2. Competitive response is slow (monitor: competitor product releases, pricing changes, customer win/loss)
3. Execution velocity high (track: hiring pace, engineering velocity, launch cadence)

**Decision Triggers (When to Shift from Base to Upside):**
- Customer growth rate (LTM) exceeds 40% for 2 consecutive quarters
- CAC declining >10% YoY with stable win rates
- Market growth data shows >12% CAGR
- Competitor response time >12 months slower than expected
- Talent acquisition 3+ months ahead of plan

---

### BASE CASE SCENARIO (50% probability)

**Narrative:** Most likely evolution. Market grows at historical rates, competitive dynamics follow observed patterns, execution meets plan, technology roadmap stays on track, regulatory environment stable.

**Dimension Ratings:**
| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Market Growth | Expected | Market grows 8-10% CAGR; TAM stable or modest expansion; adoption on S-curve |
| Competitive Dynamics | Moderate | Competitors respond at normal pace; market share shifts follow competitive logic |
| Execution Capability | Plan | Execution matches forecast; talent, org, customer success on plan |
| Technology Trajectory | Steady | Tech roadmap on schedule; enabling tech adoption at forecast pace |
| Regulatory Environment | Stable | Regulatory environment unchanged; compliance costs as planned |

**Financial Implications (Example - SaaS Company):**
- Revenue: $X at year 3 (the plan)
- CAC: $Y (as modeled)
- LTV: $Z (as modeled)
- Profitability: EBITDA margin 25% at year 3

**Strategic Implications:**
- Strategy unfolds as planned
- Competitive positioning develops on expected trajectory
- Capital requirements on plan
- Execution delivers on targets
- No major strategy adjustments required

**Key Assumptions for Validation:**
1. Market grows at 8-10% CAGR (validate quarterly: confirm growth rates, pricing, TAM)
2. Competitive dynamics follow current patterns (monitor: competitor moves, market dynamics)
3. Execution meets plan (track: all OKRs, hiring, product launches)
4. Tech roadmap on schedule (monitor: engineering milestones, tech adoption)

**Decision Triggers (When to Shift from Base):**
- Any two dimensions miss forecast for 2 consecutive quarters → downside
- Any two dimensions exceed forecast for 2 consecutive quarters → upside
- Disruptive signal emerges (new competitor, market structure change) → disruptive scenario

---

### DOWNSIDE SCENARIO (20% probability)

**Narrative:** Adverse conditions emerge. Market growth slows, competition intensifies, execution lags, or technology adoption disappoints. Strategy remains viable but requires adjustment and capital patience.

**Dimension Ratings:**
| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Market Growth | Contraction | Market growth slows to 3-5% CAGR; customer adoption hesitation; TAM contracts or expands slower |
| Competitive Dynamics | Hostile | Price competition intensifies; competitor wins accelerate; market share pressure |
| Execution Capability | Constrained | Talent acquisition slower, org friction emerges, execution velocity 20% below plan |
| Technology Trajectory | Disruptor | Tech adoption slower; enabling tech emerges in directions we didn't anticipate; we must adapt |
| Regulatory Environment | Restrictive | New regulatory requirements increase compliance costs; create barriers |

**Financial Implications (Example - SaaS Company):**
- Revenue: -25% vs. Base Case at year 3
- CAC: +25% vs. Base Case (harder customer acquisition)
- LTV: -15% vs. Base Case (lower retention/expansion)
- Profitability: EBITDA margin 15% vs. 25% Base Case; path to profitability extended

**Strategic Implications:**
- Market opportunity smaller than planned
- Competitive moat builds slower
- Capital requirements increase (longer to profitability)
- May need to defer growth investments, focus on profitability
- Talent acquisition more competitive
- Strategy requires adaptation (pivot, exit market, reduce scope)

**Key Assumptions for Validation:**
1. Market growth slows (watch quarterly: growth rates, customer hesitation, pricing pressure)
2. Competition intensifies (monitor: pricing, wins/losses, competitor moves)
3. Execution lags (track: hiring challenges, execution velocity, delivery delays)

**Decision Triggers (When to Shift into Downside):**
- Customer growth rate (LTM) falls below 20% for 2 consecutive quarters
- Market growth data shows <5% CAGR
- Win rate declines >20% or deal cycle lengthens >3 months
- Talent acquisition >6 months behind plan
- CAC increasing >20% without corresponding LTV increase

**Kill Conditions (When to Exit Strategy):**
- Market growth falls below 0% (market contracting)
- Win rate falls below X% (no longer viable in market)
- CAC exceeds LTV / payback >24 months
- Competitor dominance makes defensible position impossible

---

### DISRUPTIVE SCENARIO (10% probability)

**Narrative:** Discontinuous change. Market structure transforms, new competitors emerge, technology paradigm shifts, or regulatory framework transforms. Entire industry reorganizes. Previous strategy may become partially or fully obsolete.

**Dimension Ratings:**
| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Market Growth | Transformation | Market TAM redefined; new customer segments emerge; legacy segments collapse |
| Competitive Dynamics | Restructuring | New competitors; alliances reshape industry; business model disruption |
| Execution Capability | Requires Adaptation | Strategy must adapt rapidly; existing execution model may become obsolete |
| Technology Trajectory | Paradigm Shift | New foundational technology (e.g., quantum, AI, biotech); current tech becomes obsolete |
| Regulatory Environment | Structural Change | Industry structure changed by regulation (breakup, consolidation, new frameworks) |

**Financial Implications (Example - SaaS Company):**
- Revenue: Highly uncertain; current business model may be partially obsolete
- CAC/LTV: Metrics may not apply in new market structure
- Profitability: Path to profitability in new competitive structure unclear
- Valuation: Significant upside OR downside depending on adaptation

**Strategic Implications:**
- Existing strategy may become partially or fully obsolete
- Need for rapid adaptation or pivoting
- New competitive landscape with different winners
- Significant opportunity for adaptable companies; existential risk for rigid ones
- May require entering new markets or exiting current ones

**Key Indicators of Disruption (Watch For):**
1. New competitor with fundamentally different business model
2. Technology paradigm shift (e.g., new AI capability, new materials science, regulatory breakthrough)
3. Regulatory transformation (e.g., antitrust action, new frameworks)
4. Customer behavior shift (e.g., new channel adoption, new buying model)
5. Incumbent vulnerability (e.g., legacy business model under pressure)

**Examples of Disruptive Scenarios:**
- **SaaS Company:** AI-native competitor emerges with 10x better product (paradigm shift)
- **Financial Services:** Blockchain enables decentralized finance; traditional intermediaries disrupted
- **Transportation:** Autonomous vehicles mature; ride-hailing and ownership models transform
- **Healthcare:** New diagnostic technology disrupts entire diagnostic industry structure
- **Telecom:** Satellite internet or new spectrum opens completely new competitive dynamics

**Response Strategy:**
- In Base/Downside mode, monitor for disruptive signals
- If disruptive signals emerge, shift strategy to "defensive positioning" for existing business + "exploration" for new business models
- Examples: Apple exploration of health devices while defending iPhone; traditional media exploration of streaming while defending legacy business
- Accept lower margins in legacy business to fund exploration in new business models

## 4. Skill-Specific Scenario Applications

Every skill applies the scenario framework in its domain. Here's how:

### STRATEGY PARTNER

**Role:** Diagnose what's happening now; frame scenarios as "what could change?"

**Scenario Application:**
- **Base Case:** Current diagnosis assumes Base Case dynamics (market growth, competitive response, etc.)
- **Scenarios as Risk Layers:** "What if market growth slows (Downside)? What if a new competitor disrupts (Disruptive)?"
- **Scenario Planning Module:** Explicitly runs Scenario Planning analysis in Standard/Deep Dive modes
- **Decision Frame:** Includes scenario-specific decision paths (IF Upside, THEN X; IF Downside, THEN Y)

**Output:**
- Base Case diagnosis + confidence
- Scenario-specific diagnoses (what changes in each scenario?)
- Early warning indicators for scenario shifts

---

### RUMELT STRATEGY FORGE

**Role:** Create coherent strategy; test resilience across scenarios.

**Scenario Application:**
- **Kernel Resilience Testing:** Does the strategy kernel remain coherent and viable in each scenario?
- **Base Case Strategy:** Strategy optimized for Base Case probability
- **Scenario Variant Planning:** If Upside occurs, how do we capture extra value? If Downside, what's the adapted approach?
- **Break-Even Scenario:** What's the worst-case scenario where strategy still delivers acceptable returns?

**Output:**
- Strategy kernel optimized for Base Case
- Resilience scoring: Is kernel viable in Upside? Downside? Disruptive?
- Adaptation rules: IF [scenario shifts], THEN [adapt strategy to X]
- Kill conditions: IF [scenario bottoms out], THEN [exit strategy]

---

### GROWTH STRATEGY

**Role:** Model growth curves and market assumptions across scenarios.

**Scenario Application:**
- **Market Sizing by Scenario:** TAM, SAM, SOM differ by scenario
- **Growth Curve Modeling:** Growth rate assumptions differ per scenario
- **Customer Economics by Scenario:** CAC/LTV differ by scenario
- **Expansion Roadmap by Scenario:** Sequence and timing of market entry varies by scenario

**Output:**
- Growth model for Base Case
- Growth models for Upside, Downside, Disruptive scenarios
- Sensitivity: What growth rate assumptions are make-or-break?

**Example - SaaS Growth Strategy:**
| Scenario | TAM | Year 1 Growth | Year 3 Growth | CAC | LTV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upside | $10B | 60% | 50% | $1,000 | $25,000 |
| Base | $7B | 40% | 30% | $1,300 | $20,000 |
| Downside | $4B | 25% | 15% | $1,600 | $15,000 |
| Disruptive | $8B (redefined) | Uncertain | Uncertain | Unknown | Unknown |

---

### GTM STRATEGY

**Role:** Design customer acquisition and positioning; validate across scenarios.

**Scenario Application:**
- **Value Proposition by Scenario:** Why customers buy differs per scenario
- **Sales Process by Scenario:** Cycle length, deal size, win rate varies
- **Messaging by Scenario:** Key messages resonate differently in different scenarios
- **Customer Economics by Scenario:** CAC and customer acquisition funnel differs

**Output:**
- GTM motion for Base Case
- GTM variants for each scenario (e.g., premium positioning in Upside, value positioning in Downside)
- Customer acquisition funnel metrics for each scenario

**Example - SaaS GTM Strategy:**
| Scenario | Target Customer | Value Prop | Positioning | Sales Cycle | Win Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upside | Enterprise premium | AI-native advantage | Market leader | 4-6 months | 25% |
| Base | Mid-market | Balance of features & price | Strong alternative | 6-8 months | 20% |
| Downside | SMB / cost-conscious | ROI-focused | Budget option | 8-12 months | 15% |
| Disruptive | New segment emerging | Solves new problem | Disruptor | Unknown | Unknown |

---

### FINANCIAL STRATEGY

**Role:** Model business unit economics and capital requirements; full P&L across scenarios.

**Scenario Application:**
- **Revenue by Scenario:** Growth assumptions, unit economics, pricing differ per scenario
- **Profitability by Scenario:** Cost structure and margins differ per scenario
- **Cash Flow by Scenario:** Working capital and cash needs differ per scenario
- **Capital Requirements by Scenario:** Upside requires growth capital; Downside requires survival capital

**Output:**
- Full 5-10 year P&L/Cash Flow for each scenario
- Key metrics (LTV:CAC, months to payback, EBITDA margin, etc.) by scenario
- Valuation ranges (Upside, Base, Downside valuations)
- Capital requirement and runway by scenario

## 5. Scenario Assumption Dependency Map

This matrix shows which assumptions, if invalidated, shift which scenario. Use this to identify "make-or-break" assumptions per scenario and set up monitoring.

### Example Assumption Dependency Map (SaaS Company)

| Assumption | Make-or-Break For | Invalidation Threshold | Scenario Shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market grows 8-10% CAGR | Base Case | If growth <5% CAGR | Shift to Downside |
| Market grows 8-10% CAGR | Base Case | If growth >15% CAGR | Shift to Upside |
| Win rate 20% | Base Case | If win rate <15% for 2 quarters | Shift to Downside |
| Customer retention 90% annual | Base Case | If retention <85% for 2 quarters | Shift to Downside |
| CAC $1,300 | Base Case | If CAC >$1,700 for 2 quarters | Shift to Downside |
| Talent acquisition on plan | Base Case | If hiring 6+ months behind plan | Shift to Downside |
| Competitors respond in 6 months | Base Case | If competitor product in 3 months | Shift to Disruptive |
| Tech roadmap on schedule | Base Case | If tech delays >6 months | Shift to Downside |
| Regulatory environment stable | Base Case | If major new regulation emerges | Shift to Downside or Disruptive |
| New disruptive competitor emerges | Disruptive | Any signal of new competitor with different model | Activate Disruptive scenario |

### How to Use This Map:
1. Identify 10-15 critical assumptions for your strategy
2. For each assumption, define the "invalidation threshold" (when is the assumption broken?)
3. For each threshold breach, specify the scenario shift
4. Set up monitoring dashboard to track these 10-15 assumptions quarterly
5. When assumption breached, shift scenario probability accordingly

## 6. Scenario Monitoring & Triggers

In Standard and Deep Dive engagements, all skills establish monitoring for scenario shifts. Here's the framework:

### Real-Time Scenario Monitoring

**Every engagement establishes:**

1. **Key Scenario Indicators** (10-15 per skill)
   - Market growth rate
   - Win rate / customer acquisition rate
   - CAC / LTV metrics
   - Competitor moves
   - Talent acquisition progress
   - Product adoption rates
   - Execution velocity
   - etc.

2. **Monitoring Cadence**
   - Weekly: High-velocity metrics (sales activity, hiring progress, product usage)
   - Monthly: Medium-velocity metrics (win rate trends, CAC trends)
   - Quarterly: Scenario assessment and probability update

3. **Trigger Thresholds**
   - Define specific thresholds that trigger scenario shifts
   - Example: "If quarterly customer growth <20%, enter Downside assessment"

4. **Reporting Dashboard**
   - Central dashboard tracking all scenario indicators
   - Color-coded: Green (scenario supporting), Yellow (scenario uncertain), Red (scenario contradicting)
   - Automated alerts when threshold breached

### Scenario Shift Decision Process

**When evidence suggests scenario shift:**

1. **Assess Evidence Quality**
   - Is the signal strong enough to act on? (avoid false positives)
   - Is data reliable? (exclude one-month anomalies)

2. **Update Scenario Probabilities**
   - Adjust probability weighting for each scenario
   - Example: If market growth evidence shifts, Base Case down from 50% to 40%, Downside up from 20% to 30%

3. **Recommend Strategy Adaptation**
   - What strategy changes if scenario shifts?
   - Should we accelerate (Upside), hold (Base), or adapt (Downside)?
   - What's the execution plan for adaptation?

4. **Decision Point for Leadership**
   - Present scenario shift evidence + recommended adaptation
   - Get leadership approval before shifting strategy execution

### Disruptive Scenario Early Warning System

**Because Disruptive scenarios are low-probability but high-impact, establish explicit early warning system:**

**Weak Signals to Monitor:**
- New competitors with different business models
- Technology breakthroughs in adjacent fields
- Regulatory proposals that could reshape industry
- Customer behavior shifts
- Incumbent vulnerability signals
- Venture capital concentrating in adjacent areas

**Monitoring Approach:**
- Weekly scanning of tech news, startup funding, regulatory proposals
- Quarterly assessment: "Are any disruptive signals emerging?"
- If disruptive signal detected, trigger exploratory analysis (dedicated team explores "what if this continues?")

**Disruptive Scenario Response Strategy:**
- Maintain "defensive positioning" in existing business (protect cash, manage legacy business)
- Fund "exploration initiatives" in potential disruptive spaces (5-10% of capital)
- Stay flexible: ability to pivot if disruptive trend accelerates
- Build optionality: maintain multiple growth paths

## 8. Worked Example: SaaS Company Growth Strategy

Let's walk through how a SaaS company (Example: "DataViz Pro" - data visualization platform for SMBs) applies scenario framework across multiple skills.

### Strategic Context
- **Current State:** $3M ARR, 40% growth, dominant in SMB segment
- **Strategic Question:** Should we move upmarket to mid-market? What's the growth opportunity?
- **Time Horizon:** 3-year strategy

### Strategy Partner Diagnosis (Base Case)

**Situation:** Market growing 8-10% CAGR, SMB adoption strong, mid-market opportunity emerging but competition from established players is real.

**Scenarios Identified:**
- **Upside:** Mid-market customers eager for lightweight alternative to established player; we capture 20% of addressable mid-market
- **Base:** Gradual mid-market entry, compete on value and ease-of-use; gain 10% of mid-market
- **Downside:** Mid-market entry harder than expected; customers prefer established solutions; we stay focused on SMB, growth slows
- **Disruptive:** Embedded analytics capability or AI-native competitor disrupts both SMB and mid-market; entire market structure shifts

### Growth Strategy Modeling (All Scenarios)

**Base Case Growth Model:**
| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARR | $5M | $10M | $18M |
| Customer Growth | 40% | 35% | 30% |
| SMB Segment | 70% of revenue | 60% of revenue | 50% of revenue |
| Mid-Market Segment | 30% of revenue | 40% of revenue | 50% of revenue |
| CAC (avg) | $2,000 | $1,800 | $1,600 |
| LTV (36-month) | $24,000 | $26,000 | $28,000 |

**Upside Scenario (20% probability):**
| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARR | $7M | $15M | $32M |
| Customer Growth | 60% | 50% | 45% |
| Mid-Market Segment | 40% of revenue by Year 2 | Becomes primary segment | 70% of revenue |
| CAC | $1,800 | $1,400 | $1,200 |
| LTV | $28,000 | $32,000 | $36,000 |

**Key Drivers:** Mid-market adoption faster than expected; product resonates strongly; competitors slow to respond

**Downside Scenario (20% probability):**
| Metric | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARR | $4M | $6M | $9M |
| Customer Growth | 25% | 20% | 15% |
| Mid-Market Segment | Delayed entry; only 10% of revenue by Year 3 | Stays focused on SMB |
| CAC | $2,500 | $2,800 | $3,000 |
| LTV | $18,000 | $16,000 | $15,000 |

**Key Drivers:** Mid-market harder to sell into; product needs customization; established competitors defend aggressively

**Disruptive Scenario (10% probability):**
- Embedded analytics in Office 365 / Google Workspace disrupts standalone visualization
- AI-native visualization tool emerges with 10x better UX
- Entire market structure shifts; standalone visualization becomes commodified
- Revenue uncertain; may need to pivot to adjacent market or specialize in vertical niche

### Financial Strategy Modeling (All Scenarios)

**Base Case Year 3 P&L:**
```
ARR:                           $18M
Gross Margin:                  82%
Gross Profit:                  $14.8M

Operating Expenses:
  Sales & Marketing:           $4.5M (25% of revenue)
  Product & Engineering:       $3M (17% of revenue)
  G&A:                         $2M (11% of revenue)
  Total OpEx:                  $9.5M (53% of revenue)

EBITDA:                        $5.3M (29% margin)
```

**Upside Case Year 3:**
- ARR: $32M
- Gross Margin: 85% (scale benefits)
- OpEx: $15M (47% of revenue, improved leverage)
- EBITDA: $12M (38% margin)

**Downside Case Year 3:**
- ARR: $9M
- Gross Margin: 78% (mix shift to SMB, lower pricing)
- OpEx: $6.5M (72% of revenue, no scale leverage)
- EBITDA: $1.2M (13% margin)

### GTM Strategy by Scenario

**Base Case GTM:**
- Primary: SMB self-serve / inside sales (existing motion)
- Secondary: Mid-market enterprise sales (new, 2-3 sales reps)
- Positioning: "Lightweight, easy-to-use alternative"
- Sales Cycle: SMB 2-4 months, Mid-market 4-6 months

**Upside GTM:**
- Accelerate mid-market enterprise sales (10+ sales reps by year 2)
- Expand marketing to build mid-market awareness
- Premium positioning: "Lightweight but enterprise-grade"
- Sales Cycle: 3-4 months (customers more eager)

**Downside GTM:**
- Remain focused on SMB motion
- Minimize mid-market investment
- Value positioning: "Most affordable data visualization"
- Sales Cycle: Extended 4-6 months (more price sensitivity)

### Monitoring & Trigger Points

**Quarterly Metrics to Track (with thresholds):**

| Metric | Base Case Target | Upside Trigger (>X) | Downside Trigger (<X) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer Growth Rate (QoQ) | 10% | 15% | 6% | If 2 quarters exceed threshold, consider Upside |
| ARR Growth Rate (YoY) | 40% | 50% | 25% | Primary growth metric |
| Mid-Market Mix | 30% | 45% | <15% | If mid-market slower than plan, shift to Downside |
| CAC Trend | Declining to $1,600 | Declining to $1,200 | Rising to $2,500 | If CAC rising, margin pressure signals Downside |
| Win Rate (vs. competitors) | 20% | 25% | <15% | Competitive position indicator |
| Customer Retention (12-month) | 95% | 97% | <90% | If declining, signals Downside |
| New Competitor Signal | None | Monitor | Monitor | If new AI-native competitor enters, trigger Disruptive assessment |

### Decision Rules Based on Scenario Shifts

**IF Q1 Results Show Upside Signals:**
- Customer growth 50%+
- Mid-market mix 35%+
- Win rate 25%+
→ **Shift to Upside strategy:** Accelerate hiring, increase marketing spend, expand sales team, bring forward product roadmap

**IF Q1 Results Show Downside Signals:**
- Customer growth <25%
- CAC rising >20%
- Win rate <15%
→ **Shift to Downside strategy:** Reduce hiring, focus on profitability, extend sales cycle focus to best-fit customers, defer product features

**IF Disruptive Signal (e.g., AI-native competitor):**
→ **Activate Disruptive Response:** Explore adjacent markets (vertical specialization), build defensive moat (integration partnerships, domain expertise), fund moonshot product (AI-native visualization), maintain optionality

## 9. Implementation Checklist

For every engagement, teams should:

- [ ] **Define scenarios** using this framework (don't create custom scenarios)
- [ ] **Probability-weight** each scenario (use guidance: Base 50%, Upside/Downside 20% each, Disruptive 10%)
- [ ] **Model every dimension** for each scenario (all 5 dimensions, even if not all relevant)
- [ ] **Create scenario-specific strategy variants** (what's the adapted strategy if scenario shifts?)
- [ ] **Identify make-or-break assumptions** (what must be true for each scenario?)
- [ ] **Set up monitoring dashboard** (track 10-15 key indicators quarterly)
- [ ] **Define trigger thresholds** (when do we shift scenario assessment?)
- [ ] **Output in standard format** (JSON schema per section 7 above)
- [ ] **Communicate to leadership** (scenarios should be known to all decision-makers)
