# Executive Communication & Synthesis (10/10 Framework)

## What Changed from Original
The original was strong on framework mechanics (SCQA, pyramid, logic) but treated communication as analysis-to-text only. Missing: objection scripts, audience consumption patterns, format selection, rehearsal methodology, presentation dynamics, narrative templates, and anti-patterns. This version adds all ten and turns analysis into unshakeable presentations.

## When to Use
Use at END of analytical workflow to convert findings into executive-ready communication. Also use as quality gate before presentations. Use objection scripts and rehearsal methodology in prep phase. Use presentation techniques during live delivery.

## PART 1: AUDIENCE ATTENTION ARCHITECTURE

Executives read in a pattern: first line, last line, then headlines. They skip middles and view charts before text.

**Attention Pattern:**
- Line 1: Decision or dramatic finding (they scan here)
- Headlines only: They read these if interested
- Charts/visuals: They glance before text
- Middle paragraphs: Skipped 60% of the time
- Last line: Conclusion and ask (they read here)

**Implication for Your Structure:**
- Bury supporting detail, not conclusions
- Put the recommendation in the first paragraph and the last paragraph
- Make slide headlines complete thoughts, not labels
- Lead with charts, follow with text
- Use visual hierarchy relentlessly

## PART 2: DOCUMENT HIERARCHY DECISION TREE

**One-Page Memo** — Use when:
- Decision is simple or obvious
- Audience already aligned
- You need fast buy-in
- Time-sensitive ask
- Structure: Situation (2 lines) → Recommendation (1 line) → Evidence (3 points) → Ask

**Five-Page Brief** — Use when:
- Multiple options need comparison
- Audience is skeptical
- Implementation details matter
- Stakeholders have conflicting views
- Structure: Executive summary → Situation → Options (with trade-offs) → Recommended path → Next steps

**Full Presentation Deck** — Use when:
- Stakes are high (board approval, major investment, strategic pivot)
- You need to rehearse and handle questions
- Multiple audiences need consistent messaging
- Analysis is complex and needs visual scaffolding
- Structure: SCQA setup → Evidence pyramid → Decision page → Q&A prep

**Email** — Use ONLY when:
- FYI communication, not a decision request
- Update on progress or completed work
- Coordination/scheduling
- NEVER for strategy recommendations or major asks
- Default to memo or brief instead

## PART 3: OBJECTION-HANDLING SCRIPTS

Pre-build responses to the 10 most common executive objections. Test in rehearsal. Adjust to your context.

**Objection 1: "We tried this before"**
- Script: "The context has shifted. [Specific change in market/competition/capability]. Our previous approach wouldn't work now because [specific barrier from then that's different now]. This accounts for why the old approach stalled."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Mention competitive moves or market changes upfront to preempt this

**Objection 2: "The data isn't conclusive"**
- Script: "You're right — we have [confidence level] confidence here. Here's what we CAN say conclusively: [one solid finding]. Even without perfect data, the cost of delay is [specific impact], and this recommendation limits downside while capturing upside."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Lead with confidence levels and acknowledge uncertainty upfront

**Objection 3: "We can't afford this"**
- Script: "Fair point. The cost is [amount]. Here's the payback timeline: [specific]. And here's what we're deferring to fund this: [trade-off]. If we don't do this, our cost is [opportunity cost or competitive risk]."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Always bring the investment conversation to resource trade-offs, not just the number

**Objection 4: "What about competitor X?"**
- Script: "Competitor X's approach is [their strategy]. It works for them because [their advantage]. We'd struggle with it because [our constraint]. Our approach is designed specifically for [our advantage], which is why we lead with [recommendation] instead."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Bring competitive comparisons yourself first; don't wait to be asked

**Objection 5: "This assumes market conditions we can't control"**
- Script: "True. Our base case assumes [assumptions]. Here's our sensitivity: if [adverse scenario], the recommendation shifts to [contingency]. We've built the plan to pivot on [specific trigger]."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Stress-test assumptions in pre-reads and show you've thought through downside

**Objection 6: "This will take too long"**
- Script: "The full timeline is [months]. But here's the critical path: [phases and what we can achieve by quarter]. We can show meaningful progress by [date] before [later milestone]."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Lead with phases and early wins, not the total duration

**Objection 7: "Execution always lags plan"**
- Script: "That's true — and here's how we account for it. [Show contingency buffer in timeline]. We're also assigning [owner] with [resources/authority] to drive this, and we'll track [specific metric] weekly, not quarterly."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Bring governance and owner conversation upfront to build confidence

**Objection 8: "Our culture/organization can't do this"**
- Script: "We'd need to change [specific capability or structure]. We've seen [comparable company] move through this in [timeframe] by [method]. We'll start with [pilot or scaled approach] to prove it works here first."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Build organizational change into the plan itself; don't treat it as separate

**Objection 9: "Why now, not later?"**
- Script: "Two things are happening now that weren't true [timeframe] ago: [market condition] and [our capability]. This window closes in [timeframe] because [competitive or market reason]. Waiting costs us [specific impact]."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Make timing logic explicit in the brief; don't assume it's obvious

**Objection 10: "Who else is doing this?"**
- Script: "[Reference company] moved this direction because [their driver]. Their outcome: [result]. In our context, we'd expect [our outcome] because [our advantages]."
- Plant in pre-meeting: Bring social proof yourself; don't wait to be asked

**Meta-Rule for Objections:**
Don't argue. Acknowledge the objection is legitimate, then reframe using new information or different logic. "That's a fair point" takes the tension out of the room.

## PART 4: FORMAT & STRUCTURE FRAMEWORK

### The SCQA Frame (Unchanged)
**Situation:** Shared context (1-2 sentences).
**Complication:** The tension or change that makes action necessary (specific, evidence-based).
**Question:** Implicit (don't state it).
**Answer:** The recommendation, direct and confident.

### Pyramid Logic (Unchanged)
- 2-4 supporting arguments below the Answer
- Each argument is a complete thought
- Vertical logic: each argument answers "why?" or "how?"
- Horizontal logic: all arguments are the same type
- Sub-arguments: 2-4 per argument, all parallel

### Decision Page Format
```
DECISION REQUESTED: [Specific ask]

RECOMMENDATION: [What we should do]

KEY EVIDENCE:
  1. [Strongest argument + data]
  2. [Second strongest argument + data]
  3. [Third strongest argument + data]

COUNTERARGUMENT & REBUTTAL:
  [The strongest case against] → [Why we still recommend]
  [Key risk] → [How we mitigate]

WHAT WE NEED: [Resources, approval, timeline]

COST OF INACTION: [Specific impact if we don't act]
```

## PART 5: NARRATIVE ARCS BY STRATEGY TYPE

Different strategy types need different emotional arcs. Match the story to the recommendation.

**Turnaround Story** — Crisis → Stabilization → Growth
- Emotional arc: Urgency → Reassurance → Hope
- Opening: "We're losing [metric] at [rate] to [cause]. This is fixable if we act now."
- Middle: "Here's the stabilization plan and the quick wins we can show in [timeframe]."
- Close: "Once we're stable, we'll be positioned to [growth vision]."

**Growth Acceleration** — Current path → Constraint → Opportunity
- Emotional arc: Pride → Ambition → Excitement
- Opening: "We've built [achievement]. Our constraint is now [specific limit]."
- Middle: "This move unlocks [specific customer/market/capability] that gets us to [ambitious target]."
- Close: "This is how we move from good to dominant in [market]."

**Defensive Repositioning** — Threat → Competitive Response → New Position
- Emotional arc: Alert → Confidence → Ownership
- Opening: "[Competitor] or [market trend] threatens [our advantage]. We need to move."
- Middle: "This repositioning puts us ahead because [competitive advantage]."
- Close: "We're not reacting — we're setting the agenda for [market segment]."

**M&A / Acquisition Case** — Fragmentation → Consolidation → Synergy
- Emotional arc: Problem → Solution → Scale
- Opening: "[Market dynamic] creates fragmentation. We can consolidate."
- Middle: "[Target] gives us [capability/customer base/market access]. We can integrate [synergy]."
- Close: "This creates a company that [competitive advantage] in [market]."

**Cost Transformation** — Inefficiency → Reallocation → Reinvestment
- Emotional arc: Pragmatism → Discipline → Vision
- Opening: "We're spending [amount] on [inefficient areas]. We can redirect that."
- Middle: "By [specific action], we save [amount] and reinvest in [growth area]."
- Close: "This keeps us competitive while funding [strategic initiative]."

## PART 6: PRE-SOCIALIZATION & LANDING ZONE TECHNIQUE

Don't present strategy cold. Pre-socialize to make the audience feel like they co-created it.

**Step 1: Plant Insights (2-3 weeks before)**
- Share one unexpected data point in a 1:1 or small group
- Ask for their reaction, not their agreement
- Listen for what resonates and what concerns them
- Example: "We're seeing [insight]. What's your take on this?"

**Step 2: Test the Frame (1-2 weeks before)**
- Describe the situation and complication (without the answer)
- Propose a straw man recommendation that's slightly weaker than yours
- Ask: "Does this frame feel right to you? What would you change?"
- Capture their suggestions and incorporate or address in the full brief

**Step 3: Build Coalition (1 week before)**
- Brief key stakeholders individually before the group meeting
- Share the recommendation and get their input
- Tell them: "I'm going to present this to the full group. Any issues I should know about?"
- Resolve objections before the room, not in it

**Step 4: Open with Alignment (In the presentation)**
- Recap the situation and complication (familiar ground)
- Acknowledge input from pre-meetings: "Based on [stakeholder name]'s point, we refined this to..."
- Present the recommendation as inevitable given the frame
- Audience feels like they informed it, because they did

## PART 7: LIVE PRESENTATION TECHNIQUES

**Handling Hostile Questions**
- Pause and repeat the question: "So you're asking whether [reframe the concern]?"
- Separate fact from opinion: "The facts here are [objective]. The interpretation is [your view]."
- Offer to go deeper: "That's a great question. We can dig into [specific area] in the breakout."
- Don't defend; reframe: "Fair point. Here's how we're thinking about [risk]."

**Buying Time**
- Never "I don't know." Instead: "That's outside the scope we analyzed, but here's my thinking..."
- Reframe to what you DO know: "The broader pattern we're seeing is [related insight]."
- Punt to follow-up: "Let me dig into that specifically and get back to you by [date]."
- Ask a clarifying question: "Are you asking about [interpretation A] or [interpretation B]?"

**Landing the Plane (When Running Out of Time)**
- Recap the core recommendation in one sentence: "Bottom line: [decision]."
- State the next step: "Here's what we'll do in [timeframe]: [action]."
- Open for questions: "What questions do we need to resolve?"
- Don't try to summarize everything; hit the decision and next step only

**Redirecting Off-Topic Questions**
- Acknowledge: "That's important."
- Explain why it's separate: "It's a separate decision from [what we're solving for today]."
- Propose a follow-up: "Let's tackle that in [separate meeting] so we stay focused here."
- Move on: "Back to [topic] — here's where we landed..."

## PART 8: DATA VISUALIZATION PRINCIPLES FOR STRATEGY

**Charts that Work:**
- **Waterfall:** Shows how you got from A to B (revenue bridge, cost reduction, headcount)
- **Timeline:** Tells sequence and shows critical path and dependencies
- **Scatter plot:** Shows correlation or trade-off between two variables
- **Stacked bar:** Compares composition across periods or groups
- **Line trend:** Shows trajectory and inflection points

**Charts that Fail:**
- 3D pie charts (angles are hard to read, looks gimmicky)
- Radar charts (almost always less clear than simpler alternatives)
- Dual-axis charts (tempting to manipulate visually)
- Cluttered dashboards (pick 3-4 metrics that matter, not 20)
- Table with 10+ columns (use chart instead)

**Strategy-Specific Guidance:**
- Show "before state" and "after state" visually side-by-side for transformations
- Use color to highlight the decision or recommendation (your option in bold color, alternatives in gray)
- Annotate surprises or counterintuitive findings directly on the chart
- If the chart requires explanation, the chart has failed — redesign it

## PART 9: FIRST 30 SECONDS TEMPLATES

Different contexts need different opens. Pre-write and memorize for confidence.

**Board Meeting (High stakes):**
"We need to [specific decision] because [one compelling fact or market shift]. This puts us ahead of [competitive threat or market trend]. We can execute this in [timeframe] with [key resource]. Here's why we should go."

**1:1 with CEO (Seeking alignment):**
"I'm stuck on [strategic question] and want your intuition. The data says [one finding], but I'm worried about [one counterargument]. What am I missing?"

**Cross-Functional Team (Seeking buy-in):**
"This affects all of us because [impact]. Here's the situation: [2-sentence frame]. We're recommending [action]. I want to walk you through the logic and hear what you see that I might be missing."

**Investor Pitch (Fundraising):**
"Most [market segment] companies do [incumbent approach]. We're doing [differentiated approach] because [competitive advantage]. That lets us capture [market opportunity] worth [$X]."

**Crisis/Emergency (Rallying the room):**
"Here's what's happening: [situation]. Here's what we're doing: [action]. Here's how you help: [your role]. We move on [timeline]."

## PART 10: ANTI-PATTERNS WITH BEFORE/AFTER REWRITES

**Anti-Pattern 1: Burying the Recommendation**
- BEFORE: "After analyzing market trends, competitive positioning, and internal capabilities, we believe that a potential path forward might be to consider a phased approach to market expansion..."
- AFTER: "We should enter the mid-market segment with a dedicated sales team by Q3. It addresses [reason 1], captures [opportunity], and positions us ahead of [competitor]."

**Anti-Pattern 2: Opinion Without Evidence**
- BEFORE: "Customer satisfaction is a key driver of growth."
- AFTER: "Customers with NPS > 50 have a 3x higher retention rate and 2x higher expansion revenue. We're at NPS 42. Moving to 50+ would add $5M annual retention."

**Anti-Pattern 3: Wrong Format for the Ask**
- BEFORE: Full 50-page analysis when CEO needs a one-pager
- AFTER: One-page memo with recommendation and top 3 reasons. Offer the full analysis if they want to dig deeper.

**Anti-Pattern 4: Vague Language**
- BEFORE: "We should leverage operational synergies to drive efficiency gains."
- AFTER: "We should consolidate our three supplier relationships into one. That saves $800K annually and reduces procurement complexity from 3 systems to 1."

**Anti-Pattern 5: Missing the Counterargument**
- BEFORE: "This is the right move for these reasons: [3 reasons]."
- AFTER: "This is the right move because [reasons]. The risk we're taking on is [honest risk]. We're mitigating it by [how]."

**Anti-Pattern 6: Ignoring Audience Attention Pattern**
- BEFORE: Pages of context before the recommendation appears in paragraph 7
- AFTER: Recommendation in first paragraph. Evidence in middle. Restatement in last paragraph.

**Anti-Pattern 7: No Clear Next Step**
- BEFORE: "In conclusion, this represents an important opportunity for the company to explore..."
- AFTER: "Here's what we need you to approve: [specific ask]. Here's when: [date]. Here's what we'll do by [date]: [first milestone]."

**Anti-Pattern 8: Treating Objections as Attacks**
- BEFORE: Defend aggressively, argue with the questioner
- AFTER: "That's a fair point. We're assuming [assumption]. If that doesn't hold, we'd pivot to [alternative]."

**Anti-Pattern 9: Showing Everything Instead of Leading**
- BEFORE: Deck with 60 slides covering all analysis
- AFTER: Deck with 8 slides telling the core story. Appendix has full analysis if needed.

**Anti-Pattern 10: Presenting for the First Time in the Room**
- BEFORE: Bringing recommendation cold to the meeting
- AFTER: Pre-socialized with key stakeholders. Presentation confirms alignment rather than fighting for it.

## REHEARSAL METHODOLOGY

**Week Before:**
- Write out full talking points for each slide
- Anticipate the 5 hardest questions you could face
- Prepare one-sentence answers to each (don't over-explain)
- Do a 30-minute dry run with a peer; ask them to challenge you

**Day Before:**
- Walk through deck once more (confidence, not perfection)
- Visit the room and test equipment
- Write down your first 30 seconds and last 30 seconds (memorize these)

**Morning Of:**
- Arrive early; do a 10-minute walkthrough
- Slow down your speaking pace (nervous people rush)
- Remember: your job is to lead the conversation, not lecture them

## FINAL QUALITY GATE

Before delivering, verify:
- [ ] Recommendation is specific and passes the Monday Morning Test
- [ ] No vague language survives
- [ ] SCQA frame is tight; Answer is direct and early
- [ ] Pyramid logic is vertical and horizontal
- [ ] Document format matches the audience and decision
- [ ] Objection scripts are prepped for your specific context
- [ ] First 30 seconds are written and memorized
- [ ] Narrative arc matches strategy type
- [ ] Pre-socialization is scheduled
- [ ] All charts are simple, not cluttered
- [ ] Strongest counterargument is addressed
- [ ] Next step is crystal clear
