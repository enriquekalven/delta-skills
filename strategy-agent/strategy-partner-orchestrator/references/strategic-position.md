# Strategic Position Assessment (10x Version)

## What Changed from the Original SWOT
The previous version added interaction analysis but still treated position as a snapshot. This 10/10 version adds quantified scoring, competitive benchmarking, erosion modeling, and time-series tracking. Advantages become measurable. Vulnerabilities get exploitation timelines. The interaction matrix produces sized strategic moves. Position migration paths turn assessment into roadmaps. You can now run a half-day workshop with this framework.

## When to Use
Use this when assessing current strategic position as input to strategic decisions. Always pair with Industry Analysis (macro context) and Competitive Landscape (specific competitor moves). Position feeds into Strategy Synthesis, which feeds into Operating Model design. Time-box each section: 30 min advantages, 30 min vulnerabilities, 20 min opportunities/threats, 30 min matrix, 10 min output.

## Step 1: Competitive Advantages (Quantified)

For each claimed advantage, apply the **Three Tests** (customer, differentiation, durability), then score on a **1-5 Advantage Capability Rubric**:

**Rubric (apply per advantage type):**
- **5:** Gap is 2-3x in our favor. Competitors would need 3+ years and major capital to match. Market recognizes this in buying behavior.
- **4:** Gap is 50%+ in our favor. Competitors would need 18-24 months to match. Customers actively prefer us for this.
- **3:** Gap is meaningful but closing. Competitors could match in 12-18 months. Customers notice but it's not the primary decision driver.
- **2:** Gap is narrow. Competitors could match in 6-12 months. Table stakes territory.
- **1:** Gap is minimal or non-existent. Table stakes; not actually an advantage.

For each scored advantage, add:
- **Benchmarking:** Specific competitor comparisons (e.g., "vs. Competitor X: 2.1x lower CAC, vs. Competitor Y: 1.3x faster delivery")
- **Erosion half-life:** Months until this advantage degrades by 50% if not reinvested (e.g., brand: 24 months, data: 12 months, process: 8 months)
- **Trajectory (3-year):** Score was [2.5 → 3.2 → 3.8] or [4.1 → 3.9 → 3.6]? Accelerating, stable, or declining?
- **Segment variation:** Advantage score by product/segment (e.g., Enterprise: 4.2, Mid-market: 3.5, SMB: 2.1)

## Step 2: Vulnerabilities (Timeline-Based)

Don't list every gap. Focus on vulnerabilities that could cause you to lose. For each, score exploitation difficulty:

**Exploitation Timeline Rubric:**
- **Immediate (0-3 months):** Competitor could build workaround or attack right now. Example: customer concentration in one vertical.
- **Near-term (3-12 months):** Requires moderate investment but no breakthrough innovation. Example: missing AI capability in a maturing market.
- **Medium-term (12-24 months):** Requires significant R&D or talent acquisition. Example: weak data infrastructure vs. data-dependent competitor.
- **Long-term (24+ months):** Structural fix needed; requires org rebuild. Example: cost structure fundamentally misaligned for competitive pricing.

For each vulnerability:
- **Exploitation likelihood:** [High/Medium/Low] — Is a specific competitor actively building an attack here?
- **Impact if exploited:** Revenue loss estimate, market share loss, capability gap widening
- **Fix cost:** Engineering effort (months), capital required, org changes needed
- **Segment exposure:** Which segments are most vulnerable? (e.g., vulnerability in segment X means 40% of revenue at risk)

## Step 3: Strategic Opportunities (Sized)

Identify windows — specific, time-bound situations where action now creates disproportionate value. For each opportunity:

- **Description:** Specific (not "AI" but "LLM-powered contract analysis for insurance underwriting")
- **Window duration:** Months until competitive saturation or market shift closes it
- **Right to win:** [High/Medium/Low] — Why us vs. others? Link to specific advantages.
- **Investment required:** [Capital, engineering months, hires, partnerships]
- **Expected return:** Revenue, margin, share gain, or strategic value (be specific)
- **Probability of success:** [H/M/L] with reasoning

## Step 4: Strategic Threats (Probability × Impact)

For each existential or material threat:

- **Description:** Specific competitive move, tech disruption, regulatory shift, or macro change
- **Probability × Impact:** Matrix:
  - **High prob × High impact:** Existential. This is what keeps you up at night.
  - **High prob × Medium impact:** Material. Significant resource allocation required.
  - **Medium prob × High impact:** Catastrophic if it hits. Build early warning and mitigation.
- **Timeline:** When could this hit? (0-6 months, 6-18 months, 18+ months)
- **Early warning signals:** What do we monitor to see this coming?
- **Mitigation:** What can we do to reduce probability or impact?

## Step 5: Segment-Level Position Mapping

Overall company position can hide segment-level crises. Create a simple 2x2 for each material segment (product, customer vertical, geography):

```
                      ADVANTAGE STRENGTH
                    High              Low
THREAT INTENSITY
    High        ⚠️ Monitor    🚨 Crisis
    Low         ✅ Defend     🤔 Develop
```

Example: "Enterprise segment: strong position (top-right), SMB segment: crisis (bottom-right)"

## Step 6: Position Migration Paths

Position assessment is only useful if it drives action. Map the path from current state to target state:

**Example:**
- **Current:** Score 3.2 in product, 2.8 in cost structure, 2.1 in data
- **Target (24 months):** Score 4.1 in product, 3.5 in cost structure, 3.8 in data
- **Path:** [Q1-Q2: +0.6 product via feature A+B, +0.4 cost via ops automation] → [Q3-Q4: +0.4 data via partnership, +0.3 cost via pricing model]
- **Resource:** 12 engineers, $2M capital, 1 hire (data scientist)

## Step 7: Strategic Interaction Matrix (Sized Moves)

Cross advantage, vulnerability, opportunity, threat to generate specific moves. For each move:

- **Specific action:** Not "deepen moat" but "expand integrations with [X] platforms to increase switching cost"
- **Sizing:** Investment (capital, FTE months), expected return (revenue, margin, risk reduction)
- **Timeline:** When should we start? When do we expect payoff?
- **Owner:** Who drives this?

**Four quadrants:**
- **Advantage + Opportunity:** Offensive moves. Invest heavily. Size expected market capture.
- **Advantage + Threat:** Defensive moves. Strengthen moat. Size investment as insurance.
- **Vulnerability + Opportunity:** Transformation moves. Only if opportunity is big enough to justify fix.
- **Vulnerability + Threat:** Danger zone. Urgent. Size as crisis response.

## Step 8: Integration Points

- **Strategy Synthesis:** Position inputs competitive strategy (cost leader, differentiator, niche). Position migration paths become strategy roadmaps.
- **Operating Model:** Sized moves determine org design, capability hires, capital allocation. Erosion timelines drive reinvestment schedules.
- **Hypothesis Engine:** Threat early warning signals become KPIs to track. Opportunity windows become experiment schedules.
