# Investment Thesis Framework

Every capital investment ≥ $[threshold] requires a written thesis that articulates: what we believe will happen, why we think we're right, what could go wrong, and what success looks like. This framework prevents fuzzy thinking, forces specificity, and creates accountability.

## Core Discipline: Written Thesis Required

**Why written?** Writing forces clarity. A vague idea sounds compelling until you try to write it down. Then you discover the gaps.

**Who writes it?** The person/team most accountable for the investment's success. Not the CFO. Not a staff analyst. The owner.

**When?** Before capital is committed. Not after.

---

## Investment Thesis Template

### 1. Investment Overview

**Name:** [Official project name]

**Type:** (Financial / Strategic / Both)
- **Financial:** Pure financial return. ROIC must exceed WACC-based hurdle rate. Success = returns at or above hurdle.
- **Strategic:** Strategic benefit is primary. Financial return is secondary. Success = strategic benefit delivered + ROIC > [strategic hurdle].
- **Both:** Financial returns AND strategic benefit. Both must be achieved.

**Total Capital Required:** $[M]
- Year 1: $[M]
- Year 2: $[M]
- Year 3: $[M]
- Total: $[M]

**Proposed Timeline:** [X years until mature / stable / breakeven]

**Proposed Hurdle Rate:** [X]%
- Rationale: [Why this rate? Industry, risk, company capital structure?]

**Who Owns This?** [Name, title, track record]

---

### 2. What Do We Believe? (The Core Hypothesis)

**One-sentence core belief:** [State the core hypothesis that the entire investment rests on]

**Examples:**
- "Low-cost manufacturers are consolidating this fragmented market; we can acquire 5-10 competitors in [X] years, achieve [Y]% combined margins, and exit at industry-median valuation."
- "Addiction to [technology] is creating [market opportunity]; if we build [product] 18 months before competitors, we can capture 15% market share and achieve 30% margins."
- "This acquisition's [capability] will reduce our [pain point], allowing us to increase prices by [X]% across our installed base, improving ROIC by [Y] percentage points."

**What specifically are we betting on?**
1. [Market belief] — specific assumption that must be true
2. [Company belief] — what we believe this investment can achieve
3. [Competitive belief] — what competitors will or won't do
4. [Timing belief] — when the payoff arrives
5. [Financial belief] — what unit economics will be

**Quantify each belief.** Not "we'll be a market leader" but "we'll capture 15% of a $[X]B market, achieving $[X]M revenue by Year 3."

**What's your probability estimate that the core hypothesis is right?** [X]%
- High conviction (80%+): Core belief is supported by data, track record, or first-mover advantage
- Medium conviction (60-79%): Core belief is reasonable but depends on execution or market timing
- Low conviction (40-59%): Core belief is speculative; this is a real option bet

---

### 3. Why Are We Right? (Evidence Base)

**What evidence supports this thesis?**

- **Data:** [What market data, customer feedback, or internal analysis proves the hypothesis?]
  - Example: "Customer interviews with [X] accounts show [Y]% would pay $[Z] for this solution"
  - Example: "Market research shows [segment] growing at [X]% CAGR; we have primary data from [Y] sources"

- **Track Record:** [Have you or your team done this before successfully?]
  - Example: "We grew [similar business] from $[X]M to $[Y]M in [timeframe] with similar unit economics"
  - Example: "Our operations team has consolidated [X] acquisitions with [Y]% synergy realization"

- **Comparative Advantage:** [Why are we better positioned than competitors to win this?]
  - Example: "Our [capability] is 18 months ahead of [competitor], giving us window to build switching costs"
  - Example: "We have relationships with [X]% of target customers; competitors don't"

- **Rational Decision-Making by Competitors:** [Why won't competitors crush this opportunity?]
  - Example: "Market is fragmented; consolidator needs [X] acquisitions to achieve scale, but valuations are [Y]% above our returns threshold"
  - Example: "This requires [capability] that only we have; competitors would need [years] to build"

- **Scenario Testing:** [Have you stress-tested the thesis against alternative scenarios?]
  - Example: "If market grows at [X]% instead of [Y]%, IRR declines to [Z]% — still above hurdle"
  - Example: "If we capture [X]% instead of [Y]% market share, payback extends to [Z] years — acceptable"

**What assumption do you least believe?** Name it. If this assumption is wrong, the thesis fails. How will you test it?

---

### 4. What Could Go Wrong? (Risks & Kill Criteria)

**Failure modes.** What could cause this investment to miss its target or fail entirely?

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Kill Trigger | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market adoption slower than forecast | Medium | High | If Year 2 adoption is <50% of plan | Run pilots with [X] customers by Month 6 to validate demand |
| Competitors respond faster | Low | High | If [competitor] launches same product within [X] months | Move up launch 2 months; build switching costs early |
| Cost structure exceeds forecast | Medium | Medium | If unit costs exceed budget by >15% | Lock vendor pricing; build contingency for [X]% cost overrun |
| Synergy realization (if M&A) | High | High | If synergies < 50% of plan | Retain management for [X] months post-close; fund integration team fully |
| Key talent leaves | Low | High | If [key person] departs in first [X] months | Offer retention bonus; have backup plan for role |

**Kill criteria:** Define the specific, measurable trigger that causes you to abandon the investment.

Examples:
- "If Month 6 customer acquisition cost exceeds $[X] (vs. planned $[Y]), kill the program"
- "If Year 2 market share is <5% (vs. planned 12%), initiate divestiture process"
- "If synergy realization by Month 18 is <$[X]M (vs. planned $[Y]M), execute exit"
- "If the core technology can't achieve [specification] by Month 12, kill and redeploy capital"

**Downside scenario:** If everything goes wrong, what's the worst-case financial outcome?
- Capital lost: $[M]
- Probability of worst case: [X]%
- Expected loss: $[M] × [X]%
- Can we afford it? (Should be <[X]% of total capital availability)

**Have you done a pre-mortem?** Assume it's 2 years from now and this investment completely failed. What happened? What warning signs did you ignore?

---

### 5. What Will Success Look Like? (Milestones & Success Metrics)

**Define the path to success with specific milestones:**

| Milestone | Timing | Success Metric | Owner | Review Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MVP shipped | Month 3 | Product meets [specification]; [X] beta users in test | [Owner] | Month 4 go/no-go |
| Customer validation | Month 6 | [X] pilot customers, NPS > [Y], willingness to pay > [Z]% of target | [Owner] | Month 7 scale/pivot/kill decision |
| Market traction | Month 12 | [X] paying customers; [Y]% of target Year 1 revenue | [Owner] | Month 13 investment decision |
| Unit economics proven | Month 18 | CAC < [X]; LTV > [Y]; payback < [Z] months | [Owner] | Month 19 scale/exit decision |
| Path to target state | Month 24 | Achieved [X]% of Year 5 revenue target; ROIC trajectory clear | [Owner] | Scale or exit |

**Leading indicators vs. lagging indicators:**

- **Lagging indicator:** ROIC (what ultimately matters, but comes last)
- **Leading indicators:** Customer acquisition cost, customer lifetime value, churn rate, market share growth, margin trajectory
- **Monitor leading indicators monthly or quarterly.** They predict ROIC performance 12-24 months forward.

**Success criteria for scaling:**
- We scale the investment if: All milestones hit AND leading indicators point to hurdle rate achievement
- We pivot if: Core hypothesis is invalidated but alternative path exists (different market, different model)
- We kill if: Kill trigger is hit OR core hypothesis is disproven

### 6. How Much Capital & What's the Return? (Financial Projection)

**5-Year Financial Model:**

| Year | Revenue | EBIT | ROIC | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $[M] | $(M) [loss] | (X)% | Heavy investment; no revenue yet |
| Year 2 | $[M] | $(M) [loss] | (X)% | Pilot phase; limited revenue |
| Year 3 | $[M] | $[M] | X% | Break even / inflection |
| Year 4 | $[M] | $[M] | X% | Ramp; approaching steady-state |
| Year 5 | $[M] | $[M] | X% | Steady-state ROIC |

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR):**
- [X]% (cumulative capital deployed, cash flows returned)
- Comparison: Hurdle rate is [Y]%. IRR exceeds hurdle by [Z] percentage points.

**Payback Period:**
- [X] years (time to recover all invested capital)
- Acceptable? [Yes / No]

**Sensitivity Analysis:**
- If revenue is 20% below forecast: ROIC = [X]%, IRR = [Y]% — still [above / below] hurdle?
- If margin is 200 bps below forecast: ROIC = [X]%, IRR = [Y]% — still [above / below] hurdle?
- If payback takes 1 extra year: IRR = [X]% — still [above / below] hurdle?

**Stress Test:** Under what scenario does this fail to meet the hurdle rate?
- Revenue needs to be [X]% below forecast, OR
- Margins need to be [Y] bps below forecast, OR
- Payback needs to extend by [Z] years
- Probability of this scenario: [Low / Medium / High]

---

### 7. How Does This Fit the Portfolio? (Strategic Context)

**Portfolio position:**
- Is this a core business extension? Adjacent? New market?
- Does it fill a gap or create overlap with existing investments?
- Is it additive (increases total portfolio value) or substitutive (replaces another investment)?

**Correlation analysis:**
- Are returns correlated with existing businesses? (Diversifying good; correlated bad)
- If this succeeds, do all other investments become more valuable? (synergy)
- If this fails, are other investments harmed? (dependency)

**Portfolio sequencing:**
- Can we do this AND our other planned investments? (Capital constraint check)
- Should we do this before or after [other investment]?
- Does this unlock or depend on [other investment]?

**Total portfolio stress test:**
- Current portfolio ROIC: [X]%
- If this investment succeeds at hurdle rate: Portfolio ROIC becomes [Y]%
- If this investment fails and capital is lost: Portfolio ROIC becomes [Z]%
- Is portfolio still healthy in failure scenario? (Should be)

## Special Case 1: Strategic Investment with Below-Hurdle Financial Returns

Some investments are strategic (they enable something bigger or block a competitor) and have financial returns below WACC.

**Framework for strategic investments:**

1. **Quantify the strategic benefit.** Not "this is strategically important" but "this enables us to increase prices by [X]% across [Y] customer base, creating $[M] value" or "this blocks [competitor] from [outcome], protecting $[M] in existing revenue."

2. **Set a financial hurdle.** Strategic investments should still earn WACC - 2% to - 3%, never below zero. If the financial loss is too severe, the "strategy" isn't real; it's empire building.

3. **Time-limit it.** "We'll invest in this for [X] years. If strategic benefit hasn't materialized, we exit." Don't let strategic investments drag on indefinitely.

4. **Measure the strategic benefit.** Track the actual strategic outcome. Did prices increase? Did we block the competitor? Did we capture the adjacent market? If not, kill it.

**Example:**
- Investment: Market entry in new geography
- Financial ROIC: 5% (below 8% WACC)
- Strategic benefit: Positions us for 3-5 year regional market consolidation worth $[X]M
- Hurdle: 5% financial ROIC + [X]% strategic return = [Y]% total expected return ≈ WACC
- Discipline: Review market consolidation progress at Year 2. If not materializing, exit.

## Special Case 2: M&A Investment Thesis

**For acquisitions, the thesis is broader:**

- **Strategic rationale:** What do we get from this company that we can't build faster/cheaper?
  - Customer relationships (TAM expansion, customer lifetime value increase)
  - Capabilities or IP (cost reduction, product innovation, market entry)
  - Cost synergies (consolidation, procurement, overhead elimination)
  - Market share (competitive positioning, pricing power)

- **Synergy thesis:** What will we do differently post-close?
  - Specify cost synergies (overlap elimination, savings amount, timeline to realization)
  - Specify revenue synergies (new customer access, cross-sell, pricing increases)
  - Realistic synergy realization: typical is 50-70% of identified, 18-36 months

- **Valuation discipline:** Is price reasonable?
  - Standalone valuation: What is the acquired company worth independent of synergies?
  - Total valuation: Standalone + synergy value
  - Price ceiling: We pay no more than [X]% of synergy value. The seller captures the rest.
  - Comparison: Is [X]x multiple reasonable vs. [industry median]?

- **Deal risks:** Integration complexity, talent retention, customer disruption, cultural fit
  - How will you mitigate each?

## Working Example: SaaS Expansion Thesis

**Investment Name:** European Market Expansion

**Type:** Strategic (market entry) + Financial (profit growth)

**Total Capital:** $2.5M
- Year 1: $1M (hiring, localization, ops setup)
- Year 2: $1M (sales, marketing, support scaling)
- Year 3: $0.5M (contingency)

**Hurdle Rate:** 10% (WACC 8% + 2% strategic premium)

---

**What do we believe?**
- European SaaS market is [X]% smaller than North America but growing [Y]% faster
- Our product has strong demand signals from European prospects (81% of requests are EMEA-based, but <10% current revenue)
- Competitors in Europe are fragmented; no dominant player; consolidation opportunity in 3-5 years
- We can enter with [X] sales hires, localized product, [Y] language support, and capture 5% market share by Year 5
- At 5% market share, European business will be $[M] revenue at [Z]% margins (consistent with North America unit economics)

**Why are we right?**
- Track record: Entered Canada market with similar model; achieved 8% market share in 36 months
- Customer validation: Surveyed [X] European prospects; [Y]% committed to pilot if we offered GDPR-compliant SaaS
- Market structure: Fragmented European market with pricing 20% below North America (room to grow into); TAM is $[B]
- Competitive advantage: We have [feature] that European competitors don't; 12-month window before replication

**What could go wrong?** (kill triggers in bold)

| Risk | Mitigation | Kill Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is higher in EMEA than NOAM | Run pilots with 5 target customers by Month 6 | If CAC > 120% of NOAM by Month 12 |
| GDPR compliance more expensive than forecasted | Budget $[X] for compliance; lock vendor pricing | If compliance costs exceed $[X] |
| Churn rate higher than NOAM (regulatory, language, service expectations) | Hire local support; build language pack properly | If churn > 5% monthly by Month 12 |
| Product-market fit is weaker than NOAM | Customize for Europe; use early customers as reference | If first 10 customers NPS < 40 |

**Milestones:**
- Month 6: Sales hire onboarded; 5 pilot customers in production; product localized (German, French)
- Month 12: $[M] annual contract value (ACV) signed; [X] customers in production; monthly churn < 4%
- Month 24: $[M] ARR; path to $[M] ARR clear; ROIC positive
- Month 36: $[M] ARR; profitable; ROIC ≥ 10%

**Financial Model:**

| Year | Revenue | EBIT | Invested Capital | ROIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $0.2M | $(0.9M) | $1M | (90%) |
| 2 | $1.2M | $(0.3M) | $2M | (15%) |
| 3 | $3.5M | $0.7M | $2.5M | 28% |
| 4 | $6M | $2M | $2.5M | 80% |
| 5 | $8M | $3.2M | $2.5M | 128% |

**Sensitivity:**
- If customer ACV is 20% below forecast: Year 5 ROIC = 92% (still strong, still above hurdle)
- If CAC is 30% higher: Year 5 ROIC = 68% (above hurdle, acceptable)
- If payback extends to Year 4: 5-year ROIC still 40%+ (above hurdle)

**Strategic fit:**
- Increases addressable market by $[B]
- Blocks competitor from using Europe as beachhead to attack North America
- Supports product development roadmap (GDPR work benefits all regions)
- No overlap or cannibalization with existing business

**Decision:** APPROVE. All milestones clear, sensitivity is robust, strategic rationale is sound, team has execution track record.

## Investment Thesis Review Checklist

- [ ] Core hypothesis is stated in one clear sentence
- [ ] Probability estimate of core hypothesis is quantified
- [ ] Evidence base includes data, track record, OR unique advantage
- [ ] Kill triggers are specific and measurable (not subjective)
- [ ] Worst-case financial loss is quantified and acceptable
- [ ] 5-year model includes revenue, EBIT, ROIC; IRR > hurdle
- [ ] Sensitivity analysis shows investment survives 20-30% variance
- [ ] Milestones are specific, measurable, owned by named person
- [ ] Leading indicators (not just revenue) are monitored monthly
- [ ] Strategic benefit is quantified (if strategic investment)
- [ ] Financial hurdle for strategic investment is ≥ WACC - 3%
- [ ] Time limit for strategic investment is defined
- [ ] Portfolio context assessed (synergies, dependencies, sequencing)
- [ ] Owner and accountable team are named
- [ ] Thesis is written before capital commitment
- [ ] Thesis is reviewed by Capital Allocation Committee or equivalent
