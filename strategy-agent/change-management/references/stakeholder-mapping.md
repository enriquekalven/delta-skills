# Stakeholder Mapping & Resistance Forecast

Map every stakeholder using Power/Interest Matrix. Assess resistance patterns.

## Power / Interest Matrix

```
               High Power
                   |
        Champions  |  Kingmakers
                   |  (manage closely)
    Low Interest --- | --- High Interest
        Bystanders  |  Critical Mass
                   |  (build coalition)
               Low Power
```

### KINGMAKERS (High Power, High Interest)
**Your priority:** Manage closely. These are your executive sponsors.

**Engagement strategy:**
- Weekly 1:1s with change lead
- Weekly steering committee meetings
- Direct accountability for coalition alignment
- Remove blockers for them
- Celebrate their leadership

### CRITICAL MASS (Low Power, High Interest)
**Your priority:** Build coalition. These are potential change champions.

**Engagement strategy:**
- Monthly change ambassador forums
- Activate as peer influencers
- Provide training + support
- Celebrate their leadership
- Use them to influence others

### CHAMPIONS (Various power levels with high engagement)
**Your priority:** Empower and sustain.

**Engagement strategy:**
- Bi-weekly town halls with direct participation
- Recognition program (monthly)
- Resource and support
- Monitor for burnout
- Rotate roles to prevent hero syndrome

### BYSTANDERS (High Power, Low Interest)
**Your priority:** Keep engaged. Avoid alienation.

**Engagement strategy:**
- Monthly updates (not too frequent)
- Include in decisions that affect them
- Signal that change is their priority (or isn't)
- Escalate to if they become resisters

## Resistance Patterns & Mitigation

Map resistance by type and severity.

| Resistance Type | Indicators | Mitigation Playbook |
|---|---|---|
| **Loss Aversion** | "We'll lose authority/budget/team" | Show what's preserved; offer new role; compensation if warranted |
| **Capability Anxiety** | "I can't do this new thing" | Intensive training + peer support + time + celebration of learning |
| **Misaligned Incentives** | Bonus tied to old metrics; new role perceived as demotion | Redesign incentives to reward new behaviors; transparent comp conversation |
| **Unclear Change Logic** | "Why are we doing this? It doesn't make sense" | Repeated, data-driven narrative; leadership modeling; Q&A forum |
| **Pace Rejection** | "This is too fast; I'm overwhelmed" | Break change into phases; extend timeline if possible; reduce concurrent changes |
| **Trust Deficit** | "Leadership promised before and failed" | Small wins early; deliver on promises; transparent communication on delays |
| **Status Quo Entrenchment** | "Current way works fine; why fix it?" | Quantified problem statement; competitive benchmarking; cost of inaction |
| **Identity Threat** | "This change makes my expertise obsolete" | Reframe expertise as foundation for new way; highlight evolution not replacement |

## Mitigation Strategy by Resistance Type

**For Loss Aversion:**
1. Mapping exercise: What does each person lose? What do they keep?
2. Conversation: "Here's what you'll keep. Here's the new role. Here's how we're supporting you."
3. New opportunity: How does the new way create opportunity for them?

**For Capability Anxiety:**
1. Training assessment: What skills do they need?
2. Intensive training: Not one-off, but ongoing support
3. Peer coaches: Assign experienced peer to help them learn
4. Safe practice: Time to practice before going live
5. Celebrate learning: Make experimentation safe; celebrate mistakes as learning

**For Misaligned Incentives:**
1. Audit current incentives: What are they rewarding?
2. Redesign to align: Tie incentives to new behaviors
3. Transparent conversation: Explain the logic, discuss impact on comp
4. Gradual transition: Phase in new incentives over time

**For Unclear Change Logic:**
1. Build the case: Quantified problem, competitive benchmarking, cost of inaction
2. Repeat message: 7-9 touchpoints in first 90 days (email, town hall, 1:1, team meetings, etc.)
3. Leadership modeling: Leaders explicitly model new behaviors
4. Q&A forum: Create space for people to ask "why?" and get answers

## Building Your Stakeholder Map

1. **Identify all stakeholder groups** (by function, level, tenure)
2. **Assess power** (Do they have decision-making authority? Budget control? Influence?)
3. **Assess interest** (How much does change affect them? Do they care?)
4. **Position on matrix** (Kingmaker / Bystander / Champion / etc.)
5. **Assess resistance pattern** (Why might they resist? What worries them?)
6. **Design engagement strategy** (What do they need to hear? Through what channel? How often?)
7. **Identify influencers for and against** (Who can convince others? Who might convince others to resist?)
8. **Create mitigation playbook** (For each high-risk group, what's our approach?)
