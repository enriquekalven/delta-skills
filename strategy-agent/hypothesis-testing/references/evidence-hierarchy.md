# Evidence Hierarchy & Confidence Building

Not all evidence is equal. Build confidence progressively through a hierarchy of evidence.

## The Evidence Hierarchy

| Level | Definition | Confidence Gain | Timeline | Cost | When to Use |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|----------|------|-------------|
| **Anecdote** | 2-3 customer conversations, pattern emerges | 20-30% → 40% | Days | $1-2K | Customer need validation, qualitative signal |
| **Survey** | 20+ respondents, structured questions, quantified % | 40% → 60% | 1-2 weeks | $3-5K | Customer willingness, segment sizing |
| **Experiment / MVP** | Functional prototype, 50+ users, metric measured | 60% → 75-80% | 2-8 weeks | $10-50K | Product feature, pricing, messaging |
| **Market Data** | Third-party data, benchmarks, competitive intel | 75% → 85% | Real-time | $2-10K | Market size, adoption rates, CAC benchmarks |
| **Validated at Scale** | Production data, 1000+ users, metric sustained | 85% → 95%+ | Weeks to months | $100K+ | Confirmation, go-to-market proof |

## Key Principles

### 1. Pick the Minimum Evidence Level That Moves the Needle

For each hypothesis, determine: "What evidence level would move us from current confidence to 70%+ (action-ready)?"

Don't oversample. If anecdotes move you from 30% to 50% (and you're trying to reach 70%), survey is the right next step—not a full product build.

### 2. Confidence Compounds, but Evidence Doesn't Reset

Once you have anecdotal evidence (40% confidence), the next evidence layer (survey) starts at 40%, not 0%. You're building, not replacing.

Each new evidence source either reinforces or challenges prior evidence. Track both.

### 3. Multiple Sources = Higher Confidence

- 1 source saying "customers will pay $50K": 60% confidence
- 2 independent sources saying same: 75% confidence
- 3+ sources saying same: 85% confidence

### 4. Contrary Evidence Matters

If 80% of customers say "yes" but 20% say "no" with strong reasoning, that 20% might matter. Capture why they said no—it's often more valuable than the yeses.

## Bayesian Updating in Practice

```
Prior confidence: 30% (based on initial assumption)

Evidence 1 (Anecdotes): 8/10 customers say they'd buy → Confidence 30% → 45%
Evidence 2 (Survey): 12/50 would actually pay $50K → Confidence 45% → 55%
Evidence 3 (MVP): 30% Day-7 retention (target was 40%) → Confidence 55% → 50%

Current confidence: 50% (mixed signals; need to iterate on value prop)
```

## Decision Points by Confidence Level

| Confidence Range | Hypothesis Status | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **≥ 80%** | SUPPORTED | Proceed with confidence. Document assumption as validated. Plan scale. |
| **60-80%** | PARTIALLY SUPPORTED | Proceed but build contingencies. Run targeted tests on remaining doubts. Monitor early indicators. |
| **40-60%** | UNCERTAIN | Value prop or customer segment needs rework. Redesign based on learnings. Retest. |
| **< 40%** | NOT SUPPORTED | This hypothesis is unlikely to be true. Reallocate resources. Document learning. |

## Building Your Evidence Plan

For each strategic hypothesis:

1. What's your prior confidence? (What do you believe before testing?)
2. What evidence level would move you to 70%+? (What's the minimum viable evidence?)
3. What's the hypothesis? (What specifically are you testing?)
4. What's success? (What result would move confidence to 70%+?)
5. What's failure? (What result would lower confidence?)
6. What confounds could affect the test? (What else could explain the result?)
