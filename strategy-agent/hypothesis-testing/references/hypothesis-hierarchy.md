# Hypothesis Hierarchy Framework

All strategy assumptions organize into three levels, each with different risk, timeline, and cost.

## The Three Levels

### STRATEGIC HYPOTHESES (Biggest risk, longest timeline, highest cost)

These are your core bets. If wrong, the strategy fails.

```
├─ Market hypothesis: Market exists at [size], growing [rate], has budget for [solution]
├─ Product hypothesis: Customer will [use/buy] solution that does [core value prop]
└─ Business model hypothesis: At [price], with [GTM], we achieve [unit economics]
```

### OPERATIONAL HYPOTHESES (Medium risk, medium timeline, medium cost)

These optimize how you execute the strategic bet.

```
├─ Pricing hypothesis: Customer perceives value at $[price]; elasticity [range]
├─ Feature hypothesis: [Feature] drives [metric] by [%] among [segment]
├─ Channel hypothesis: [Channel] acquires customers at $[CAC] with [payback]
└─ Retention hypothesis: [Cohort] retains at [%], driven by [core behavior]
```

### TACTICAL HYPOTHESES (Lowest risk, shortest timeline, lowest cost)

These optimize execution details.

```
├─ Messaging hypothesis: [Message] resonates with [segment] on [problem]
├─ UI hypothesis: [Design change] improves [metric] by [%]
└─ Sequencing hypothesis: [Step 1 then 2] works better than [1 then 3]
```

## Using the Hierarchy

1. **Always extract all three levels** when analyzing strategy. This prevents missing critical assumptions.
2. **Test strategic hypotheses first.** They have highest risk and longest timeline. Surface them early.
3. **Test operational hypotheses second.** Once strategic bets are validated, prove you can execute.
4. **Test tactical hypotheses last.** These are optimizations; worth testing only if strategic/operational are solid.
5. **Track confidence at each level.** If strategic hypothesis confidence drops, cascading failures follow.

## Example Application

**Strategy:** "Become the #1 AI-native email platform for enterprise"

**Strategic Hypotheses:**
- Market: Enterprise market for AI-email solutions is $5B+, growing 40% CAGR
- Product: Enterprises will adopt AI-native email that learns from their workflows (requires daily use)
- Business Model: Enterprises will pay $50/user/year; CAC $200, LTV $3,000

**Operational Hypotheses:**
- Pricing: Price elasticity is favorable at $50/user; competitors at $40-60 range
- Feature: AI learning from workflows drives 50%+ engagement increase vs. baseline
- Channel: Sales channel can acquire enterprise customers at $200 CAC with 18-month payback
- Retention: Annual retention >85% once customers build AI training history

**Tactical Hypotheses:**
- Messaging: "AI that learns your email habits" resonates better than "AI-powered email platform"
- UI: Dark mode for email client increases engagement by 5%
- Sequencing: "Complete profile → AI setup → First learning" flow converts better than alternatives

**Test sequence:** Test strategic in Q1, operational in Q2, tactical in Q3-Q4.
