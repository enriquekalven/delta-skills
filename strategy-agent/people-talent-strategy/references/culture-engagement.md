# Culture & Engagement Strategy

Culture is not ping-pong tables and free snacks. Culture is the operating system of your organization: how decisions are made, how people treat each other, what's celebrated and what's punished, how you talk about the customer, how you handle failure, what integrity looks like. It shapes behavior far more than incentives do. And it's the single biggest driver of retention and engagement — more than compensation or title or growth opportunity.

---

## Part 1: Culture Audit Methodology

Before designing a culture strategy, diagnose the current state. What culture actually exists (not what you think exists)?

### Culture Audit Framework

```
CULTURE AUDIT: THE SEVEN DIMENSIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. VALUES & NORMS
   What actually matters here? (Not the posted values, but the lived ones.)

   Audit Method:
     • Ask 15-20 people: "What's one value we live by?" and "What's one value
       we say we have but don't live?" Write down patterns.
     • Observe: What gets people fired? What gets people promoted?
       That reveals actual values.
     • Decision-making: What matters in important decisions?
       E.g., "We say customer-first, but we prioritize revenue over customer issues" →
       Real value is revenue, not customer-first

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ Values are clear and consistent (same 3-5 values mentioned by multiple people)
     ☐ Values are lived (people can point to decisions that reflect them)
     ☐ Values are reinforced (hiring, promotion, feedback all align with values)
     ☐ Values are challenging (they stretch people, not just comfortable norms)

2. DECISION-MAKING
   How fast do things get decided? Who decides? How transparent is the process?

   Audit Method:
     • Track 3-5 recent important decisions. Who decided? How long did it take?
       Were there hidden agendas or surprise reversals?
     • Ask: "When you disagree with a decision, can you voice it? What happens?"
     • Observe: Do people go around the chain or work within it?

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ Decisions are made at the right level (not all at CEO, not waiting for consensus)
     ☐ Process is transparent (people know how decisions are made)
     ☐ Disagreement is welcome (people can push back)
     ☐ Speed is appropriate (not paralyzed by process, not reckless)

3. PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY
   Can people be themselves? Can they admit mistakes? Can they ask for help?

   Audit Method:
     • One-on-ones: "Have you ever failed here? What happened? Did you feel safe
       admitting it?" Listen for fear or openness.
     • Observe: Do people admit what they don't know or do they BS?
       Do people jump on mistakes or help fix them?
     • Manager behavior: Do they admit their own mistakes and what they're learning?

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ People admit mistakes without fear of punishment
     ☐ Asking "I don't know" is OK
     ☐ Giving and receiving feedback is normal
     ☐ People volunteer ideas even if they might be wrong

4. COLLABORATION & SILOS
   Do functions work together or in isolation? Can product talk to engineering?
   Does sales tell the truth to customer success?

   Audit Method:
     • Map 3 major projects. How many handoffs happened? How much time was wasted
       due to miscommunication or silos?
     • Ask: "Who do you NOT work well with?" Look for patterns of broken bridges.
     • Observe: Do people eat lunch together across functions?
       Do they attend each other's meetings?

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ Cross-functional work is normal (not a special project)
     ☐ Information flows between functions
     ☐ People respect adjacent functions' constraints
     ☐ Shared success metrics (not protecting own function's metrics)

5. EMPLOYEE VOICE
   Can people influence decisions? Do they feel heard? Do they believe their voice
   matters?

   Audit Method:
     • Ask: "Name one time you influenced a decision here." If few can, voice is weak.
     • Anonymous pulse survey: "Do you feel heard by leadership?" (Anonymity matters.)
     • Observe: Do town halls have real Q&A or scripted answers?
       Do leaders change course based on employee input?

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ All-hands Q&A is real (not rehearsed)
     ☐ Employees have forums to influence decisions
     ☐ Leadership visibly acts on feedback
     ☐ Hierarchy is low-power (title doesn't override ideas)

6. GROWTH & DEVELOPMENT
   Do people have clear growth paths? Are they learning?
   Can they see themselves progressing here?

   Audit Method:
     • Ask each manager: "Does each of your direct reports have a growth plan?"
       Record: Yes with clarity / Yes vague / No.
     • Track: How many people moved to new roles in last 12 months?
     • Ask employees: "Do you know what it takes to get to the next level?"

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ Career paths are clear (not mysterious)
     ☐ Growth happens frequently (promotions, stretch assignments)
     ☐ Learning is accessible (training, mentoring, time)
     ☐ Growth is merit-based (not politics or tenure)

7. BELONGING
   Do people feel they belong here? Can they be themselves?
   Do they feel accepted and valued, not just tolerated?

   Audit Method:
     • Observe: Look at photos of your leadership team, all-hands, town halls.
       Who's visible? Who's missing?
     • Anonymous survey: "I feel I can be myself at work" / "I feel people like
       working with me" / "I have friends at work" (1-5 scale)
     • One-on-ones: "Do you feel like you belong here?" Listen for warmth
       or detachment.

   Scoring (rate 1-5):
     ☐ Leadership reflects employee diversity
     ☐ Employees socialize across groups
     ☐ Underrepresented people report belonging
     ☐ People want to work here (not just collecting a paycheck)

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

OVERALL CULTURE HEALTH SCORE: [Sum all 7 dimensions]
  28-35: Healthy culture, clear strengths and some areas to improve
  21-27: Mixed culture, significant strengths and weaknesses
  14-20: Weak culture, needs major work
  7-13: Broken culture, high risk of turnover and disengagement
```

## Part 2: Values Operationalization

Saying you value "customer obsession" means nothing. Living it means every decision, conversation, and hiring choice reflects it. Values operationalization is about embedding values into systems.

### From Values to Behaviors

```
OPERATIONALIZING A VALUE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

VALUE: "Customer Obsession"

What this means:
  • We talk to customers before deciding what to build
  • We prioritize customer needs over internal convenience
  • We measure success by customer outcomes, not features shipped
  • We admit when we're wrong about a customer need

OPERATIONALIZING IN HIRING
  Interview question: "Tell me about a time you prioritized a customer's
  need over your own goals. What did that require?"
  Scoring rubric:
    • Score 3+: Specific example, took risk for customer, learned something
    • Score 2: Generic answer about customer focus
    • Score 1-: No specific example or prioritizes self over customer

OPERATIONALIZING IN ONBOARDING
  Week 1: New hire goes on 3 customer calls with sales/CS. Must talk to
  at least 2 customers before first day of work.

  Week 1 assignment: "What did you learn from customers that surprised you?"
  Write it up, share with team.

OPERATIONALIZING IN DECISION-MAKING
  Key decision process: Before major product/engineering decisions,
  product manager must:
    1. Talk to 5+ customers
    2. Get feedback from 2+ support/CS people
    3. Present customer perspective in decision meeting
    4. If decision goes against customer input, explain why publicly

  If VP product regularly makes customer-hostile decisions, they're
  not living the value and need coaching or removal.

OPERATIONALIZING IN PROMOTION
  Promotion rubric: One criterion for engineering manager promotion is
  "Demonstrates customer empathy: regularly attends customer calls,
  influences decisions based on customer needs."

  Promotion denied for: "Great engineer but never talks to customers
  and doesn't think customer problems matter."

OPERATIONALIZING IN COMPENSATION
  Company OKR tied to customer NPS. If NPS drops, company bonus pool
  doesn't pay out, even if revenue hit. (Aligns everyone to customer
  outcomes, not just revenue.)

OPERATIONALIZING IN FEEDBACK
  In 1:1 reviews and feedback, manager specifically calls out:
    ✓ "You made a customer-friendly choice that was inconvenient for us. That's living customer obsession."
    ✗ "You optimized for engineering convenience at customer expense. Let's fix that."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

Repeat this for each core value. If values aren't in hiring, onboarding, promotion, and feedback, they're not values — they're wall art.

## Part 3: Engagement Measurement & Drivers

### Engagement Survey Design

Don't use the standard "Do you like your job?" survey. Use a diagnostic survey that reveals what actually drives engagement.

```
DIAGNOSTIC ENGAGEMENT SURVEY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Administration: Anonymous, 10-15 min, response target >80%

CORE QUESTIONS (1-5 scale, with "not applicable" option):

1. CLARITY & DIRECTION
   ☐ I understand how my work contributes to company success
   ☐ I have clear goals for the year
   ☐ I know what success looks like in my role

2. GROWTH
   ☐ I am developing skills in areas important to me
   ☐ I can see a clear path to my next role
   ☐ I have access to learning opportunities (mentoring, training, stretch projects)

3. MANAGER EFFECTIVENESS
   ☐ My manager gives me clear feedback
   ☐ My manager cares about my growth
   ☐ My manager advocates for me
   ☐ I trust my manager

4. PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY
   ☐ I can admit mistakes without fear
   ☐ I feel safe taking reasonable risks
   ☐ I can disagree with my manager/colleagues

5. COMPENSATION & BENEFITS
   ☐ My compensation is fair for my role and market
   ☐ Benefits meet my needs
   ☐ I understand how compensation is decided

6. BELONGING
   ☐ I feel I belong at this company
   ☐ I can be myself at work
   ☐ I have friendships at work (not just work relationships)

7. WORK-LIFE BALANCE
   ☐ I can maintain healthy work-life balance
   ☐ Workload is sustainable
   ☐ Flexibility is available when needed

8. ORGANIZATIONAL HEALTH
   ☐ Leadership is trustworthy
   ☐ Values are lived, not just posted
   ☐ I would recommend this company as a place to work

FOLLOW-UP: For scores <3, open-ended:
  "What would help improve this for you?"
  (Gives you solutions, not just problems)

ANALYSIS:
  • Overall engagement score: Average of questions (1-5 scale)
  • Segment by: department, level, tenure, remote/in-office, demographics
  • Find largest gaps: Which dimensions have lowest scores?
  • Identify risk: Which people/departments are most disengaged?
  • Trend: Compare year-over-year
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Top Engagement Drivers

Research consistently shows engagement is driven by (in order):

| Driver | What It Looks Like | What Breaks It |
|--------|---|---|
| **Growth opportunity** | Clear path to next role, learning, stretch assignments | No progression, no learning, stuck in same role |
| **Manager quality** | Manager cares about growth, gives feedback, advocates | Indifferent manager, no feedback, political |
| **Psychological safety** | Can admit mistakes, ask for help, disagree | Fear of retaliation, mistakes = punishment |
| **Clarity** | Know what matters, how work contributes, what success looks like | Conflicting goals, unclear priorities, invisible impact |
| **Belonging** | Feel accepted, can be yourself, have friendships | Feel tolerated, have to pretend, isolated |
| **Fair compensation** | Paid at market for role/level | Significantly below market for role |

## Part 4: Culture Change Playbook

Changing culture is slow and hard. Most culture change initiatives fail because companies underestimate the effort or expect change top-down without reinforcement.

### Pre-Change Assessment

```
CULTURE CHANGE READINESS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Question: "Are we ready for culture change?"

YES indicators:
  ✓ CEO visibly committed (not just words; they're in the work)
  ✓ Board of directors understands culture change takes 12-24 months
  ✓ We're willing to exit misaligned people (even if talented)
  ✓ Budget is allocated (hiring great managers, training, tools)
  ✓ We've done a real audit (not assumed what culture is)
  ✓ Leadership team alignment (not arguing about what matters)

RED FLAGS:
  ✗ CEO wants culture change in 90 days (not realistic)
  ✗ No budget allocated
  ✗ "We can't exit bad cultural fits, we need them"
  ✗ Leadership team disagrees on values
  ✗ No audit — guessing at what's wrong

If you have 3+ red flags, don't attempt culture change yet.
Fix the foundation first.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Culture Change Sequence (12-24 months)

**Phase 1: Diagnose & Align (Months 1-2)**
- Run culture audit
- Leadership offsite: What does good culture look like?
- Define 3-4 core values
- Identify 1-2 behaviors to fix (don't try to change everything)

**Phase 2: Operationalize (Months 3-6)**
- Embed values into hiring (training interviewers, update questions)
- Embed values into onboarding (first experience of company culture)
- Embed values into feedback/performance management
- Embed values into promotions
- Manager training: How to reinforce values in 1:1s and feedback

**Phase 3: Reinforce (Months 7-18)**
- Monthly all-hands: Highlight values-aligned behaviors
- Quarterly surveys: Track engagement improvement
- Manager 1:1s: "How are we living [value] this quarter?"
- Exit interviews: Ask why people leave (often reveals broken values)
- Reward compliance: Promote managers who build culture you want;
  exit managers who don't

**Phase 4: Embed & Sustain (Months 19-24)**
- Culture is "the way we do things"
- New hires feel it immediately
- Fewer reminders needed; culture is self-reinforcing
- Continue hiring/exiting for fit
- Quarterly culture audit: Are values still lived?

### Red Flags in Culture Change

**Culture change is stalling if:**
- Leadership talks about values but decisions contradict them
- You hire someone who's misaligned but talented (they pollute culture)
- Manager coaching isn't happening (words without reinforcement)
- Old behaviors are still celebrated

**When to abort:**
- CEO loses commitment
- Board doesn't support the timeline
- You discover you can't exit key misaligned people (they have political protection)
- After 18 months, engagement hasn't improved

## Part 5: Hybrid & Remote Culture

Culture at distance is harder. But it's not impossible if you're intentional.

### Remote Culture Design

**What changes:**
- You can't rely on "vibes" or water cooler conversations
- Intentionality matters more (everything must be explicit)
- Time zones complicate synchronous collaboration
- Loneliness is a real risk (people feel isolated)

**What works:**
- **Async communication defaults** — Assume people are in different zones. Document decisions, rationale in writing. Let people contribute async.
- **Regular video sync** — Weekly all-hands (recorded for async people). Team meetings are synchronous.
- **Virtual offices** — Tools like Gather, Slack huddles, video drop-ins. People can "sit together" without meetings.
- **Intentional off-sites** — 2x/year in-person. Use for strategy, bonding, informal connection.
- **Social connection** — Virtual coffee matches, team game nights, Slack channels for non-work interests.
- **Explicit check-ins on isolation** — In 1:1s, ask: "Do you feel connected to the team? Do you have someone you can grab coffee with?" If answer is no, help them build relationships.

**Red flags in remote culture:**
- People only interact in meetings (no spontaneous collaboration)
- Time zones create "primary" and "secondary" people
- Managers don't 1:1 regularly (can't gauge engagement at distance)
- No psychological safety feedback from isolated people

## Quality Gates for Culture & Engagement Strategy

**Gate 1: Audit Quality** — Is the audit real (multiple data sources) or guessing? If you haven't interviewed people, observed behavior, and looked at data (turnover, promotion patterns), you don't have a real diagnosis.

**Gate 2: Values Clarity** — Can you state each value in one sentence? Can you give 3 examples of what it looks like in action? If values are vague, operationalization will fail.

**Gate 3: Operationalization Specificity** — For each value, is it embedded in hiring, onboarding, feedback, promotion? Or are you just hoping culture will improve? Operationalization is the lever.

**Gate 4: Leadership Alignment** — Is the leadership team aligned on what culture you want? If they disagree, culture change will fail. Get alignment before rolling out.

**Gate 5: Sustainability** — Is this a one-time initiative or an ongoing system? Culture change requires sustained reinforcement (12-24 months minimum). If you're not prepared for the long game, don't start.
