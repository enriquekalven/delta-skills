# Workforce Planning & Capability Gap Analysis

The foundation of people strategy is understanding what capabilities you need vs. what you have. Workforce planning translates business strategy into headcount, skills, and capability requirements. It identifies gaps — where you're under-resourced, misaligned, or exposed. It surfaces build vs. buy vs. borrow decisions early, before you're in crisis hiring mode.

---

## Part 1: The Business-to-Capability Bridge

Before building a workforce plan, you must answer: What does the business require?

### Step 1: Extract Capability Requirements from Business Strategy

Start with the strategic imperatives:
- **What drives revenue?** (Sales, product, operations, customer success?)
- **What must we be world-class at?** (What's the asymmetric advantage?)
- **What's table-stakes?** (What do we need to be competent at just to play?)
- **What are the critical dependencies?** (Where do things break if we get it wrong?)
- **What's the growth plan?** (New markets? New products? New channels?)

For each, identify the functional capability required and the talent density (how many skilled people per unit of work).

**Example:** A B2B SaaS company has:
- Core capability: Product development (world-class) — needs high talent density
- Core capability: Sales (world-class) — needs high talent density
- Table-stakes: Operations, finance — needs competent but lower talent density
- Critical dependency: Early customer insights (inform product) — needs tight integration

Level definitions:
- **L1 (Individual Contributor):** Early career, execution-focused, learning rapidly
- **L2 (Senior IC):** Experienced, delivers independently, mentors juniors
- **L3 (Lead/Manager):** Manages people or major initiatives, sets strategy within domain
- **L4 (Director+):** Owns functional area, sets strategy, interfaces with exec team

### Step 3: Estimate Talent Density by Role Family

Talent density = how many highly-skilled people you need to deliver the capability.

| Role Family | Business Impact | Talent Density | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engineering** | Revenue-critical, years to replace | Very High | Hire A-players, heavy training, retention focus |
| **Product** | Strategic, hard to scale | Very High | Hire experienced PMs, long ramp, deep domain knowledge |
| **Sales** | Direct revenue | High | Hire proven reps, rapid scaling possible, high churn tolerance |
| **CS** | Retention/expansion | Medium | Mix of experienced + junior, teachable model |
| **Finance** | Essential but not differentiating | Medium | Hire competent, can be more junior, teachable |
| **Operations** | Enabling, scales with process | Low-Medium | Junior can scale with good process, lower talent bar |

High-density roles demand better hiring, higher comp, more mentoring, careful retention.
Low-density roles can scale with junior talent and clear processes.

## Part 3: Gap Analysis & Headcount Planning

### Build vs. Buy vs. Borrow Decision Framework

For each capability gap, decide: Do we hire (Build), contract (Buy), or partner (Borrow)?

```
BUILD vs. BUY vs. BORROW DECISION MATRIX
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DECISION FACTORS

**BUILD (Hire Internal)** — When:
  ✓ Capability is core to competitive advantage (e.g., engineering in a tech company)
  ✓ Long-term sustained need (3+ years)
  ✓ Talent market has viable candidates
  ✓ Company can invest in training/ramp
  ✓ Headcount budget allows
  Cost: Higher upfront, lower ongoing. Requires management overhead.
  Examples: VP Sales, Senior Engineers, Product Managers for core platform

**BUY (Contractor/Consultant)** — When:
  ✓ Capability is needed but not core (e.g., HR consulting, legal drafting)
  ✓ Short-term need or uncertain duration
  ✓ High specialization (e.g., patent attorney, sales trainer)
  ✓ Budget allows for higher hourly/project cost
  ✓ Low management overhead needed (project-based)
  Cost: Higher per-unit, but flexible and temporary.
  Examples: Legal counsel (contract), executive coach, specialized consultant

**BORROW (Partnership)** — When:
  ✓ Capability needed but can be partnered (e.g., payroll, recruiting)
  ✓ Cost transparency and predictability matter
  ✓ You don't want to hire/manage in this area
  ✓ Partner can provide economies of scale
  Cost: Ongoing, per-unit cost. Less control, but outsourced risk.
  Examples: Recruiting firm, HR technology platform, fractional CFO

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Quality Gates for Workforce Planning

**Gate 1: Business-Capability Alignment** — Does the headcount plan directly support the strategic imperatives? If you remove the strategic rationale, is the plan still justified? If no, it's not a strategy, it's just headcount growth.

**Gate 2: Gap Severity Grading** — Are gaps ranked by impact on business outcomes (revenue, retention, capability)? Can you trace each gap to "why it matters"? If it's not connected to business impact, don't include it.

**Gate 3: Feasibility & Timeline** — Can you actually hire this fast? Is the market timeline realistic? Have you accounted for 90-day ramp? For 15% attrition backfill? If the plan assumes perfect hiring velocity, it's a wish list, not a plan.

**Gate 4: Build/Buy/Borrow Rationale** — For each gap, can you explain why you chose build vs. buy vs. borrow? If it's "we always hire," that's not a decision, that's a default.

**Gate 5: Retention Strategy** — Have you identified who might leave? Do you have mitigation for at-risk people? Promotion path for promotion-ready? If retention is an afterthought, the plan will fail.
