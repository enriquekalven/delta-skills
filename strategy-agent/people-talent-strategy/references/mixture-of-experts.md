# Mixture of Experts: People Strategy Review Panel

Every people strategy needs to be stress-tested by expert lenses. A single perspective misses blind spots. This reference describes a 5-expert panel that reviews people strategy work and provides critiques from different vantage points.

---

## The Five Experts

### Expert 1: Fortune 100 CHRO

**Background:** 20+ years at large enterprises. Has scaled from 5K to 50K+ employees. Board-level perspective. Regulatory and risk expertise.

**Lens:** Enterprise scale, governance, risk management, pay equity, regulatory compliance, board requirements.

**Key Questions They Ask:**
- Is this scalable to 10x the current size?
- What's the legal/regulatory risk?
- Is pay equity defensible?
- Are we building infrastructure that works at scale?
- Does the board understand and support this?
- What's the catastrophic failure scenario?

**Scoring Dimensions (1-5):**
- Scalability (can this work at 100K employees?)
- Risk management (what are we missing?)
- Compliance (pay equity, discrimination, legal?)
- Board-readiness (can we present this to the board?)
- Enterprise culture fit (does this work in big org?)

**Common Critiques:**
- "This assumes perfect execution. What if it breaks?"
- "You haven't thought about compliance implications."
- "At this scale, this architecture doesn't work. Here's why."
- "What's your succession plan for this function?"

---

### Expert 2: Startup People Leader

**Background:** 10+ years building people function at startup scale. Scaled from 50 to 500+ employees. Knows startup operating model. Speed and scrappiness matter. Equity-heavy compensation.

**Lens:** Startup constraints (speed, limited budget, culture preservation), scrappy execution, founder collaboration, high growth, equity comp strategy.

**Key Questions They Ask:**
- Can you execute this in 30 days or does it require 6 months?
- What's the MVP (minimum viable people process)?
- Have you aligned the founders on this?
- Is this bleeding resources without ROI?
- How does this scale culture, not just headcount?
- What's the cheapest way to achieve this?

**Scoring Dimensions (1-5):**
- Speed (can we do this quickly?)
- Founder alignment (do they buy it?)
- Budget efficiency (cost-effective?)
- Founder culture preservation (does culture scale?)
- Scalability without re-working (build for 10x?)

**Common Critiques:**
- "This requires a team of people you can't afford yet."
- "You're building processes before you know what you need. Go simpler."
- "The founders won't support this investment. How do you change their mind?"
- "Good plan but you need buy-in from the founder first."

---

### Expert 3: Organizational Psychologist

**Background:** 15+ years researching and applying behavioral science to organizations. PhD in organizational psychology. Knows the research on engagement, culture, behavior change.

**Lens:** Behavioral science, research-backed practices, culture measurement, psychological safety, engagement drivers, change psychology.

**Key Questions They Ask:**
- What's the behavioral science behind this approach?
- Is this evidence-based or folklore?
- How do you measure the psychological constructs you're trying to build?
- What's the change management psychology here?
- Are you designing for human nature or against it?
- What research supports this approach?

**Scoring Dimensions (1-5):**
- Evidence-basis (is this backed by research?)
- Measurement rigor (can you measure culture/engagement scientifically?)
- Behavioral design (does it align with how humans actually behave?)
- Change readiness (have you thought about adoption barriers?)
- Psychological validity (are you measuring what you think you're measuring?)

**Common Critiques:**
- "There's good research on this, but you're not applying it correctly."
- "You're measuring the wrong thing. Here's what the research shows you should measure."
- "People don't change behavior because you tell them to. Here's how to change behavior."
- "This sounds good but research shows it doesn't work at this scale."

---

### Expert 4: Compensation & Benefits Consultant

**Background:** 20+ years in compensation consulting. Knows market data deeply. Tax implications, equity mechanics, pay equity analysis, incentive design.

**Lens:** Market competitiveness, pay equity, incentive alignment, tax/legal implications, equity mechanics, benefit design.

**Key Questions They Ask:**
- Is this at market for the role/location/level?
- Have you audited for pay equity gaps?
- What are the tax implications?
- Does the incentive structure align with business goals?
- Are you over-paying or under-paying relative to peers?
- What's the equity vesting math?

**Scoring Dimensions (1-5):**
- Market competitiveness (is this at market?)
- Pay equity (gaps between similar roles?)
- Incentive alignment (does it reward right behavior?)
- Tax/legal compliance (any issues?)
- Retention efficiency (are you getting ROI on comp?)

**Common Critiques:**
- "Your market data is a year old. Current market is 15% higher."
- "You have a material pay gap here. This is a legal risk."
- "Your incentive structure incentivizes the wrong behavior. Here's the fix."
- "This equity pool is too large. You'll dilute yourself."

---

### Expert 5: DEI Strategist

**Background:** 15+ years in diversity, equity, inclusion strategy. Knows systemic bias, representation metrics, inclusive culture, DEI ROI, belonging science.

**Lens:** Diversity representation, inclusive hiring, belonging culture, systemic bias, DEI metrics, representation gaps, inclusive design.

**Key Questions They Ask:**
- Is your pipeline diverse or homogeneous?
- What's your representation by level and function?
- Where are the attrition gaps (do certain groups leave more)?
- Is this inclusive by design or an afterthought?
- Are you measuring belonging or just representation?
- Have you identified and addressed systemic barriers?

**Scoring Dimensions (1-5):**
- Representation (are you diverse by level, function, background?)
- Inclusive hiring (is the process designed to surface diverse talent?)
- Belonging (do underrepresented people feel they belong?)
- Systemic change (are you fixing root causes or applying band-aids?)
- Measurement (can you track DEI outcomes?)

**Common Critiques:**
- "Your hiring process has homophily bias. Here's how to remove it."
- "You have representation at junior levels but not at senior. Where's the pipeline?"
- "This sounds inclusive but doesn't address attrition. Women are leaving at 2x rate."
- "You're tokenizing. Representation without belonging won't work."

## Expert Panel Review Process

### Step 1: Prepare the Strategy for Review

Before convening experts, prepare a strategy document that includes:
- Business context and strategic imperatives
- Current state assessment (gaps, risks, engagement, turnover, demographics)
- Proposed people strategy across all 6 phases
- Key recommendations and tradeoffs
- Implementation plan with timelines and owners
- Success metrics and kill conditions

### Step 2: Expert Review Sequence

Run each expert's review independently (each expert shouldn't see other experts' feedback yet).

**Expert 1 (Fortune 100 CHRO)** — 30 min conversation
- "Here's the strategy. What am I missing from an enterprise/scale perspective?"
- Score each dimension
- Identify top 3 risks they'd flag

**Expert 2 (Startup People Leader)** — 30 min conversation
- "Here's the strategy. Can we execute this at startup speed with startup budget?"
- Score each dimension
- Identify founder alignment gaps

**Expert 3 (Organizational Psychologist)** — 30 min conversation
- "Here's the strategy. What's the behavioral science here? Is this evidence-based?"
- Score each dimension
- Identify measurement gaps

**Expert 4 (Compensation Consultant)** — 30 min conversation
- "Here's the comp strategy. Is it market? Any pay equity issues?"
- Score each dimension
- Identify comp/equity risks

**Expert 5 (DEI Strategist)** — 30 min conversation
- "Here's the people strategy. Is it inclusive? What are the representation gaps?"
- Score each dimension
- Identify systemic bias risks

### Step 3: Synthesis & Calibration

Convene all experts (virtually) for a 90-minute synthesis meeting:

```
EXPERT PANEL SYNTHESIS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

AGENDA (90 min):

1. INDIVIDUAL SCORES (10 min)
   Each expert shares scores (1-5) on their dimensions.
   Immediate disagreements noted.

2. ALIGNMENT AREAS (20 min)
   What did multiple experts agree on?
   Examples: "Enterprise CHRO and psychologist both flagged
   change management as risk. That's a must-fix."

3. CONTRADICTION AREAS (20 min)
   Where did experts disagree?
   Example: "Startup expert says this is too much process.
   Enterprise CHRO says not enough. How do we thread that needle?"

4. HARD STOPS (15 min)
   Are there 2+ experts who say "this won't work" or "this has
   legal risk"? Those are hard stops. Must be addressed before
   execution.

5. RECOMMENDATIONS (15 min)
   Top 3-5 things to fix before rolling out.
   Prioritized by impact and feasibility.

6. PASS/CONDITIONAL/REJECT (10 min)
   Overall verdict:
   PASS = Ready to execute
   CONDITIONAL = Ready with fixes to [X specific things]
   REJECT = Strategy has fundamental flaws, requires major rework
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Hard Stops & Veto Logic

Certain issues warrant rejection or major rework. The panel uses these criteria:

**Hard Stop: Pay Equity Issue**
If compensation consultant finds material (10%+) unjustified pay gaps, REJECT until fixed.
Reason: Legal and retention risk too high.

**Hard Stop: Systemic Bias in Hiring**
If DEI strategist finds hiring process systematically excludes underrepresented groups, CONDITIONAL.
Require: Hiring process redesign before rolling out strategy.

**Hard Stop: Change Management Failure Risk**
If psychologist flags "this will fail in adoption because [behavioral barrier]," CONDITIONAL.
Require: Change management strategy that addresses barrier.

**Hard Stop: Founder Misalignment**
If startup expert flags "founders won't support this," CONDITIONAL.
Require: Founder alignment before execution.

**Hard Stop: Compliance Risk**
If enterprise CHRO flags legal/regulatory risk, REJECT until addressed.
Reason: Legal risk outweighs strategic benefit.

## When to Use Expert Panel Review

Run expert panel review:
- ✓ Before finalizing any 6-phase people strategy
- ✓ Before major org redesign
- ✓ Before comp/equity changes that affect >20% of people
- ✓ Before rolling out new culture/engagement initiatives
- ✓ When people strategy has high stakes or high risk

Don't need full panel:
- Quick tactical HR decision (hire one person, promotion)
- Process improvement (updating employee handbook)
- Administrative change (office hours, benefits enrollment)

Use subset of experts:
- Comp-only question → Use Expert 4
- Hiring/diversity question → Use Expert 5
- Change management question → Use Expert 3
