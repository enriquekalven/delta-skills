# Talent Acquisition & Employer Brand Strategy

Talent acquisition is both an operational capability and a strategic advantage. Most companies treat it as transactional: open a job, post on LinkedIn, interview, hire. World-class companies treat it as strategic: they build an employer brand, design for pipeline, optimize interview quality, and compete for the top talent their business model demands.

---

## Part 1: Hiring Strategy by Role Type

Different roles demand different hiring strategies. A sales hire is not an engineering hire. An early employee at a startup is not a late-stage hire. Match the strategy to the role.

### Sales Hiring Strategy

**Profile:** Sales roles scale fastest. Ramp time is 3-6 months. Talent market is deep. Turnover is expected (15-25% annual).

**Sourcing:**
- **Recruiter-led outreach** (top 50%): Direct sourcing from competitors, through recruiting agencies
- **Internal referrals** (top 30%): Best source, but requires referral incentive ($2K-5K bounty)
- **LinkedIn/job board** (remaining 20%): Attracts inbound, but mixed quality

**Interview Design:**
1. **Phone screen** (15 min) — Does candidate have basic sales skills/experience? Are they interested?
2. **Discovery conversation** (30 min) — Deep dive on sales approach, pipeline methodology, deal closing
3. **Sales simulation** (45 min) — Pitch the product, handle objections, close the deal. Watch for:
   - Do they ask discovery questions or just pitch?
   - How do they handle pushback?
   - Do they follow a methodology or wing it?
4. **Manager 1:1** (30 min) — Fit with manager, compensation expectations, team dynamic
5. **Reference calls** (2-3 calls) — Critical for sales. Call their managers. Ask:
   - Did they hit quota? How consistently?
   - How did they handle losing deals?
   - What were their strengths and weaknesses?

**Hiring Bar:** Hire for proven quota attainment + cultural fit. Don't hire for potential; hire for track record. A rep who hit 120% of quota at Company X will likely hit quota at your company. One who missed will miss again, no matter how smart.

**Compensation Positioning:**
- **Highly competitive ($150K-300K+ for AEs)** — Sales talent is mobile. If your comp is below market, they leave. Spend on base + commission.
- **Equity:** 0.05%-0.2% for senior reps (shows long-term value)
- **Benefits:** Standard + car allowance + unlimited PTO common for sales

### Engineering Hiring Strategy

**Profile:** Engineering roles are high-impact but slow to hire. Ramp time is 3-6 months. Talent market is tight. Turnover is low (10-15% annual) for good cultures.

**Sourcing:**
- **Passive recruitment** (top 50%): Recruiter outreach to employed senior engineers from target companies
- **Referrals** (top 30%): Engineer referrals → highest quality
- **Job boards** (remaining 20%): Attracts some inbound, but often junior or mid-level

**Interview Design:**
1. **Phone screen** (30 min) — Background, experience, technical level. Light coding Q.
2. **Technical assessment** (1-2 hours, async or live) — Code a small problem. Not LeetCode hard; realistic to your work.
3. **System design** (if senior, 1 hour) — Design an API, database, or service. Can they think about scale, tradeoffs, dependencies?
4. **Manager conversation** (30 min) — Team, growth path, technical challenges
5. **Team conversations** (2-3 peer engineers, 30 min each) — Culture fit, collaboration, technical depth
6. **Reference calls** (1-2) — Manager and peer. Ask:
   - What was their technical contribution?
   - How did they collaborate/communicate?
   - What did they learn from this person?

**Hiring Bar:** Hire for technical rigor + customer empathy + team collaboration. You can teach someone your tech stack. You can't teach curiosity or collaboration. A strong engineer who cares about customer needs is rare.

**Compensation Positioning:**
- **Highly competitive ($150K-350K+ for senior engineers)** — Tech talent has options. Match market or lose to big tech.
- **Equity:** 0.1%-0.5% for senior engineers (engineers care about ownership)
- **Benefits:** Laptop allowance, learning budget, home office setup

### Product Hiring Strategy

**Profile:** Product roles are critical but hard to evaluate. Ramp time is 3-6 months. Talent market is competitive but not as tight as engineering.

**Sourcing:**
- **Recruiter + PM network** (top 50%): Your VP Product's network, warm intros
- **Referrals** (top 30%): Other PMs refer great PMs
- **Companies known for great product** (20%): Microsoft, Google, Apple, Figma, Stripe, etc.

**Interview Design:**
1. **Cover letter/portfolio review** — Have them send a one-pager on:
   - A product problem they solved
   - How they approached it
   - What metrics mattered
   - What they'd do differently
2. **Product sense interview** (1 hour) — Give them a product challenge:
   - "How would you improve Uber?"
   - "Design a feature for Slack."
   - Watch for: Do they ask clarifying Qs? Do they define success metrics? Do they consider user psychology?
3. **Analytics/Metrics interview** (1 hour) — Give them a metric and ask them to diagnose:
   - "DAU dropped 15%. Why? What would you do?"
   - Watch for: Hypothesis generation, data literacy, prioritization
4. **Manager conversation** (30 min) — Working style, ambition, growth path
5. **Cross-functional conversation** (engineering lead + designer, 30 min each) — Can they influence without authority?
6. **Reference calls** (2) — Manager and skip-level. Ask:
   - What was their best product decision?
   - When did they get it wrong? What did they learn?
   - How did they influence engineering & design?

**Hiring Bar:** Hire for customer obsession + data literacy + influence. A PM who's never dug into data or talked to 20 customers in a week is at a disadvantage.

**Compensation Positioning:**
- **Competitive ($150K-280K+ for strong PMs)** — Product talent is valued but less scarce than engineering
- **Equity:** 0.1%-0.3% for strong PMs
- **Non-comp:** Title, autonomy, impact matter to good PMs

### Customer Success Hiring Strategy

**Profile:** CS scales with company. Ramp time is 1-3 months. Talent market is broad but quality varies. Good CS managers are scarce.

**Sourcing:**
- **Inside sales promotions** (top 30%): Great reps often make great CSMs
- **Customer-facing companies** (top 40%): Hospitality, sales, support experience
- **Referrals** (remaining 30%)

**Interview Design:**
1. **Phone screen** (20 min) — Customer service philosophy, experience, listening skills
2. **Customer scenario roleplay** (30 min) — Customer has problem/complaint. How do you handle?
   - Do they listen first or jump to solution?
   - How do they manage frustration?
   - Do they think about business value or just fix the problem?
3. **Manager conversation** (30 min) — Team, workflow, success metrics
4. **Peer conversation** (30 min) — How will they work with sales, product, support?
5. **Reference calls** (1-2) — Focus on customer feedback. Have they managed difficult customers well?

**Hiring Bar:** Hire for empathy + business orientation + resilience. A CS person who can't handle customer anger or doesn't think about customer ROI will fail.

**Compensation Positioning:**
- **Moderate ($80K-150K for CSMs, $120K-200K for managers)** — CS is valuable but less scarce than engineering or sales
- **Equity:** 0.05%-0.2% for strong CSMs
- **Incentive:** Success bonuses (if customer hits usage targets, CSM gets bonus)

## Part 2: Employer Brand & Value Proposition

Your employer brand is the answer to: "Why would a great person work here instead of our competitors?"

### Defining Your Employer Value Proposition (EVP)

```
EMPLOYER VALUE PROPOSITION FRAMEWORK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
EVP is what you offer employees. Not compensation alone. Not mission alone.
It's the complete value exchange. What do people get that they can't get elsewhere?

For each candidate persona, define:

1. COMPENSATION & BENEFITS
   What can employees earn, and what's the total rewards package?
   • [Early-career engineer] gets: $150K salary + 0.15% equity + health + learning budget
   • [Experienced sales rep] gets: $80K base + commission-based total comp $200K+
   • [Product manager] gets: $200K salary + 0.25% equity + unlimited PTO
   Be specific. "Competitive compensation" means nothing.

2. CAREER & LEARNING
   How do people grow here?
   • [Engineer] path: IC → Senior IC → Architect → Director of Engineering
   • [Sales] path: SDR → AE → Senior AE → Sales Manager → Director
   • [Product] path: Associate PM → PM → Senior PM → Director
   Be specific about: time in each level, what skills they develop, what they need to do to progress.

3. WORK & CULTURE
   What's the day-to-day like?
   • [Early-stage startup]: High autonomy, you own your domain, move fast
   • [Scale-up]: Structured enough that you can focus, collaborative, still nimble
   • [Large enterprise]: Clear career path, strong mentorship, good work-life balance
   Be honest about tradeoffs.

4. MISSION & IMPACT
   Why does the work matter?
   • [Climate company]: Building technology that reduces emissions by 1B tons by 2030
   • [Healthcare company]: Empowering doctors to spend more time with patients
   • [B2B SaaS]: Enabling SMBs to compete with enterprise customers
   Be authentic. If your mission is "maximize shareholder value," that's fine, but don't
   pretend it's something else.

5. LOCATION & FLEXIBILITY
   Where is the work, and how flexible is it?
   • [Remote]: Fully remote, hire globally, async-first
   • [Hybrid]: 3 days in office, flexibility for core hours
   • [In-person]: Must be in San Francisco, collaborative culture
   Be clear about requirements and flexibility.

EXAMPLE EVP STATEMENT:
"At TechCorp, we're building the operating system for supply chains. If you're an engineer,
you'll work with a team of 4 other senior engineers on core infrastructure that 10K+ companies
depend on. You'll go from 0→1 on major systems, have input on our technical direction, and
learn from founders who built the last company from $0→$100M. Salary is $220K, equity is 0.2%,
and we're office-optional (2 days/week in SF). You'll progress from Senior IC to Architect to
potentially Director by year 4. We care about your growth."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Building Your EVP

**Step 1: Interview your best employees**
Ask: "Why did you join? Why did you stay? What would you miss if you left?" Their answers are your EVP.

**Step 2: Benchmark competitors**
What's Google offering engineers? What's Stripe offering PMs? What's HubSpot offering sales? Be honest about where you're competitive and where you're not.

**Step 3: Identify your asymmetric advantages**
You likely can't match Google on compensation. But you might offer:
- Equity with real upside (if you're pre-liquidity event)
- Autonomy and impact (early-stage advantage)
- Domain expertise (if you're in a specialization)
- Customer relationships (if you're known in your market)
- Learning opportunity (if your team is world-class)

**Step 4: Build your messaging**
Use your EVP in:
- Job descriptions (don't just list responsibilities; tell people why they'd want this role)
- Interview conversations (sell, don't just screen)
- LinkedIn posts and company content
- Recruiter conversations (your recruiters should know your EVP cold)

  Stage 1 (Prospects): 30 people identified + outreached (5 months of hiring)
  Stage 2 (Screened): 12 people passed phone screen
  Stage 3 (Interviewed): 6 people passed sales sim
  Stage 4 (Offers): 2 people made offers
  Stage 5 (Accepted): 1 offer accepted

Conversion rates:
  Prospect → Screened: 40% (you reject 60% without interview)
  Screened → Interview: 50% (they reject themselves or you eliminate them)
  Interview → Offer: 33% (6 interviews → 2 offers)
  Offer → Acceptance: 50% (2 offers → 1 accepted)
  Overall: 30 → 1 acceptance = 3.3% close rate

Time in each stage:
  Sourcing: 2-4 weeks (finding people)
  Screen: 1-2 weeks (scheduling)
  Interviews: 2-3 weeks (4-5 conversations, feedback)
  Offer → Acceptance: 1-2 weeks (negotiation)
  Total: 6-11 weeks per hire

Hiring formula: To hire 4 AEs/year, you need:
  4 hires ÷ 3.3% = ~120 prospects in pipeline at all times

### Leading Indicators of Hiring Health

Track these metrics to predict whether you'll hit hiring targets:

| Metric | Target | What It Means |
|--------|--------|---|
| **Sourced candidates/month** | [Role-specific] | Are you building pipeline fast enough? If low, you'll miss targets. |
| **Phone screen conversion** | 40-60% | Should most candidates you screen move to next stage? If <30%, your targeting is off. |
| **Interview-to-offer rate** | 25-50% | Are your interviews working? If <15%, interview quality is weak. |
| **Offer acceptance rate** | 60-80% | Are your offers competitive? If <40%, compensation or messaging is off. |

## Part 4: Diversity & Inclusive Hiring

Diversity is not HR virtue-signaling. It's a competitive advantage: diverse teams make better decisions, have fewer blind spots, and build products that work for more customers.

### Bias Reduction in Hiring

**1. Job Description Language** — Remove gender-coded language:
  - ✗ "rockstar," "ninja," "guru" → ✓ "expert," "strong,"
  - ✗ "aggressive growth mindset" → ✓ "ambitious, strategic thinking"
  - ✗ "willing to work 80-hour weeks" → ✓ "flexible hours, high output"

**2. Interview Panel Diversity** — Ensure interview panels include women, people of color, and people outside the dominant group. Research shows homogeneous panels exhibit implicit bias.

**3. Structured Interviews** — Use the same questions for all candidates. Don't improvise per-candidate (introduces bias).

**4. Rubric Scoring** — Establish scoring rubrics pre-interview. Score candidates immediately after each conversation (memory bias fades). Don't compare candidates until all are scored.

**5. Reference Call Diversity** — Ask different questions for candidates who are similar and dissimilar to interviewers. Watch for confirmation bias (asking softball Qs to "us" candidates, tough Qs to "them").

## Quality Gates for Talent Acquisition Strategy

**Gate 1: Role-Specific Hiring Strategy** — Is the hiring approach tailored to the role? Sales hiring ≠ engineering hiring. If you're using a one-size-fits-all process, you're leaving quality on the table.

**Gate 2: Sourcing Diversification** — Are you sourcing from multiple channels? If all your engineers come from LinkedIn job posts, your pipeline is fragile and not diverse.

**Gate 3: EVP Clarity** — Can you articulate, in one paragraph per persona, why someone would work for you instead of a competitor? If you can't, your EVP is weak.

**Gate 4: Interview Quality** — Are interviews structured and scored consistently? Or is every interview different, introducing bias? Inconsistency kills quality.

**Gate 5: Diversity in Practice** — Are your diversity goals connected to sourcing, interview process, and offer strategy? Or are they just aspirational numbers? Goals without levers don't work.
