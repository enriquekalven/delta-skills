# Compensation & Incentive Design Strategy

Compensation is both a retention tool and a competitive advantage. Get it wrong and you lose people, breed resentment, and misalign behavior. Get it right and you attract world-class talent, motivate performance, and build a fair, meritocratic organization. Compensation is not just what people make — it's a statement of what you value.

---

## Part 1: Total Rewards Framework

Total rewards ≠ salary. Total rewards = salary + bonus + equity + benefits + work-life quality + growth opportunity + impact.

### Building a Total Rewards Philosophy

```
TOTAL REWARDS PHILOSOPHY TEMPLATE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

OUR TOTAL REWARDS PHILOSOPHY

Principles:
  1. MARKET COMPETITIVE: We pay at or above market for the role/level.
     Why: We need to attract world-class talent. Underpaying signals we don't value them.

  2. INTERNAL EQUITY: Same level, same market = similar compensation.
     Why: Pay gaps breed resentment and legal risk. Unfair.

  3. PERFORMANCE-DIFFERENTIATED: Two strong performers in same role can have 20%+
     comp variation based on performance, impact, growth trajectory.
     Why: Rewards excellence. Motivates growth.

  4. TRANSPARENT: Employees understand how comp is determined.
     Why: Transparency builds trust. Opacity breeds suspicion.

  5. ALIGNED WITH BUSINESS: Compensation incentives must align with business strategy.
     Why: If we incentivize revenue but need product excellence, we fail.

Compensation Structure by Level:
  Early Career (L1, e.g., SDR, Junior Eng):
    • 80% base + 20% variable (bonus/equity)
    • Why: Need income stability. Variable should matter but not dominate.

  Mid-Career (L2, e.g., AE, Senior Eng):
    • 60% base + 40% variable
    • Why: Performance matters more. Can take more risk.

  Senior (L3+, e.g., VP, Director):
    • 50% base + 50% variable + equity
    • Why: Fully aligned with business outcomes. Significant equity.

COMPETITIVE POSTURE BY FUNCTION:
  Sales: 90th percentile (market top 10%) — Talent is mobile, need to win
  Engineering: 85th percentile (market top 15%) — Strong talent market, high demand
  Product: 75th percentile (market top 25%) — Good talent market, less mobile than eng/sales
  Customer Success: 60th percentile (market average) — Large talent pool, teachable role
  Finance/HR: 50th percentile (market average) — Competent is enough
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Part 2: Benchmarking Methodology

"Market competitive" means nothing without data. Benchmarking is how you prove you're at market.

### Market Data Sources

| Source | Cost | Accuracy | Coverage | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Radford, Mercer, Towers Phlips** | $10K-50K | Very high | Comprehensive | Enterprise roles, benchmarking studies |
| **Levels.fyi** | Free | Medium (self-reported) | Tech heavy | Engineering/product roles |
| **PayScale** | $10K+ | Medium | Broad | General benchmarking |
| **Salary.com** | Free | Low (dated) | Very broad | Quick checks only |
| **LinkedIn Salary** | Free | Medium (self-reported) | Broad | Quick checks, trending |
| **Glassdoor** | Free | Low-medium (self-reported) | Broad | Quick checks |
| **Recruiter conversations** | Free | High | Specific market | Local market rates |
| **Equity databases** (Carta, Pulsar) | Subscription | High | Startups | Startup equity benchmarking |

```

1. DEFINE THE MARKET
   Geography: SF Bay Area (remote-first, but core team in SF)
   Company size: $50M-500M ARR, growth stage
   Industry: SaaS / infrastructure (similar to us)
   Talent density: Can work independently, mentors others

2. GATHER DATA FROM MULTIPLE SOURCES
   Source A (Radford study, 2024):
     • Median: $240K (base $180K + bonus 15% + equity value $60K over 4 years)
     • 25th percentile: $210K
     • 75th percentile: $285K

   Source B (Levels.fyi, 2024):
     • Median: $265K (base $190K + bonus 15% + equity)
     • Range: $220K-$320K

   Source C (Recruiters, phone conversations):
     • "Market for strong backend seniors is $200-250K base in SF"
     • Equity: 0.15%-0.4% for growth stage
     • One recruiter: "We're seeing $300K all-in for A-round companies"

   Source D (Our recent hires):
     • Hired 2 senior engineers in past 6 months
     • Offers accepted: $210K base, $250K all-in (with equity)

3. TRIANGULATE THE MARKET RANGE
   Based on 4 sources:
     Radford: $240K
     Levels.fyi: $265K
     Recruiters: $225K (mid-point of $200-250K)
     Our hires: $250K
     Average: $245K

   Market range: $220K-$280K (50th-75th percentile)
   Our target: $240K (50th percentile) for strong performer
   Our range: $210K (25th percentile) - $290K (75th percentile)

4. SET OUR COMPENSATION
   Entry-level senior (just promoted, learning the role):
     $210K base

   Strong performer (doing the job well):
     $240K base

   Star performer (top 10%, doing multiple roles):
     $280K base

   Equity: 0.25% for strong performer (4-year vest, 1-year cliff)

5. MONITOR & ADJUST
   Quarterly: Check Levels.fyi and LinkedIn salary for trends
   Annually: Run full benchmarking study
   When: If we're 15%+ below market, re-adjust
```

## Part 3: Equity Strategy

Equity is an asymmetric lever: costs you very little today but can unlock tremendous retention and alignment for long-term employees.

### Equity Philosophy

```
EQUITY FRAMEWORK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PRINCIPLE 1: Equity is for retention and alignment, not cheap compensation.
Never use equity as an excuse to underpay base salary.

PRINCIPLE 2: Equity must have real value.
If your company is pre-product or on a failure trajectory, equity is worthless.
If your company is growth-stage and on path to IPO/acquisition, equity matters.

PRINCIPLE 3: More equity early, less equity late.
Early employees take more risk and have less salary premium.
Late employees have high salary; equity is smaller multiple.

EQUITY GRANTS BY STAGE & LEVEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

SEED STAGE ($1M-5M ARR, high risk)
  Engineer (L2/L3): 0.5%-1.5%
  Product Manager: 0.3%-0.8%
  Sales: 0.2%-0.6%
  VP/Director: 1.0%-3.0%

SERIES A ($5M-20M ARR, medium risk)
  Engineer (L2/L3): 0.2%-0.8%
  Product Manager: 0.15%-0.4%
  Sales: 0.1%-0.4%
  VP/Director: 0.5%-1.5%

SERIES B+ ($20M+ ARR, lower risk)
  Engineer (L2/L3): 0.1%-0.4%
  Product Manager: 0.1%-0.2%
  Sales: 0.05%-0.2%
  VP/Director: 0.2%-0.8%

CLIFF & VESTING
  Standard: 1-year cliff, 4-year vest (can't cash out anything for 12 months,
           then 25% vests annually for 4 years)
  Rationale: Retention (leaving at month 13 → keep all equity; month 12 → lose all)

REFRESH GRANTS
  High performer who has vested: Give refresh grant (smaller, new cliff/vest)
  Goal: Keep equity meaningful as equity value grows
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Part 4: Sales Compensation Design

Sales compensation is unique: revenue is directly attributable, so comp can be highly variable and performance-driven.

### Sales Comp Structure

```
SALES COMP DESIGN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ROLE: Enterprise Account Executive, $500K-$2M ACV contracts

Total Comp Goal: $250K
  Break-down: $100K base + $150K commission

Why this structure:
  • Base ($100K) = 40% — Provides income stability. Reps can live on base if needed.
  • Commission ($150K) = 60% — Heavily incentivized on revenue. Motivates quota attainment.
  • If rep hits 100% quota → $250K. If hits 150% quota → $325K.

COMMISSION STRUCTURE: Tiered accelerator
  0-75% quota: $3 per $1 of revenue (low percentage, no bonus)
  75-100% quota: $4 per $1 of revenue (80% commission rate)
  100-125% quota: $5 per $1 of revenue (accelerator)
  125%+ quota: $6 per $1 of revenue (high accelerator)

Quota: $150K ARR to hit 100%
  75% quota ($112.5K ARR): Earn ~$340K + base = $440K total (miss)
  100% quota ($150K ARR): Earn ~$600K + base = $700K total (hit)
  125% quota ($187.5K ARR): Earn ~$937K + base = $1.037M total (crush it)

Why tiered accelerator:
  • Punishes missing quota (low commission rate at 75-100%)
  • Rewards hitting quota (commission kicks up at 100%)
  • Incentivizes overachievement (accelerator at 125%+)
  • Avoids "bonus cliff" (if rep hits 100.1%, don't leave comp on table)

DEAL MECHANICS:
  • Commission paid when contract is signed (not when revenue is recognized)
    Why: Motivates closing. Rep gets paid when customer commits.

  • If deal is cancelled in year 1, rep claws back commission proportionally
    Why: Prevents reps from closing bad deals. Aligns with customer success.

  • Split commission on multi-threading (if rep + manager close deal, they split)
    Why: Manager involvement on large deals. Fair split.

BONUS POOL (annual):
  If company misses revenue target, sales bonus pool doesn't pay
  (even if reps hit their quotas)
  Why: Aligns sales to company success, not just personal quota
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Part 5: Pay Equity Audit

Pay equity issues (systematically paying women or minorities less for same work) are both legal risk and talent risk.

### Pay Equity Analysis Process

```
PAY EQUITY AUDIT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

STEP 1: EXTRACT DATA
  Get from HRIS: [Name, role, level, salary, bonus, equity, demographics]
  Anonymize: [Use ID instead of name]

STEP 2: CONTROL FOR JOB & LEVEL
  Engineers, level 3:
    ├─ Person A: $260K (White male, 4 years tenure)
    ├─ Person B: $235K (Asian female, 3 years tenure) ← Gap: $25K
    ├─ Person C: $248K (Black male, 2 years tenure)
    └─ Person D: $242K (Latina female, 2.5 years tenure) ← Gap: $6K

STEP 3: ANALYZE VARIANCE
  Compare within same role/level:
    • Is gap explained by tenure? (Person B has 1 year less tenure → $5K gap is reasonable)
    • Is gap explained by performance? (Person B rated lower than Person A → $5K gap is reasonable)
    • Is gap explained by location? (If different offices/markets, may be legitimate)
    • Is gap unjustified? (Same level, tenure, performance, location → pay differently = issue)

STEP 4: FLAG ANOMALIES
  When to investigate further:
    • Women paid 10%+ less than men in same role/level with same tenure
    • Minorities paid 10%+ less than white majority with same tenure
    • Patterns (not just one outlier) suggest systemic issue

STEP 5: REMEDIATE
  For justified gaps: Document rationale (tenure, performance, market)
  For unjustified gaps:
    • Small gap ($2K-5K): Document and monitor
    • Medium gap ($5K-15K): Adjust up by 50% of gap, monitor
    • Large gap ($15K+): Adjust to market, conduct legal review

STEP 6: SYSTEMIC FIX
  If gap is systemic:
    • Review hiring process (are certain groups hired at lower levels?)
    • Review promotion process (are certain groups promoted slower?)
    • Review raise process (are certain groups given smaller raises?)
    • Update hiring/promotion rubrics to remove bias
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Part 6: Bonus & Incentive Design

Beyond base salary and equity, bonuses align behavior and reward performance.

### Individual + Company Bonus Structure

```
BONUS DESIGN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ROLE: Product Manager
Base salary: $200K
Target bonus: 20% (goal is $40K)

BONUS CALCULATION:
  Individual performance (50% weight):
    • "Launch new feature on time" — Weighting: 20% → Max $4K
    • "Improve NPS by 5 points" — Weighting: 20% → Max $4K
    • "Complete user research on 3 initiatives" — Weighting: 10% → Max $2K

  Company performance (50% weight):
    • "Hit revenue target" — Weighting: 35% → Max $7K
    • "Hit customer retention target" — Weighting: 15% → Max $3K

SCORING:
  Individual performance:
    ✓ If PM launches on time = 20/20 points → Get $4K
    ✓ If PM improves NPS by 3 points (60% of target) = 12/20 → Get $2.4K
    ✓ If PM completes 2 of 3 research projects = 7/10 → Get $1.4K
    Total individual: $7.8K

  Company performance:
    ✓ If company hits 95% of revenue target = 19/20 → Get $6.65K
    ✓ If company hits retention target = 20/20 → Get $3K
    Total company: $9.65K

  TOTAL BONUS: $7.8K + $9.65K = $17.45K (44% of $40K target)

Why this structure:
  • Individual metrics (PM controls): Rewards own effort
  • Company metrics (Team controls): Aligns PM to company success
  • 50/50 split: Balanced. PM cares about their work and company success.
  • Transparent: PM knows exactly what they need to do to earn bonus
  • Not all-or-nothing: 60% attainment = 60% bonus (not cliff)

WHEN TO USE BONUS vs. NO BONUS:
  Use bonus for: Sales (obvious), PM (strategic), Eng Manager (team performance)
  Skip for: Junior IC Engineers (focus on learning, not bonus), CS IC (commission better)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## Quality Gates for Compensation Strategy

**Gate 1: Benchmarking Rigor** — Is comp based on real market data or guessing? If you haven't benchmarked, you don't know if you're at market.

**Gate 2: Philosophy Clarity** — Can you articulate your comp philosophy in one page? If it's vague, it will be applied inconsistently.

**Gate 3: Transparency** — Can employees understand how compensation is determined? Or is it a black box? Black boxes breed resentment. Transparency builds trust.

**Gate 4: Pay Equity** — Have you audited for pay gaps? If you haven't, you're flying blind on legal and retention risk. Do the audit.

**Gate 5: Alignment** — Does compensation incentivize the behavior you want? If you want customer obsession but commission reps for deals at any cost, you've misaligned incentives.
