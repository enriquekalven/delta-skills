# Target Screening: From Landscape to Shortlist

This playbook converts a strategic rationale into a prioritized list of acquisition targets. The process is funnel-shaped: wide at the top (landscape scan), progressively narrower as you apply screening criteria, ending with 3-8 qualified shortlist targets.

## Stage 1: Industry Landscape Mapping

Before identifying specific targets, map the addressable universe. This prevents you from missing obvious targets and helps you understand competitive dynamics.

### Step 1A: Define the Market Scope

Based on your acquisition thesis, define the boundaries of the addressable market.

**Example 1 (Capability Acquisition):** You want to acquire AI/ML talent and technology in the fraud detection space. Market scope: Companies building fraud detection models, anywhere globally, any stage (pre-revenue to $100M+).

**Example 2 (Market Entry):** You want to enter the SMB accounting software market via acquisition. Market scope: Accounting software vendors focused on SMB segment, in North America, revenue range $5M-$100M.

**Example 3 (Consolidation):** You want to consolidate the fragmented IT services space. Market scope: IT services firms offering managed services, US-based, revenue $20M-$500M, within 200 miles of your offices.

### Step 1B: Identify Target Sources

Use multiple channels to surface potential targets. No single channel is complete.

| Source | Coverage | Quality | Effort |
|--------|----------|---------|--------|
| **Publicly traded companies** | Complete, but limited to large companies | Very high (disclosed financials) | Low |
| **VC databases (Crunchbase, PitchBook)** | Good for tech/growth companies | Medium (not all companies listed) | Low |
| **Industry directories** | Variable by industry | Medium (may be outdated) | Medium |
| **Competitor websites** | Good for understanding ecosystem | High (current) | Medium |
| **Industry conferences** | Emerging companies, ecosystem view | Medium (small sample) | High |
| **Sell-side advisors** | Comprehensive, some selection bias | High (advisors have relationships) | Medium (requires relationship) |
| **Buy-side searches** | Comprehensive, custom scoping | High (professional) | High (expensive) |
| **LinkedIn / ZoomInfo** | Complete for companies with web presence | Medium (verification required) | Medium |
| **Regulatory filings / SEC** | Complete for public and some private | Very high | Low |

**Best practice:** Use 2-3 sources in parallel. Cross-reference to ensure comprehensive coverage.

### Step 1C: Build Your Longlist

Consolidate all sources into a single longlist. Aim for 20-50 companies for the initial landscape.

| Company | Source(s) | Revenue Est. | Stage | Geographic Focus | Key Notes |
|---------|-----------|--------------|-------|------------------|-----------|
| Target A | Crunchbase, LinkedIn | $2-5M | Growth | San Francisco | Strong team, new market entrant |
| Target B | Industry directory | $12M | Mature | Northeast | Profitable, possible founder burnout |
| Target C | Competitor analysis | $8-10M | Growth | Midwest | Growing 40%+ YoY, private equity backing |

## Stage 2: Screening Criteria Definition

Not all targets are equal. Define criteria that reflect your strategic rationale. This prevents you from chasing targets that don't actually solve your problem.

### Screening Dimension 1: Strategic Fit

Does the target help you execute your acquisition thesis?

| Criterion | Evaluation | Weight |
|-----------|------------|--------|
| **Business model alignment** | Does target's business model (SaaS, product, services, platform) match your acquirer profile? | High |
| **Customer overlap** | Do targets' customers represent a valuable cross-sell/retention opportunity? | High (if revenue synergy is material) |
| **Technology/capability fit** | Does target's tech/IP/talent fill a specific gap in your roadmap? | High (if capability acquisition) |
| **Market geography** | Does target operate in your target markets or adjacent markets you want to enter? | High (if market entry) |
| **Go-to-market compatibility** | Can target's sales/support model integrate with yours? | Medium (depends on integration model) |
| **Competitive positioning** | Is target a leader, challenger, or emerging player in its space? | Medium |
| **Industry/ vertical focus** | Does target serve your target verticals or adjacent verticals? | Medium-High (depends on strategy) |

**Scoring:** For each criterion, score 1-5 (1 = poor fit, 5 = perfect fit). Average across criteria to get strategic fit score (1-5).

**Quality gate:** Strategic fit score must be ≥3.5 to advance. If lower, the target doesn't actually solve your problem.

### Screening Dimension 2: Financial Profile

Can you afford the target and will it make financial sense?

| Criterion | Evaluation | Threshold |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| **Revenue / EBITDA** | Is target large enough to have material impact but small enough to integrate? | Revenue $5-100M (adjust for your scale) |
| **Profitability** | Is target profitable or on path to profitability? | EBITDA margin >0% or clear path to it |
| **Growth trajectory** | Is target growing revenue (even if not profitable)? | Revenue growth >5% YoY (higher is better) |
| **Working capital efficiency** | Does target require significant capex or working capital? | <15% of revenue in annual capex/NWC |
| **Leverage** | Does target have debt? Can you refinance or pay off? | Debt <2x EBITDA (lower is better) |
| **Price paid must make math work** | Even with optimistic synergies, does the deal pay off? | Price <7x EBITDA (adjust for growth/margins) |

**Scoring:** For each criterion, note if target meets threshold (Y/N). If >1 criterion is a hard no, screen out.

**Quality gate:** Must pass revenue size, profitability trajectory, and working capital tests to advance.

### Screening Dimension 3: Integration Complexity

How hard will this be to integrate?

| Factor | Evaluation | Complexity Score |
|--------|------------|------------------|
| **Technology stack** | How different is target's tech from yours? | 1 (identical) to 5 (completely different) |
| **Organizational size** | Larger targets = more complex integrations | 1 (very small) to 5 (very large) |
| **Customer base** | Is customer base sticky? Would churning risk be high? | 1 (contractual, sticky) to 5 (month-to-month, transactional) |
| **Key person dependency** | How dependent is target on specific founder/CTO/CEO? | 1 (low) to 5 (very high) |
| **Geographic dispersion** | Are offices consolidated or scattered? | 1 (concentrated) to 5 (highly dispersed) |
| **Operating model gap** | How different are operating models, cultures, incentives? | 1 (similar) to 5 (very different) |

**Scoring:** Average all factors to get integration complexity score (1-5, where 1 = easy, 5 = very hard).

**Quality gate:** Complexity score >3.5 is a yellow flag. If complexity >3.5 AND strategic fit <4, screen out (hard to integrate + not perfect fit = bad trade-off).

### Screening Dimension 4: Cultural Compatibility

Will the organization mesh? Will key talent stay?

| Factor | Evaluation |
|--------|------------|
| **Leadership values** | Do target's leaders operate with similar values/ethical standards? |
| **Decision-making style** | Data-driven vs. intuition-driven; consensus vs. hierarchical? |
| **Risk tolerance** | Conservative vs. aggressive? Long-term vs. short-term focused? |
| **Work style** | Remote-first vs. in-office; flat vs. hierarchical? |
| **Talent retention risk** | Do you expect key people to stay, or likely to leave? |

**Scoring:** For each, note potential culture clash (0 = major clash, 5 = perfect alignment). If any factor is 0-1 AND that person is critical to deal value, red flag it.

**Quality gate:** If cultural compatibility score is <2 average AND deal depends on retaining key talent, screen out.

## Stage 3: Longlist to Shortlist Funnel

### Step 3A: Apply Screening Filters

Run each longlist target through the screening criteria. Create a screening matrix.

```
SCREENING MATRIX
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Company | Strategic Fit | Financial | Complexity | Culture | Overall Score | Decision
--------|---------------|-----------|------------|---------|---------------|----------
Target A | 4.2 | Pass | 2 | 3.5 | 3.4 | ADVANCE
Target B | 2.8 | Fail (debt) | — | — | SCREEN OUT | No
Target C | 4.5 | Pass | 2.5 | 4.0 | 3.75 | ADVANCE
Target D | 4.0 | Pass | 4.0 | 2.0 | 3.25 | CONDITIONAL
Target E | 3.8 | Pass | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.08 | CONDITIONAL
Target F | 2.5 | Pass | 2.5 | 4.0 | 3.08 | CONDITIONAL

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

ADVANCE: Strategic fit ≥3.5 AND all financial tests pass AND complexity ≤3
CONDITIONAL: All metrics >2.5 but with at least one yellow flag (investigate further)
SCREEN OUT: Any hard fail on strategic fit, financial profile, or culture

Results: Advance 1, Conditional 4, Screen Out 1. Shortlist = Advance + top Conditionals = 3-4 targets.
```

### Step 3B: De-Risk the Conditional Targets

For targets flagged as "CONDITIONAL," run quick diligence to resolve the yellow flag.

**Conditional Example 1:** Strategic fit is 3.8 (borderline). Before advancing, do 3-5 customer reference calls to validate whether your customers actually want this target's product/capabilities.

**Conditional Example 2:** Integration complexity is 4.0 (high). Before advancing, interview target's technical leadership to assess whether technology stack gap is a bridge or a chasm.

**Conditional Example 3:** Culture compatibility is 2.5 (below threshold). Before advancing, deepen cultural due diligence: interview 10-15 employees, assess leadership alignment.

## Stage 4: Competitive Dynamics Intelligence

For each shortlist target, assess whether you're the only buyer or if competitive dynamics will drive up price.

### Step 4A: Who Else Might Bid?

| Potential Bidder Type | Likelihood | Price Impact | Advantage vs. You |
|----------------------|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Strategic buyer (competitor)** | High | High (competitive tension) | May have stronger synergies; will overpay |
| **Strategic buyer (adjacent market)** | Medium | Medium | Different synergies; may value differently |
| **PE/Financial buyer** | Medium | Medium-High | Leverage; less reliant on synergies; will pay for EBITDA |
| **Other corporate acquirers** | Low-Medium | Low-Medium | Different synergies; may not understand business |

**Example analysis:** "Target X is a niche software company. Likely bidders: (1) Competitor Y (VERY likely to bid, will probably overpay because platform synergies), (2) Industry platform buyer (Medium likelihood), (3) PE firm looking at EBITDA multiple (Medium-High likelihood). Competitive tension: HIGH."

### Step 4B: What Would Make Price Explode?

Identify trigger points that would heat up an auction:

- Advisor engagement by target (hiring banker = signal of sale intent)
- Leak to press that company is in sale process
- Multiple bidders in formal process
- Founder/management signal that they want to exit

### Step 4C: Timing and Approach Strategy

For each shortlist target, develop a timing and approach plan:

| Approach | Timeline | Rationale |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Direct outreach (friendly)** | 2-4 weeks to initial conversation | You reach out directly to founder/CEO; pitch acquisition |
| **Warm introduction (friendly)** | 2-4 weeks plus relationship setup | Mutual introduction via advisor/investor; less formal approach |
| **Banker process (competitive)** | 3-6 months to close | Target hires banker; you compete in formal auction |
| **Opportunistic (distressed)** | 1-2 months | Company in financial distress; you step in with stabilization offer |
| **Announce then approach** | Varies | You announce strategic move, then approach targets who fit profile |

**Best practice:** Approach friendly before targets hire bankers. Once a banker is engaged, competitive auction dynamics usually ensue.

## Stage 6: Shortlist Prioritization

Rank your shortlist by a weighted scoring approach.

```
SHORTLIST RANKING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Rank | Target | Strategic Fit | Financial Health | Integration Risk | Timing | Overall Score | Priority |
-----|--------|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|---------------|----------
1 | Target A | 4.5 (40%) | 4.0 (30%) | 3.5 (20%) | 4.0 (10%) | 4.15 | PRIMARY |
2 | Target C | 4.0 (40%) | 4.5 (30%) | 3.0 (20%) | 3.0 (10%) | 3.95 | SECONDARY |
3 | Target E | 3.8 (40%) | 3.5 (30%) | 3.5 (20%) | 4.5 (10%) | 3.78 | TERTIARY |

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Scoring rubric:
- Primary target: Approach immediately (within 2 weeks)
- Secondary target: Approach if primary doesn't move forward (4-6 week backup)
- Tertiary target: Hold as option if primary and secondary fail
```

## Execution Checklist: Screening Phase

Use this checklist to ensure you've done thorough target screening before moving to diligence.

```
PRE-DILIGENCE VERIFICATION CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

□ Acquisition thesis documented and approved (one-paragraph statement)
□ Strategic rationale type identified (Consolidation / Capability / Market Entry / etc.)
□ Longlist generated from multiple sources (2+ sources, 20-50 targets)
□ Screening criteria defined (Strategic Fit / Financial / Integration / Culture)
□ Longlist scored against screening criteria (matrix completed)
□ Targets flagged as "CONDITIONAL" have been de-risked via quick diligence
□ Shortlist created (3-8 primary targets advancing to formal diligence)
□ Competitive dynamics researched for each shortlist target
□ Target approach strategy documented (timing, intermediary, warm intro vs. cold)
□ Target profiles created for each shortlist candidate
□ Shortlist prioritized by weighted scoring
□ Walk-away price estimated for primary target(s)
□ Board/leadership approval to proceed to formal diligence on shortlist

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## Common Screening Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Screening Too Narrowly

**What it looks like:** You define such tight criteria that only 1 target qualifies. You anchor on that target. You lose negotiating leverage.

**How to avoid:** Aim for 3-8 shortlist targets. Anything fewer than 3 = too narrow. If too many (>10), tighten criteria.

### Mistake 2: Favoring Targets with Existing Relationships

**What it looks like:** You have a relationship with Founder A. You screen them favorably despite mediocre fit. You miss better targets.

**How to avoid:** Run everyone through the same objective screening criteria. Score first, then assess relationships. Relationships are useful for negotiation, not for overriding screening rigor.

### Mistake 3: Underestimating Integration Complexity

**What it looks like:** "This company is 95% aligned with us. Integration will be easy." You acquire it. You discover the 5% misalignment is the core business. Integration is a nightmare.

**How to avoid:** For high-complexity integrations, invest in deep cultural and organizational due diligence before LOI. Don't assume alignment. Test it.

### Mistake 4: Not Assessing Competitive Dynamics

**What it looks like:** You approach Target X. You didn't know that Competitor Y is also in discussions. You get into bidding war. You overpay.

**How to avoid:** Before approaching any target, do diligence on who else might bid. Is the target actively in market? Is there banker involvement? If so, assume competition and adjust price expectations.

### Mistake 5: Anchoring on First Targets Identified

**What it looks like:** You identify Target A, like it, approach immediately. You don't do full landscape mapping first. Better targets exist, but you're already in diligence.

**How to avoid:** Complete full landscape mapping and screening BEFORE you approach any target. Lock in the shortlist first. Then prioritize approach.
