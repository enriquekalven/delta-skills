# Due Diligence: De-Risking the Target

Due diligence has one purpose: de-risk the target before you close. Every finding should be converted into a decision: accept the risk, mitigate it, renegotiate terms, or walk.

## Core DD Principle: Parallel Workstreams, Escalation Protocol

Don't run workstreams sequentially (financial DD, then legal, then cultural). Run them in parallel. Each workstream owns a risk register. RED flags get escalation decision protocol before signing.

---

## Workstream 1: Commercial Due Diligence

**Purpose:** Validate the target's business model, market position, customer quality, and growth trajectory.

### Step 1A: Market Position Assessment

| Check | Validation Method | Red Flag |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **Market share** | Third-party data (Gartner, Forrester, industry reports) | Declining share or overestimated market position |
| **Competitive positioning** | Customer interviews, win/loss analysis | Losing deals to specific competitors; unable to articulate differentiation |
| **Product roadmap** | Product leadership interviews | Roadmap is vague or dependent on single person |
| **Customer perception** | NPS, CSAT, reference calls | NPS <30 or net churn >10% |
| **Barriers to entry** | Competitive analysis | No defensibility; customers switching freely to competitors |

### Step 1B: Customer Quality & Concentration

| Check | Data Needed | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Top customer concentration** | List of top 20 customers by revenue | Top 3 customers = >40% of revenue |
| **Contract terms** | Master contract + 5 largest customer contracts | Month-to-month or easy termination (auto-renewal assumed) |
| **Churn analysis** | Monthly customer attrition by cohort (12-month history) | Net churn >10% YoY; accelerating churn trend |
| **Gross retention** | Revenue retention from existing customers (12-month) | <85% (losing existing customer spend) |
| **Customer concentration by product** | Revenue by product/module (which products sticky?) | Single product = >70% of revenue (diversification risk) |
| **Customer health indicators** | Usage metrics, support tickets, NPS by customer | Key accounts showing declining engagement |

**Example red flag:** Top 5 customers = 60% of revenue. Of those, 3 have month-to-month contracts. 1 customer indicates they're evaluating alternatives post-close. Risk: $X revenue at risk on close.

**Escalation decision:** Do you accept this risk, require customer commitment letter pre-close, reduce price to account for churn risk, or walk?

### Step 1C: Sales & Marketing Performance

| Check | Validation Method | Red Flag |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **Sales capacity / productivity** | Sales rep count, quota, attainment (past 12 months) | Declining attainment; top sales rep turnover |
| **Sales cycle** | Average time from first conversation to close (by product) | Increasing sales cycle; longer than your sales cycle |
| **CAC (Customer acquisition cost)** | Sales & marketing spend / new customers acquired (LTM) | CAC >24 months payback (unprofitable growth) |
| **Product-market fit** | Demand generation sources (inbound vs. outbound ratio) | >80% outbound (weak PMF); <20% inbound (not sticky) |
| **Marketing effectiveness** | Pipeline generation by source; conversion rates | Declining conversion rates; over-reliance on paid acquisition |
| **Pricing power** | Price changes over time; customer willingness to pay | Constant discounting; unable to increase prices |

**Red flags:**
- Average sales cycle is 12 months; your sales cycle is 2 months (incompatible with your go-to-market)
- CAC is $500K; LTV is $1.2M (not enough margin to sustain)
- 90% of new customers require 30%+ discount to close

### Step 1C-Extended: GTM & Influence Due Diligence

In industries where adoption is influenced by relationships, advocacy, or partnerships (pharma, medtech, enterprise software, professional services), commercial DD must extend beyond sales metrics:

| Check | Validation Method | Red Flag |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **KOL relationship mapping** | Advisory board rosters, consulting agreements, conference speaker lists | KOL relationships are informal/personal (don't transfer post-close); top KOLs aligned with competitors |
| **KOL relationship strength** | Contracted (advisory board, consulting) vs. informal (conference network) | >70% of KOL relationships are informal and tied to specific individuals who may leave |
| **Existing partnerships & alliances** | Partnership agreement review, exclusivity terms, co-marketing agreements | Exclusive partnerships that conflict with acquirer's portfolio; partnerships that terminate on change-of-control |
| **Partnership transferability** | Legal review of change-of-control provisions in partnership agreements | Key partnerships require partner consent for transfer; partner is a competitor of acquirer |
| **Marketing & medical affairs depth** | Field force size, medical science liaison (MSL) count, marketing team capabilities | Understaffed marketing/medical affairs relative to portfolio size; over-reliance on partner co-promotion |
| **Advocacy network health** | Press coverage sentiment analysis, conference presence, professional society relationships | Negative press momentum; declining conference presence; competitor gaining advocacy share |
| **Sales force capability for cross-sell** | Sales rep product knowledge, cross-sell training, incentive alignment | Sales team trained on single product; no cross-sell motion exists; incentives don't reward portfolio selling |

**Red flags:**
- Top 10 KOLs are all aligned with a single executive who has no retention agreement
- Key distribution partnership terminates on change-of-control with no renewal guarantee
- Target has no field medical affairs team in a KOL-driven therapeutic area
- Press/media coverage is increasingly negative or competitor-favorable

**Escalation decision:** If KOL/partnership risks are material to the thesis, require relationship transferability plan pre-close or adjust valuation to account for relationship rebuilding cost and timeline.

### Step 1D: Growth Trajectory & Sustainability

| Check | Analysis | Red Flag |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Revenue trend** | Plot quarterly revenue past 3 years | Declining growth rate (from 50% → 30% → 10%); hitting a ceiling |
| **Customer acquisition** | New customers added by quarter, trend | Sharply declining new customer additions |
| **Expansion revenue** | % of revenue from existing customer expansion (quarterly) | Expansion revenue declining (red flag for retention) |
| **Market growth vs. company growth** | Company growth rate vs. market growth rate | Company growing slower than market (losing share) |
| **Basis for growth** | What's driving growth? New products, new customers, expansion? | Growth is from one-time customer win; not repeatable |

**Example analysis:**
```
Revenue Trend (LTM):
Q1: $20M (YoY growth: 40%)
Q2: $21M (YoY growth: 35%)
Q3: $21.5M (YoY growth: 28%)
Q4: $22M (YoY growth: 20%)

Trend: Growth decelerating. If trend continues, company will hit growth cliff in 6-12 months.
Questions: What's driving deceleration? Market saturation? Competitive pressure? Execute capability?
```

### Step 1E: Customer Reference Calls

Run 8-12 reference calls with customers, focusing on:

| Question | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| "How long have you been a customer?" | Understand tenure |
| "How critical is [target's product] to your operations?" | Switching cost assessment |
| "What's your NPS for this vendor?" | Satisfaction (look for <7 = risky) |
| "Are you considering alternatives?" | Churn risk |
| "If you were acquired by [your company], would you continue using this product?" | Post-close retention |
| "What could cause you to leave?" | Failure modes |
| "Would you be interested in [your company's products]?" | Cross-sell validation |

**Red flags during calls:**
- Customer says "we're evaluating alternatives"
- Customer reveals undisclosed contract/SLA issue
- Customer mentions key person dependency (if that person is leaving, they leave)
- Unexpected pricing concerns or margin pressure

## Workstream 2: Financial Due Diligence

**Purpose:** Validate the quality and sustainability of target's financials.

### Step 2A: Revenue Quality (The Most Important DD)

Quality of earnings = how much of reported revenue is real, recurring, and sustainable?

| Check | Validation | Red Flag |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Revenue recognition policy** | Review accounting policy vs. ASC 606 | Non-standard revenue recognition; aggressive timing |
| **Revenue by customer** | List all customers, revenue, contract terms | >50% of revenue from 5 customers; concentration risk |
| **Deferred revenue** | Deferred revenue balance, trend | Deferred revenue declining (customers not renewing) |
| **Month-to-month vs. multi-year** | % of revenue under annual+ contracts | >50% month-to-month (recurring assumption is weak) |
| **Refund / return rate** | % of revenue refunded/returned | >2% refund rate (quality issue) |
| **Customer discounting** | % of deal price discounted to close | >30% average discount (price integrity issue) |
| **One-time revenues** | Any non-recurring revenue (license sales, services) | >20% from one-time (not sustainable) |
| **Pro forma adjustments** | Management adjustments to GAAP numbers | >$5M of adjustments (questions basis for numbers) |

**Example red flag:** Company reports $100M revenue. On closer inspection:
- 30% is from deferred revenue (cash collected upfront, not yet earned)
- 15% is from one-time customer implementation services
- 10% is from one customer (concentration)
- Actual recurring, core SaaS revenue: ~$45M (vs. reported $100M)

**Impact:** Deal value is cut in half.

### Step 2B: Quality of Earnings Deep Dive

| Check | Analysis | Red Flag |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Organic revenue growth** | Revenue growth excluding acquisitions, one-time items | Growth is lower than reported; relies on acquisitions or one-time events |
| **Reported EBITDA vs. cash EBITDA** | EBITDA adjusted for non-cash charges | EBITDA includes large non-cash items (stock comp, depreciation) inflating profit |
| **Working capital build** | Is company building working capital (cash drag)? | WC building >5% of revenue growth (cash runway risk) |
| **Accounts receivable aging** | Days sales outstanding, aging schedule | DSO is 90+ days (collection risk) or accelerating (credit quality issue) |
| **Inventory valuation** | For product companies: obsolescence rate | Inventory write-downs expected post-close |
| **Revenue quality adjustments** | Adjust for non-recurring, one-time revenue | Adjusted revenue 15%+ lower than GAAP |

### Step 2C: Profitability & Unit Economics

| Check | Calculation | Red Flag |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Gross margin trend** | Gross margin by year (past 3 years) | Declining (pricing power loss or cost inflation) |
| **Operating margin** | OPEX as % of revenue, trend | OPEX growing faster than revenue (inefficiency) |
| **Customer LTV / CAC** | LTV = (Gross margin × customer lifetime) / CAC | LTV:CAC <3:1 (unprofitable unit economics) |
| **Magic number** | (Revenue gain this quarter - last quarter) / sales & marketing spend (previous quarter) | <0.75 (inefficient growth spend) |
| **Rule of 40** | Revenue growth % + EBITDA margin % | <40 (not balancing growth and profitability) |

**Example:** Company reports 40% YoY growth and 20% EBITDA margin. Sounds good. But:
- Gross margin has declined from 75% → 70% (pricing pressure)
- OPEX is growing 60% (hiring faster than revenue; unsustainable)
- Without the large one-time revenue, actual EBITDA margin is 5%
- True rule of 40: 40% growth + 5% margin = 45% (actually OK, but different story than 40% + 20%)

### Step 2D: Balance Sheet & Liabilities

| Check | Review | Red Flag |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Debt / leverage** | Debt schedule; debt covenants; refinancing risk | Debt >3x EBITDA; covenant violations likely post-close |
| **Contingent liabilities** | Pending litigation, warranties, indemnities | Material contingent liabilities not accrued |
| **Off-balance-sheet liabilities** | Operating leases (now capitalized under ASC 842), purchase commitments | >$10M in off-balance-sheet liabilities (understates leverage) |
| **Deferred tax assets** | Does company have NOLs or tax credits? | NOLs or credits may be limited-use post-acquisition (Section 382) |
| **Intangible assets** | Goodwill, patents, customer relationships | Goodwill >50% of total assets (acquisition risk) |

### Step 2E: Capex & Working Capital

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Historical capex** | Capex as % of revenue past 3 years | Ramping capex (large spend coming) |
| **Deferred capex** | Projects deferred due to cash constraints | Deferred projects = future obligations (not disclosed) |
| **Receivables aging** | Days to collect revenue | Increasing DSO (collection getting worse) |
| **Payables** | Days to pay vendors | Stretched payables (vendor relationships stressed) |
| **Inventory turns** | For product companies: inventory days | High inventory days (cash tied up; obsolescence risk) |

## Workstream 3: Operational Due Diligence

**Purpose:** Understand how the target actually operates and where integration risks lie.

### Step 3A: IT Infrastructure & Systems

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Core systems** | What ERP, CRM, HRIS, accounting systems are used? | Multiple legacy systems; no integration; high technical debt |
| **Cloud vs. on-prem** | What's cloud, what's on-prem? Upgrade plans? | On-prem systems requiring costly migration |
| **Integration landscape** | How are systems connected? APIs or custom integrations? | Custom integrations; fragile data flows |
| **Data quality** | Data governance, master data management | Poor data quality; duplicate data; no single source of truth |
| **IT staffing** | IT team size, capability, documentation | Understaffed IT team; reliance on consultants; poor documentation |
| **Security & compliance** | Certifications (SOC2, ISO 27001)? Vulnerabilities? | Missing certifications; known vulnerabilities; compliance issues |
| **Disaster recovery** | RTO/RPO plans? Tested? | No DR plan; RTO >24 hours |
| **Tech stack modernity** | End-of-life systems? Upgrade roadmap? | End-of-life systems (support ending); major upgrade needed |

**Integration risk assessment:** For each major system, assess: Can this integrate with our systems or does it need replacement? Replacement cost? Timeline?

**Example:** Target's ERP is 10 years old, on-prem only. Full replacement needed (estimate: $3M, 18 months). Not budgeted in deal model. This is a deal risk.

### Step 3B: Operations & Supply Chain

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Key processes** | Map critical end-to-end processes (order-to-cash, purchase-to-pay) | Poorly documented; dependent on specific people |
| **Suppliers** | Who are key suppliers? Concentration? | >50% from 2-3 suppliers; long lead times; relationship dependent |
| **Manufacturing / fulfillment** | If product company: where manufactured, quality, capacity | Single manufacturing location; no redundancy; quality issues |
| **Capacity utilization** | Current utilization vs. capacity | Running at 95%+ capacity; limited growth headroom without capex |
| **Key dependencies** | What would break the business if lost? | Key person, key customer, key supplier, key facility |
| **Service delivery** | If service company: delivery model, utilization, bench | Overly utilized; bench is thin; difficult to scale |

### Step 3B-Extended: Cross-Border Operational Friction

For cross-border acquisitions or targets with geographically distributed operations, assess operational friction that compounds post-close:

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Language barriers** | Primary operating languages across entities; translation/localization capabilities; technical communication gaps | Primary languages differ with no bilingual leadership; technical documentation in single language only |
| **Timezone challenges** | Hours of real-time overlap for collaboration; impact on decision speed; meeting logistics | <4 hours of overlap (e.g., US-Japan = 3 hours); critical decisions require 24-48 hour async cycles |
| **Travel logistics** | Frequency needed for integration, cost per trip, visa requirements, pandemic/disruption resilience | Quarterly executive travel required at $15K+/trip; visa processing adds 4-6 weeks; no travel budget in deal model |
| **Regulatory jurisdiction differences** | Overlapping vs. distinct regulatory bodies; approval timeline implications; compliance cost differences | Different regulatory frameworks require separate submissions; 6-12 month approval gaps between jurisdictions |
| **Therapeutic area / product overlap** | Does overlap create internal competition or collaboration barriers? | Acquirer and target compete in same therapeutic area — sales teams may resist cross-promotion; cannibalization risk |
| **Cultural work norms** | Decision-making speed, hierarchy expectations, communication directness, meeting culture | Hierarchical culture (target) meets flat culture (acquirer) — decision paralysis during integration |

**Quantification guidance:**
- Timezone: Calculate "effective collaboration hours" = overlap hours × meeting efficiency factor (typically 0.6-0.8)
- Travel: Estimate Year 1 integration travel budget = (# trips × cost/trip × # travelers). Typically 2-3x normal travel for 18 months.
- Language: If bilingual integration leads are needed, budget $150-250K/year per geography for dedicated liaison roles.
- Decision cycle: Estimate async decision cycle time. If critical decisions take >48 hours due to timezone/language, multiply integration timeline by 1.3-1.5x.

**Impact on deal model:** Cross-border friction often adds 6-12 months to integration timeline and 15-25% to integration costs. If not budgeted, this directly erodes synergy realization.

### Step 3C: Facilities & Real Estate

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Leased vs. owned** | What's leased, what's owned? Lease terms? | Key leases expiring soon; above-market lease rates |
| **Lease obligations** | Remaining lease commitments | >$50M in undisclosed lease obligations |
| **Facility condition** | Age, condition, needed improvements | Aging facility requiring capex; environmental issues |

## Workstream 4: Legal & Regulatory Due Diligence

**Purpose:** Identify legal risks and compliance issues that could become liabilities post-close.

### Step 4A: Material Contracts

| Check | Validation | Red Flag |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Customer contracts** | Review 10 largest customer contracts | Change-of-control clause triggers termination on acquisition |
| **Vendor contracts** | Review critical supplier contracts | Change-of-control requires supplier consent; renegotiation risk |
| **Employment agreements** | Founder/key person agreements; change-of-control triggers | Founder has significant golden parachute; key people have change-of-control triggers |
| **Partnership agreements** | Any strategic partnerships, reseller agreements | Partner termination or renegotiation likely post-close |
| **Financing arrangements** | Any debt, lines of credit | Debt acceleration on change-of-control |
| **Lease agreements** | Headquarters lease | Lease termination or rent increase on change-of-control |

**Change-of-control risk assessment:** For each material contract, assess: Does this contract terminate or renegotiate on change-of-control? What's the revenue/expense impact?

**Example red flag:** 40% of revenue is under customer contracts with change-of-control clauses. Post-close, 25% of those customers invoke the clause and leave. Revenue impact: $10M churn.

### Step 4B: Intellectual Property

| Check | Verification | Red Flag |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| **Patents** | Review patent portfolio; pending applications | Few patents; limited IP protection; pending challenges |
| **Trademarks** | Owned trademarks; pending; conflicts with others | Trademark disputes; key trademark unprotected in key markets |
| **Copyrights** | Software, content copyrights; licensing | Code sourced from open-source with viral licenses; copyleft obligations |
| **Trade secrets** | Are trade secrets protected? Employee agreements? | No non-compete agreements; IP ownership unclear |
| **Third-party IP rights** | Using licensed technology? Royalty obligations? | Royalty rates high; dependent on third-party licensed tech |
| **IP disputes** | Any pending IP litigation or infringement claims? | Competitor claiming patent infringement; litigation ongoing |

**IP due diligence is critical for tech acquisitions.** Delayed or improper IP vesting can derail a deal.

### Step 4C: Litigation & Regulatory Compliance

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Pending litigation** | Any lawsuits against the company | Material litigation; loss is probable; amount not accrued |
| **Regulatory investigations** | Any regulatory inquiries (SEC, FTC, DOJ) | Active investigation; potential fines/remediation |
| **Compliance status** | GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, SOX, anti-corruption | Known compliance failures; no remediation plan |
| **Environmental compliance** | If manufacturing: environmental permits, violations | Environmental violations; cleanup required (not accrued) |
| **Antitrust risk** | Would your acquisition trigger antitrust concerns? | Deal likely to face antitrust challenge; conditions required |

**Antitrust considerations:** For consolidation deals, assess whether combined market share triggers FTC review. If likely, factor in delay and remedies cost.

### Step 4D: Employee & Labor Compliance

| Check | Review | Red Flag |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Employment contracts** | Change-of-control triggers, severance | Founder severance is >$50M; key person has golden parachute |
| **Benefit plans** | Pension obligations, deferred comp, bonus plans | Unfunded pension liability; excess executive compensation; tax issues |
| **Stock options / equity** | Unvested equity; acceleration on change-of-control | Large equity pool; significant vesting acceleration cost post-close |
| **Union representation** | Any unionized operations | Union contract; renegotiation likely post-close |
| **Labor disputes** | Any pending labor claims | DOL investigation; wage violation claims |

## Workstream 5: Cultural & Organizational DD

**Purpose:** Assess organizational health, leadership capability, and cultural compatibility.

### Step 5A: Organizational Structure & Depth

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Org chart depth** | Is there 2+ levels below CEO? Or is it flat/founder-dependent? | Founder-dependent; <2 levels of management (scale risk) |
| **Key person dependency** | How many critical functions depend on 1-2 people? | >30% of key functions dependent on 1-2 people |
| **Leadership bench** | Are there internal candidates for promotion? | No internal bench; all leaders are external hires |
| **Succession plans** | Are succession plans in place for critical roles? | No succession planning; will require external search |
| **Accountability structure** | Are roles, responsibilities, and KPIs clear? | Unclear accountabilities; finger-pointing, poor execution |

### Step 5B: Talent & Retention Risk

| Check | Assessment | Red Flag |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **Turnover rate** | Annual turnover in last 2 years (by level) | >20% annual turnover; trending higher; loss of experienced staff |
| **Key person retention agreement** | Do key people have retention agreements? | Key people have no retention agreements; high departure risk post-close |
| **Compensation competitiveness** | How do salaries compare to market? | Below-market compensation; retention risk |
| **Employee satisfaction** | eNPS, recent survey results | Low eNPS (<20); cultural issues; morale problems |
| **Diversity & inclusion** | Diversity metrics; inclusion initiatives | Homogeneous org; lack of diversity; potential integration friction |

### Step 5C: Leadership Capability Assessment

| Check | Evaluation | Red Flag |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **CEO capability** | Track record, strategic acumen, people management | CEO is founder, not professional manager; limited scale experience |
| **Leadership team alignment** | Do leaders agree on strategy, priorities? | Leadership team is fragmented; power struggles |
| **Decision-making speed** | How quickly can leaders make and execute decisions? | Slow decision-making; consensus culture impedes speed |
| **Customer/market orientation** | Do leaders understand customer needs, market dynamics? | Insular leaders; limited customer interaction |
| **Change management capability** | Have leaders managed organizational change before? | Limited change management experience; worried about integration |

### Step 5D: Cultural Assessment

| Assessment | Method | Red Flag |
|------------|--------|----------|
| **Core values** | Review company values, listen to how they're actually lived | Values are aspirational but not lived (e.g., "innovation" but risk-averse culture) |
| **Decision-making style** | Data-driven? Intuitive? Consensus? | Incompatible with acquirer's decision-making style (decision speed mismatch) |
| **Risk tolerance** | Conservative or aggressive risk-taker? | Risk profile opposite of acquirer's (one is aggressive, one is conservative) |
| **Innovation approach** | Build internal, partner, acquire? | Target acquires fast; acquirer builds slow (integration model conflict) |
| **Talent development** | Does org invest in people, or burn out? | High-burn culture; talent exodus likely post-close |
| **Work-life balance** | Startup grind or sustainable pace? | High-burn culture; acquirer has sustainable culture (clash) |

**Cultural scoring:** For each dimension, rate alignment (-2 to +2). Anything <-1 is a yellow flag. Multiple <-1 scores = high cultural integration risk.

## Due Diligence Risk Register & Escalation

Create a master risk register consolidating all workstreams. Each risk gets a decision.

```
DUE DILIGENCE RISK REGISTER
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Risk | Workstream | Severity | Evidence | Decision | Owner | Deadline
─────|───────────|----------|----------|----------|-------|──────────
Customer concentration >50% | Commercial | RED | Top 5 = 55% of revenue | Renegotiate, customer commitment letters required before close | CEO/BD | [Date]
Revenue quality: 20% from one-time | Financial | YELLOW | Q1-Q4 breakdown shows services revenue nonrecurring | Adjust EBITDA downward in model; inform board of quality | CFO | [Date]
ERP replacement required | Operational | YELLOW | 10-year-old on-prem system; $3M to replace | Budget replacement cost in integration plan; adjust deal value | CTO | [Date]
Founder has golden parachute >$50M | Legal/HR | RED | Employment agreement review | Renegotiate founder package OR reduce price to account for cost | CEO/Legal | [Date]
High leadership turnover (25% YoY) | Cultural | YELLOW | HR data shows 3 departures in 12 months | Run retention interviews; may need golden handcuffs for top 5 | CHRO | [Date]

Decision codes:
RED STOP: Escalate to board; may walk depending on mitigation
YELLOW ACCEPT: Accept risk with mitigation plan or price adjustment
YELLOW MITIGATE: Deploy resources to reduce/eliminate risk pre-close
GREEN ACCEPT: Accept risk as immaterial

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

## Pre-Close Decision Gates

Before signing a LOI, all DD workstreams should be complete and risk decisions made.

```
PRE-LOI CHECKLIST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Commercial DD
  □ 8-12 customer reference calls completed
  □ Market position validated (internal, external sources align)
  □ Churn analysis reviewed (net churn <10% acceptable)
  □ Top customer concentration risk accepted or mitigated
  □ Growth trajectory sustainable (not artificially inflated)

Financial DD
  □ Quality of earnings validation (revenue quality ≥80% of reported)
  □ GAAP vs. adjusted EBITDA gap understood
  □ Working capital needs modeled (not a surprise post-close)
  □ One-time items excluded from synergy/valuation assumptions
  □ Off-balance-sheet liabilities identified and quantified
  □ Debt and covenant compliance status verified

Operational DD
  □ IT systems assessed (replacement vs. integration decision made)
  □ Critical processes documented (key person dependencies identified)
  □ Supply chain assessed (single points of failure identified)
  □ Facility and real estate obligations quantified

Legal DD
  □ Material contracts reviewed; change-of-control triggers identified
  □ IP ownership confirmed; no third-party conflicts
  □ Pending litigation and amount of exposure documented
  □ Regulatory compliance status verified
  □ Employment agreements and equity vesting reviewed

Cultural DD
  □ Leadership interviews completed
  □ Organization depth assessed (key person dependency map)
  □ Employee satisfaction assessed (eNPS, survey)
  □ Cultural compatibility scored (red flags identified)
  □ Retention plan drafted for top 20 people

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

Once all boxes are checked and RED risks are either mitigated or accepted with price adjustment, you're ready for LOI.
