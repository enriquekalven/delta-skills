# Mixture of Experts: M&A Decision Panel

Before recommending an acquisition, subject the deal to review by 5 expert personas. Each brings a distinct lens and catches different risks. Use this framework to stress-test your deal thesis before it goes to the board.

---

## Expert 1: The Investment Banker

**Background:** 20+ years in investment banking. Has done 200+ deals. Knows valuation models inside-out, understands market dynamics, and can smell a bad deal a mile away.

**Review lens:** Deal economics, valuation, market positioning, competitive pressure, deal execution risk.

**Key questions:**
- "Is this price defensible vs. recent comps in the market?"
- "Who else might bid? Are we getting this at a discount or overpaying?"
- "Are we buying at peak valuation or trough?"
- "What's our MOIC (money multiple) if everything goes perfectly? If things go wrong?"
- "Is this the right timing in the market cycle?"
- "What's our exit scenario in 5-7 years? Can we get our money back?"

**Scoring criteria (0-20 points):**
- Valuation (0-5): Is price fair relative to comps and FCF fundamentals? (5 = best-in-class value)
- Market timing (0-5): Are we buying at the right point in the cycle? (5 = excellent timing)
- Deal risk (0-5): How likely is this to close at the proposed terms? (5 = high confidence)
- Exit optionality (0-5): Can we achieve a good return on exit? (5 = multiple exit paths)

**Typical verdict:**
- **PASS (16-20):** "Valuation is right, timing is good, deal will likely close, exit is clear. Recommend proceeding."
- **CONDITIONAL (10-15):** "Valuation is rich but acceptable if synergies pan out. Recommend using earnout to bridge."
- **REJECT (<10):** "Price is too high given execution risk and exit optionality. Recommend walking or significant renegotiation."

**Red flags this expert watches for:**
- Price is >10x EBITDA for mature business (unjustifiable unless high growth)
- Valuation is driven by unproven synergies (dangerous assumption)
- Market is heating up and you're racing to close (overpayment risk)
- Deal has to close to hit board expectations (bad incentive; means overpayment risk)

## Expert 2: The Corporate Development VP

**Background:** 10+ years as a buyer in corporate M&A. Has done 15+ acquisitions. Understands strategic buyer perspective: what synergies are realistic, how to integrate, what kills deals post-close.

**Review lens:** Strategic fit, synergy achievability, integration complexity, organizational readiness, value preservation.

**Key questions:**
- "Do we actually need this acquisition? Why not build or partner?"
- "Are the synergies real or are we deluding ourselves?"
- "Can our organization absorb this acquisition without dropping current balls?"
- "Is this target the right choice among 5-10 options?"
- "What's the integration timeline? What could go wrong?"
- "Will our board approve this? Will investors love it or question it?"

**Scoring criteria (0-20 points):**
- Strategic fit (0-5): Does this actually solve a real strategic problem? (5 = crystal clear fit)
- Synergy realism (0-5): Are synergies achievable or are we overpromising? (5 = conservative estimate with proof points)
- Integration feasibility (0-5): Can we pull off this integration without breaking the business? (5 = straightforward, low risk)
- Organizational readiness (0-5): Are we ready for this acquisition? (5 = have bandwidth, proven integration team)

**Typical verdict:**
- **PASS (16-20):** "Strategic fit is clear, synergies are conservative and achievable, integration is straightforward. Recommend proceeding."
- **CONDITIONAL (10-15):** "Strategic fit is good but integration is complex. Recommend using lighter integration model and longer timeline."
- **REJECT (<10):** "Strategic fit is unclear or synergies are overstated. Recommend walking or reconceiving the deal."

**Red flags this expert watches for:**
- "We need this to be big" (goal-driven acquisition, not problem-driven)
- Synergies depend on revenue synergies >50% of total (those rarely work)
- Your org is already running hot (no bandwidth for integration)
- Leadership team is fractured on the rationale (sign of poor thesis)
- This is your first acquisition (execution risk is very high)

## Expert 3: The PE Operating Partner

**Background:** 12 years in private equity operations. Has run 20+ integrations from the PE side. Knows value creation playbooks, operational improvement methodology, and how to fix broken companies.

**Review lens:** Operational value creation, management team capability, value protection, downside scenario planning, leverage capacity.

**Key questions:**
- "If this company grows nothing post-close, can we still create value through operational improvements?"
- "What's the value-at-risk? What kills the economics?"
- "Is management good enough to execute? Can they improve operations?"
- "What's the leverage capacity? Can we hold debt without covenant stress?"
- "What's the downside scenario? Can we survive a 30% revenue decline?"
- "What are the value protection levers if things go sideways?"

**Scoring criteria (0-20 points):**
- Management quality (0-5): Is the CEO/leadership team A-grade? (5 = best in class, proven operators)
- Operational improvement opportunity (0-5): Can we improve EBITDA margins by 3-5 points? (5 = clear, low-hanging fruit identified)
- Leverage safety (0-5): Can this cash flow support the debt load? (5 = conservative leverage, covenant-friendly)
- Downside protection (0-5): If everything goes wrong, do we lose money? (5 = downside protected by cash flow or asset value)

**Typical verdict:**
- **PASS (16-20):** "Management is strong, operations can improve, leverage is safe. Can create value even with no growth."
- **CONDITIONAL (10-15):** "Management is mixed, some operational improvements possible. Recommend lighter debt load to protect downside."
- **REJECT (<10):** "Management is weak, operations can't improve fast enough, leverage is high. Recommend waiting for better opportunity."

**Red flags this expert watches for:**
- CEO is founder; never run a larger organization (unproven at scale)
- EBITDA margins are already best-in-class (nowhere to go operationally)
- Working capital is high and growing (cash efficiency is poor)
- Customer concentration is high (leverage pressure if customer leaves)
- Leverage is >4x EBITDA for cyclical business (covenant risk)

## Expert 4: The Integration PMO Lead

**Background:** Has run 30+ post-close integrations. Obsessed with execution risk, change management, and synergy realization. Knows exactly where integrations derail.

**Review lens:** Integration complexity, execution risk, people risk, customer risk, synergy realization timeline, PMO readiness.

**Key questions:**
- "How hard is this integration, honestly?"
- "Which 5 things are most likely to go wrong?"
- "Do we have the PMO capacity to run this?"
- "What customer risk are we taking?"
- "How long will synergies actually take to realize?"
- "What's the integration budget? Is it adequate?"
- "Do we have the change management capacity?"

**Scoring criteria (0-20 points):**
- Integration complexity (0-5): How hard is the integration? (5 = straightforward, low complexity)
- People risk (0-5): How much talent will we lose? (5 = low departure risk, retention agreements in place)
- Customer risk (0-5): How much customer churn will we take? (5 = low churn risk, customers called pre-close)
- Execution capacity (0-5): Do we have the team/budget/bandwidth to pull this off? (5 = PMO is ready, leadership bandwidth available)

**Typical verdict:**
- **PASS (16-20):** "Integration is straightforward, people risk is low, customer risk is low, we're ready to execute."
- **CONDITIONAL (10-15):** "Integration is complex, people risk is moderate. Recommend slower integration timeline and dedicated PMO."
- **REJECT (<10):** "Integration is too complex, people risk is high, we don't have the bandwidth. Recommend delaying or reconceiving."

**Red flags this expert watches for:**
- Integration timeline is 6 months (unrealistic for most integrations)
- Cost synergies are modeled to hit 100% in Year 1 (too aggressive; plan for 50-70%)
- No retention agreements with key talent (people will leave)
- No PMO has been built yet; planning to "let the teams figure it out" (recipe for failure)
- Your last integration was rough (organizations struggle to improve integration capability)
- Customer concentration >40% (customer risk is high; can't afford churn)

## Expert 5: The Antitrust Counsel

**Background:** Antitrust lawyer with 15+ years. Has navigated 50+ regulatory reviews. Knows which deals get challenged, what remedies look like, and when to be worried.

**Review lens:** Regulatory approval risk, market concentration, competitive effects, remedy likelihood, deal timeline impact.

**Key questions:**
- "Will this deal trigger an FTC/DOJ review?"
- "If reviewed, is it likely to be approved?"
- "What's the remedy scenario? How much value would be destroyed?"
- "What's the timeline risk? Could review delay close 6-12 months?"
- "Are there state-level issues (California, Texas AGs)?"
- "Are there international issues (CFIUS, EU, UK)?"
- "What's our defense? Can we prove this is pro-competitive?"

**Scoring criteria (0-20 points):**
- Regulatory approval likelihood (0-5): How likely is this to be approved? (5 = low or no FTC risk)
- Remedy likelihood (0-5): If challenged, how acceptable are remedies? (5 = light remedies, not value-destructive)
- Timeline certainty (0-5): Will close happen on schedule? (5 = clear path, no delays expected)
- Competitive defense (0-5): Can we defend this as pro-competitive? (5 = clear procompetitive rationale)

**Typical verdict:**
- **PASS (16-20):** "No material regulatory risk. Deal will close on expected timeline."
- **CONDITIONAL (10-15):** "Moderate regulatory risk. Second request is possible. Plan for 12-18 month timeline and potential light remedies."
- **REJECT (<10):** "High regulatory risk. Likely to face Second Request or challenge. Remedies could be value-destructive. Recommend reconceiving."

**Red flags this expert watches for:**
- Combined HHI post-acquisition is >2500 (FTC red zone)
- You already have >30% of market; this deal pushes you to >40% (concentration concern)
- Competitor has filed antitrust complaints against the target (evidence of competitive harm)
- This is a consolidation in an already consolidated market (scrutiny will be intense)
- CFIUS issues (foreign buyer acquiring US tech, telecom, defense, energy)
- You're acquiring only viable competitor in a small market

## How to Run an Effective MoE Review

### Step 1: Prep the Experts

Give each expert a 2-page briefing document on the deal:
- Deal overview (target, rationale, price, synergies)
- Key assumptions (strategic fit, synergy sources, integration model)
- Major risks (as you see them)
- Your recommendation (so experts know what you're asking them to critique)

### Step 2: Run 5 Individual Interviews

Schedule 30-45 minute interviews with each expert. In the interview:
1. **Present your case** (15 min): Walk through the deal thesis, why you think this is good
2. **Get critique** (20 min): Ask them to poke holes, raise concerns, score
3. **Capture scoring** (5-10 min): Lock in their scoring on the dimensions

Take notes. Capture specific red flags and recommendations.

### Step 3: Synthesis Meeting

Bring all 5 experts together (or do this via video conference). In this meeting:
1. **Present overall scorecard** (5 min): Show how each expert scored
2. **Surface disagreements** (10 min): Where do experts disagree?
3. **Identify consensus red flags** (10 min): What are the 2-3 things 3+ experts flag?
4. **Make recommendation** (5 min): PASS, CONDITIONAL, or REJECT

### Step 4: Decision

If experts give PASS (16-20 points, <2 rejections): Recommend to board.

If experts give CONDITIONAL (12-16 points, all experts see mitigatable risk): Recommend to board WITH CONDITIONS.
- "We recommend proceeding IF we execute these mitigations..."

If experts give REJECT (3+ experts score <10): Recommend walking or significantly reconceiving.

## Hard Stop Rules

**If ANY of these are true, STOP and renegotiate or WALK:**

1. **PMO expert says integration risk is too high** — Integration failures destroy value faster than anything else. If the person responsible for execution says "this is too risky," listen.

2. **Antitrust counsel says deal faces likely challenge with value-destructive remedies** — Regulatory risk can delay close 18+ months and destroy value. Can't be ignored.

3. **3+ customers (top 20) indicate they'd consider leaving** — Customer attrition wipes out synergies. Deal can't succeed if customers leave.

4. **Your walk-away price is breached and seller won't negotiate** — Overpayment destroys value. Better to walk than overpay.

5. **Key talent signs departure agreement (3+ of top 20 departing)** — Talent exodus means you can't execute. Without execution, deal fails.

6. **Quality of earnings analysis shows revenue is 20%+ lower than reported** — Deal economics are based on fictional revenue. Walk.

7. **Banker says price is >15% above market comps and you're being pressured to close** — Overpayment under pressure = deal destruction. Walk.

## When Experts Disagree

Sometimes experts will disagree. This is valuable.

**Example:** PMO says integration is high-risk. Corp Dev says the integration is straightforward. Who's right?

**Resolution:**
1. Have them discuss their disagreement (usually reveals different assumptions)
2. Decide: Is one expert's lens more relevant? (PMO's execution lens is probably most relevant here)
3. If both lenses matter, document the disagreement: "PMO flagged high execution risk due to [X]. Corp Dev sees integration as straightforward because [Y]. We'll assume PMO is right and budget accordingly."
4. Use disagreement to reduce risk (in this case, spend more on PMO and integration planning)

**Disagreement is often the most valuable output of the MoE review.** It forces you to clarify what you're assuming and to stress-test those assumptions.
