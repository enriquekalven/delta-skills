# Synergy Modeling: Quantifying Value Creation

Synergies are liabilities until proven. This playbook teaches you to quantify synergies rigorously, probability-weight them, and identify integration costs that are often underestimated.

## Core Principle: Synergies Are Realized or Regretted

Every synergy dollar you claim must be:
1. **Specific** — tied to a real customer, contract, or cost item (not abstract)
2. **Achievable** — realizable within 24-36 months with realistic resourcing
3. **Probability-weighted** — discounted for execution risk
4. **Tracked post-close** — monitored monthly; shortfalls trigger corrective action

If you can't meet all four criteria, don't count it.

## The Four Categories of Synergies

### Category 1: Revenue Synergies (The Dream)

Revenue synergies come from selling more to more customers. They're valuable but notoriously difficult to realize.

#### Type 1A: Cross-Sell to Acquirer's Customers

**What it is:** Sell target's products/services to your existing customer base.

**When it works:**
- Your customers have stated interest in target's product (validated via reference calls)
- Target's product solves a pain point your customers currently have
- Sales teams are incentivized to cross-sell (commission structure aligned)
- No customer contract restrictions prevent cross-selling

**Example:** You sell accounting software to SMBs. You acquire a payroll software company. You can now offer payroll as add-on to your existing customers. Validated: 20 of your 500 customers have mentioned needing payroll software. Potential: $2M new ACV × 100 customers over 3 years = $200M total contract value / $40M ACV.

**Probability weighting:**
- Customer expressed interest (reference calls): 70% probability
- Sales team will actually cross-sell (execution risk): 50-60% probability
- Combined: 35-42% probability of full realization

**Synergy value (conservative):** $200M × 40% = $80M

**Red flags:**
- >20% of synergy is "upmarket pricing" (risky; customers will resist)
- Customer reference calls didn't mention needing this product
- Sales team has no experience selling adjacent products
- Commission structure doesn't incentivize cross-sell (no upside for reps)

#### Type 1B: Cross-Sell to Target's Customers

**What it is:** Sell your products to target's existing customer base.

**When it works:**
- Your products complement target's products (natural fit)
- Target's customers have budget for additional software/services
- Target's sales team will actually sell your stuff (incentive-aligned)
- No customer concentration risk (you're not dependent on a few customers)

**Example:** You sell enterprise data warehousing. You acquire a data integration company. You can now upsell your data warehouse to their customers. Validated: Their 50 customers have average enterprise footprint; 30% of them are good prospects for your warehouse. Potential: 15 new customers × $500K ACV = $7.5M new revenue.

**Probability weighting:**
- Target's customers are appropriate buyer persona: 80% probability
- Sales team conversion rate (typical for new product): 20-30% probability
- Combined: 16-24% probability

**Synergy value (conservative):** $7.5M × 20% = $1.5M

**Red flags:**
- Target's customers are SMBs; you sell enterprise (poor fit)
- Target's customers already have your product (no cross-sell opportunity)
- Typical sales cycle is 9-12 months; integration timeline is 6 months (won't realize Year 1)

#### Type 1C: Platform Expansion

**What it is:** Acquire target as a module in your platform. Existing and new customers get target's capabilities as part of your platform.

**When it works:**
- Target's technology is a natural module/bolt-on to your platform (not requiring deep rewrite)
- Your product roadmap has a gap that target fills
- Your customer base has requested this capability

**Example:** You operate a marketing automation platform. You acquire a lead scoring AI company. You integrate their AI into your platform's lead scoring module. Your 5,000 existing customers automatically get access. Value: 20% of your customers upgrade to include this module; 15% price increase on those customers = 1,000 customers × $10K additional ACV = $10M new revenue.

**Probability weighting:**
- Integration is achievable without rewrite: 90% (technical feasibility)
- Customers will upgrade (attach rate): 50-60% (adoption risk)
- Price increase sticks (customer acceptance): 70% (value perception risk)
- Combined: 32-57% probability

**Synergy value (conservative):** $10M × 40% = $4M

**Red flags:**
- Integration requires target's entire platform to be rewritten (high technical risk)
- Your product roadmap already has this on the roadmap; target is acceleration (don't double-count)
- Customers are likely to want this feature for free, not for premium (pricing power is weak)

#### Type 1D: Market Entry or Accelerated Growth

**What it is:** Target has distribution, brand, or customer relationships in a new market. You leverage those to enter faster than you could build.

**Example:** You're a US SaaS company wanting to enter EMEA. You acquire a EMEA reseller with existing customer relationships and regulatory knowledge. You can now sell directly into EMEA via their customer base and team. Potential: 50 new customers in Year 1-2 × $200K ACV = $10M new revenue.

**Probability weighting:**
- Target's customer relationships transfer to you: 70% (depends on contracts and loyalty)
- New customer acquisition is incremental to what you'd do anyway: 60% (you might have hired a team and done this yourself)
- Combined: 42% probability

**Synergy value:** $10M × 42% = $4.2M

**Red flags:**
- Target's customers are concentrated with 2-3 large accounts (relationship risk is high)
- Regulatory or language barriers make your products unsuitable (market fit is poor)
- Customer contracts are non-exclusive but customers have strong alternative (leverage is low)

### Category 2: Cost Synergies (The Reality)

Cost synergies come from eliminating redundancy, consolidating purchasing, or absorbing target into your operating model. They're more reliable than revenue synergies but still subject to execution risk.

#### Type 2A: Overhead Elimination

**What it is:** Eliminate duplicate functions (finance, HR, legal, IT, etc.) by absorbing target into your operations.

**How to calculate:**
1. Identify target's overhead costs (CFO, Controller, HR Director, Office Manager, etc.)
2. Estimate which roles are redundant with your organization
3. Assume 60-70% of costs are actually eliminable (some expansion may be needed)
4. Calculate run-rate cost savings

**Example:** Target has 100 employees with 8 FTE in finance/accounting (CFO, Controller, 2 accountants, 1 AR, 2 AP, 1 FP&A). Your company has finance team. Redundancy: Can eliminate target's CFO, Controller, AR, AP headcount (5 FTE). Cost: 5 FTE × $200K fully loaded = $1M/year. Timeline: Most saves achieve 50% in Year 1 (retention agreements, transition time), 100% in Year 2.

**Year 1 synergy:** $1M × 50% = $500K
**Year 2+ synergy:** $1M × 100% = $1M
**Net present value (3-year):** $500K + $1M + $1M = $2.5M (discounted at 10% = ~$2.1M)

**Probability weighting:** 80% (overhead elimination is fairly reliable)

**Synergy value (conservative):** $2.1M × 80% = $1.7M

**Red flags:**
- You're estimating >70% of overhead as redundant (unrealistic; you'll need some expansion)
- You're assuming all savings in Year 1 (too aggressive; transitions take time)
- You're not accounting for retention packages or severance (real cost)
- Target's office is in expensive market; you'll save rent but need to relocate people (disruption risk)

#### Type 2B: G&A and Shared Services Consolidation

**What it is:** Consolidate back-office functions (recruiting, benefits administration, finance systems, IT support) onto your platforms.

**Example:** Target has 200 people. Current G&A spend (recruiting, benefits admin, facilities, IT support): $2M/year. You can absorb them into your shared services platform. Incremental cost: $300K to add 200 people to your systems. Net savings: $2M - $300K = $1.7M/year. Realization: 60% Year 1 (transition period), 100% Year 2+.

**Year 1 synergy:** $1.7M × 60% = $1M
**Year 2+ synergy:** $1.7M × 100% = $1.7M

**Probability weighting:** 75% (depends on shared services scale and quality)

**Synergy value (conservative):** ($1M + $1.7M + $1.7M) / 1.1^1.5 × 75% ≈ $1.8M

#### Type 2C: Procurement Savings

**What it is:** Consolidate target's vendor contracts with yours to get volume discounts.

**How to calculate:**
1. Map target's vendor spend (software, cloud, vendors, suppliers)
2. Identify categories where target is already buying what you buy
3. Estimate volume discount available (typically 10-30% depending on category)
4. Calculate run-rate savings

**Example:** Target spends $5M annually on vendor spend:
- $2M on cloud infrastructure (AWS, Salesforce, etc.)
- $1.5M on professional services
- $1M on office/operational spend
- $0.5M on software tools

You already negotiate volume pricing with AWS, Salesforce, and major vendors. By consolidating target's cloud spend with yours:
- AWS: Target pays $500K; you negotiate additional $75K/year discount by reaching volume tier
- Salesforce: Target pays $300K; consolidation saves $30K/year
- Procurement savings potential: $105K/year

Total procurement savings potential: $105K + [similar analysis for other categories] = $250-400K/year

**Probability weighting:** 70% (some vendors won't grant discounts; some savings are one-time)

**Synergy value (conservative):** $300K × 70% = $210K per year

#### Type 2D: Facility Consolidation

**What it is:** Consolidate target's offices into your office footprint.

**Example:** Target occupies 20,000 sq ft in San Francisco at $70/sq ft/year = $1.4M/year. Your office has excess capacity. By moving target's team into your office, you eliminate target's lease (but incur $200K moving/restructuring costs). Net annual savings: $1.4M - $200K = $1.2M.

**Realization:** Takes 12-18 months to execute (lease run-out, renovations, team relocation). Year 1 savings: $400K. Year 2+ savings: $1.2M.

**Probability weighting:** 65% (depends on lease terms, local real estate, team retention through move)

**Synergy value:** ($400K + $1.2M + $1.2M) / 1.1^1.5 × 65% ≈ $650K

### Category 3: Capability Synergies

Capability synergies are often overlooked but can be valuable. These are the internal efficiencies and skill transfers that result from combining two organizations.

#### Type 3A: Operational Improvement

**What it is:** Target has superior operational capabilities (manufacturing, supply chain, quality) that improve your business when adopted.

**Example:** Target has manufacturing process improvements that reduce cost of goods by 3%. You combine their process with your manufacturing footprint. Annual COGS impact: Your revenue is $200M; COGS is $100M. 3% improvement = $3M annual savings.

**Realization:** 50% Year 1, 100% Year 2+

**Probability weighting:** 80% (operational improvements are generally reproducible)

**Synergy value:** ($1.5M + $3M + $3M) / 1.1^1.5 × 80% ≈ $1.9M

#### Type 3B: Best Practice Adoption

**What it is:** Target has superior sales methodology, customer service process, or product development approach that you adopt.

**Example:** Target's customer success team has 25% lower churn rate than your team. Adopting their playbook across your customer base could save 2% of revenue (prevented churn). Your revenue is $200M. Impact: $4M in retained revenue.

**Realization:** 50% Year 1, 100% Year 2+

**Probability weighting:** 60% (behavior change is hard; not all teams will adopt)

**Synergy value:** ($1.2M + $4M + $4M) / 1.1^1.5 × 60% ≈ $1.8M

### Category 4: Financial Synergies

Financial synergies come from improved financing terms, working capital optimization, or tax benefits.

#### Type 4A: Cost of Capital Improvement

**What it is:** As a combined entity, you negotiate better debt terms or can access capital markets at lower rates.

**Example:** You currently have $500M debt at 5%. Target has $100M debt at 6.5%. Post-close, combined entity refinances at 4.8%. Savings: ($600M × 4.8%) - ($500M × 5% + $100M × 6.5%) = $28.8M - $28.5M = $0.3M/year.

**Realization:** One-time in Year 1

**Probability weighting:** 85% (if capital structure supports it)

**Synergy value:** $0.3M × 85% = $255K

#### Type 4B: Working Capital Optimization

**What it is:** Target has higher working capital requirements than necessary. You optimize their cash conversion cycle.

**Example:** Target's working capital is 20% of annual revenue ($100M revenue = $20M NWC). Your company operates at 12% NWC. Improvement opportunity: 8% × $100M = $8M cash freed up. At 5% cost of capital, this equals $400K annual benefit.

**Realization:** Year 1 (cash freed when consolidation completes)

**Probability weighting:** 70% (working capital optimization takes time and discipline)

**Synergy value:** $400K × 70% = $280K one-time benefit

## Integration Costs (What Kills Deals)

Integration costs are almost always underestimated. Budget properly or your synergies evaporate.

### Category 1: Severance and Retention

| Cost Item | Calculation | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| **Severance** | Headcount eliminated × 6-9 months salary | Varies by geography (EU higher than US) |
| **Retention bonuses** | Key talent count × (3-12 months salary) | Prevent exodus of critical people |
| **Management separation** | Founder/founder package (if negotiated) | Often 1-2 years of salary |

**Example:** You eliminate 50 headcount (target had 200). Average salary $150K. Severance (7 months average): 50 × $150K × 7/12 = $4.4M

You want to retain 15 key engineers post-close. 12-month retention bonus: 15 × $150K = $2.25M

**Total severance + retention:** $6.65M

### Category 2: One-Time Integration Costs

| Cost Item | Typical Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Systems migration / IT consolidation** | $500K - $5M+ | Depends on systems complexity |
| **Process reengineering** | $250K - $2M | New workflows, training, change mgmt |
| **Facilities transition** | $200K - $1M | Moving costs, office setup, lease breakage |
| **Professional services (integration advisors)** | $500K - $3M | PMO, change management, specialized advisors |
| **Customer communication / retention** | $100K - $500K | Customer visits, relationship management, incentives |
| **Tax / legal / structural work** | $500K - $2M | Deal closing, earnout administration, restructuring |

**Total integration costs rule of thumb:** Budget 10-20% of deal value in one-time integration costs.

**Example:** $500M deal. Budget $50-100M in integration costs.

### Category 3: Recurring Integration Costs

Some costs continue beyond Year 1:

| Cost Item | Nature | Duration |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Dual running of systems** | Maintain legacy systems in parallel while migrating | 6-18 months |
| **Increased management layer** | PMO, integration office, transition team | 12-24 months |
| **Customer transition support** | Extra support during account migrations | 6-12 months |

**Example:** Running dual ERP systems for 12 months costs $1M/year = $1M additional cost (beyond salary/operational headcount).

## Synergy Realization Tracking (Post-Close)

After closing, track synergy realization monthly. Shortfalls trigger immediate investigation and corrective action.

```
SYNERGY TRACKING SCORECARD
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Synergy Category | Planned (Year 1) | Realized (Q1) | Realized (Q2) | YTD % | Status
─────────────────|────────────────|───────────────|────────────────|──────|────────
Revenue: Cross-sell | $5M | $0.5M | $1.2M | 34% | YELLOW
Revenue: Platform | $3M | $0M | $0.3M | 5% | RED (investigate)
Cost: Overhead | $2M | $1M | $1.2M | 110% | GREEN
Cost: Procurement | $0.5M | $0.1M | $0.2M | 60% | YELLOW
TOTAL | $10.5M | $1.6M | $2.9M | 43% | YELLOW

Status codes:
GREEN: On track or ahead (>85% of plan)
YELLOW: At risk (50-85% of plan)
RED: Significantly behind (<50% of plan) - escalate immediately

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

For RED items, immediately:
1. Understand root cause (what assumption was wrong?)
2. Adjust Year 2-3 expectations
3. Deploy resources to close the gap
4. Report variance to leadership

## Common Synergy Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Double-Counting Synergies

**What happens:** Cost synergy for "eliminating target's CFO" and "consolidating finance" both counted. You count overhead reduction twice.

**How to avoid:** Build synergy model linearly. Once you eliminate a headcount, track it only once. Use a detailed org chart to prevent double-counting.

### Mistake 2: Assuming Revenue Synergies at 100%

**What happens:** You assume all cross-sell opportunities materialize. In reality, 30-50% do. Deal value is overestimated by 50%.

**How to avoid:** Weight revenue synergies at 30-50% probability. Validate with customer reference calls. For each customer, what's their actual likelihood to buy?

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Integration Costs

**What happens:** You model $50M in synergies. Integration costs are $15M (unbudgeted). Net synergy value is 70% of forecast. Deal doesn't meet return hurdle.

**How to avoid:** Budget integration costs upfront (10-20% of deal value). Separate gross synergies from net synergies. Inform board that net value is lower than gross.

### Mistake 4: Assuming 100% Realization in Year 1

**What happens:** Most synergies take 18-36 months to realize. You model them all in Year 1. Year 1 earnings miss expectations. Stock takes a hit.

**How to avoid:** Model realistic timelines. Cost synergies: 50-80% in Year 1, 100% in Year 2+. Revenue synergies: 30-50% in Year 1, 50-70% in Year 2, 70%+ in Year 3.

### Mistake 5: Not Tracking Synergies Post-Close

**What happens:** You model $50M in synergies. Post-close, you stop tracking. By Year 2, only $20M has materialized. No one knows why.

**How to avoid:** Build synergy tracking into PMO governance. Monthly tracking against plan. Rapid escalation for shortfalls. Assign accountability.
